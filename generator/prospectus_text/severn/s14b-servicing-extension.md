### Collections and payment methods

Borrowers pay their monthly instalments to the Mortgage Administrator. Each Mortgage Loan is repayable by
monthly instalments due on a day of the month chosen by the borrower at completion (the "Monthly Payment
Day"). The great majority of borrowers pay by direct debit under the Direct Debit Scheme, collected by the
Mortgage Administrator through the Bacs payment system into the Collection Account described in Section 15.
Borrowers may also pay by standing order, by faster payment or other bank transfer, by debit card through the
Mortgage Administrator's contact centre or website, or over the counter at a branch of the Society. Payments
received in any of those ways are credited to the Collection Account.

The Mortgage Administrator applies every receipt to the borrower's account on the day of receipt, first to
any amounts due other than interest and principal (such as fees and insurance premiums), then to interest and
then to principal, in accordance with the mortgage conditions, unless the borrower has directed that a
payment be applied as an overpayment. The Mortgage Administrator identifies each receipt as a revenue receipt
or a principal receipt of the Issuer on that basis, and reports it to the Cash Manager for the purposes of the
Revenue Ledger and the Principal Ledger described in Section 15. Redemption monies, including early repayment
charges, are received by the Mortgage Administrator from the borrower's conveyancer and credited to the
Collection Account.

Where a direct debit is returned unpaid, the mortgage administration system re-presents it automatically on
the date set by the Mortgage Administrator's collections policy, unless the borrower has cancelled the
mandate, in which case the Mortgage Administrator contacts the borrower to obtain a new mandate or another
means of payment. A returned direct debit is treated as a missed payment for the purposes of the arrears
management process described below from the Monthly Payment Day on which it fell due.

The Mortgage Administrator will, on behalf of the Issuer, set the Standard Variable Rate and any other
discretionary rate applicable to the Mortgage Loans. It has agreed in the Mortgage Administration Agreement
that it will not set the Standard Variable Rate applicable to the Mortgage Loans at a rate higher than the
Society's standard variable rate applicable to comparable loans that it holds for its own account, and that
it will set it at a rate not lower than the minimum rate determined in accordance with the Mortgage
Administration Agreement by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA, unless the Issuer and the Security Trustee
agree otherwise. In setting the rate, the Mortgage Administrator must comply with the mortgage conditions,
MCOB and the Consumer Duty, and the Issuer's rights are subject to those requirements.

### Arrears management

The Mortgage Administrator applies the Society's arrears policy to the Mortgage Loans, as summarised at the
start of this Section 14. That policy is designed to identify borrowers in payment difficulty early, to
establish their circumstances and to agree an arrangement that is sustainable for the borrower, in accordance
with MCOB 13 and the Consumer Duty. The arrears management process is organised in the following stages,
although a borrower's account may move between them, or be dealt with outside them, according to the
borrower's circumstances.

(a) **Early arrears.** When a payment is missed, the borrower is contacted by letter, text message or
telephone within ten days, as stated above. The Mortgage Administrator's early arrears team seeks to establish
the reason for the missed payment, whether it is a temporary or a longer-term difficulty, and the borrower's
income and expenditure. It gives the borrower the information required by MCOB 13, including information about
free and impartial debt advice. Where the difficulty is short-term, the borrower is asked to clear the arrears
or offered a short payment arrangement.

(b) **Established arrears.** Where the arrears continue, the account is transferred to the Mortgage
Administrator's specialist financial support team. That team carries out a full assessment of the borrower's
income and expenditure, using the standard financial statement used in the debt advice sector, and considers
which of the forms of forbearance described below is appropriate. It sends the statements of arrears and the
information sheet required by MCOB 13 and records every contact and every arrangement on the mortgage
administration system.

(c) **Pre-litigation.** Where no arrangement has been agreed, or an arrangement has failed and the borrower does
not engage with the Mortgage Administrator, the account is reviewed by a manager in the financial support team
before any step towards litigation is taken. The review confirms that the borrower has been offered every
appropriate form of forbearance, that the requirements of the relevant pre-action rules described below have
been met, and that the borrower is not in a vulnerable situation that requires a different approach.

(d) **Litigation and recovery.** Only after that review may the account be passed to the Mortgage
Administrator's litigation team for proceedings to be brought, as described under "Litigation and possession"
below. Possession is sought only as a last resort, as stated above.

The Mortgage Administrator identifies and records the characteristics of vulnerability of borrowers in
accordance with the FCA's guidance on the fair treatment of vulnerable customers, and adapts its contact and
its forbearance accordingly. It may suspend the arrears process for a borrower who has been bereaved, has a
serious illness or is in a breathing space under the Debt Respite Scheme (Breathing Space Moratorium and
Mental Health Crisis Moratorium) (England and Wales) Regulations 2020, during which it may not take enforcement
action and must suspend the application of default interest and charges as those Regulations require.

### Forbearance and the Mortgage Charter

Under MCOB 13, as amended by the FCA's rules on tailored support for borrowers in payment difficulties, the
Mortgage Administrator must consider the forms of forbearance that are appropriate to the borrower's
circumstances and must not apply a single form of forbearance to all borrowers. The forms of forbearance that
the Mortgage Administrator may offer include:

(a) an arrangement to pay the arrears over a period in addition to the monthly instalment;

(b) a temporary reduction in the monthly payment, or a temporary interest-only arrangement, for a defined
period, after which the payment is recalculated;

(c) an extension of the term of the Mortgage Loan, subject to the borrower's age and to the requirement in the
Loan Warranties as to final maturity set out in Section 12;

(d) the capitalisation of arrears, where the borrower has demonstrated that he or she can sustain the
recalculated monthly payment;

(e) a change to the repayment basis, including a permanent conversion of part of the loan to an interest-only
basis, where the borrower has a credible repayment strategy for that part; and

(f) the deferral of the remedy of arrears while the borrower sells the property voluntarily.

The Society is a signatory to the Mortgage Charter agreed between HM Treasury and mortgage lenders, with the support of the FCA, in
June 2023, and the Mortgage Administrator applies the commitments of the Mortgage Charter, as amended or
extended from time to time, to the Mortgage Loans. Those commitments include, in particular, that a borrower
who is up to date with payments may switch to interest-only payments for a temporary period, or extend the
mortgage term for a temporary period with the option to revert to the original term, without a new
affordability assessment and without the change affecting the borrower's credit file, and that a borrower will
not be required to leave the property without his or her consent within twelve months of the first missed
payment, except in exceptional circumstances. The FCA has amended MCOB to support those commitments.

The Mortgage Administrator will report to the Cash Manager, for inclusion in the investor report described
below, the number and aggregate current balance of the Mortgage Loans that are subject to each form of
forbearance. A Mortgage Loan that is subject to forbearance remains in the portfolio of the Issuer. The
Mortgage Administrator may agree any form of forbearance that is consistent with the Society's arrears policy,
MCOB and the Consumer Duty, and is not required to obtain the consent of the Issuer or the Security Trustee to
it. Forbearance may delay or reduce the receipts of the Issuer, and the arrears of a Mortgage Loan subject to
forbearance are counted for the purpose of the Arrears Trigger described in Section 8 in accordance with the
Arrears Ratio as defined in the Glossary of Defined Terms.

### Litigation and possession

Proceedings to enforce the Related Security are brought by the Mortgage Administrator in the name of the
Society, as legal owner of the Mortgage Loans, until a Perfection Event occurs, and thereafter in the name of
the Issuer. The costs of enforcement are recoverable from the borrower under the mortgage conditions and, to
the extent that they are not recovered, are deducted from the proceeds of enforcement before the Realised Loss
is calculated.

**England and Wales.** Before issuing a claim for possession of a residential property, the Mortgage
Administrator must comply with the Pre-Action Protocol for Possession Claims based on Mortgage or Home
Purchase Plan Arrears in Respect of Residential Property, which requires it, among other things, to provide
the borrower with information about the arrears, to consider any reasonable proposal for payment, to consider
postponing proceedings where the borrower has made a claim to a government agency or is pursuing the sale of
the property, and to inform the borrower of the date and time of any hearing. A claim is brought in the
County Court under Part 55 of the Civil Procedure Rules. The court may adjourn the proceedings, or suspend or
postpone an order for possession, under section 36 of the Administration of Justice Act 1970 and section 8 of
the Administration of Justice Act 1973, if it appears that the borrower is likely to be able to pay the sums
due within a reasonable period. An order for possession is enforced by a warrant of possession executed by a
county court enforcement officer or, with permission, by a writ of possession executed by a High Court
enforcement officer. The Mortgage Administrator must give notice to the occupiers of the property under the
Mortgage Repossessions (Protection of Tenants etc.) Act 2010 before an order is enforced.

**Scotland.** The enforcement of a standard security over a residential property in Scotland is governed by the
Conveyancing and Feudal Reform (Scotland) Act 1970, as amended by the Home Owner and Debtor Protection
(Scotland) Act 2010. Before applying to the court, the Mortgage Administrator must comply with the pre-action
requirements in section 24A of the 1970 Act, which include providing the borrower with information about the
arrears, making reasonable efforts to agree a proposal for payment, and considering any application by the
borrower to a debt payment programme or for a time to pay order. It must then serve a calling-up notice or a
notice of default, and apply to the sheriff court for a decree under section 24 of the 1970 Act. The court may
grant the decree only if it is satisfied that the pre-action requirements have been met and that it is
reasonable in the circumstances to do so. The borrower and any entitled resident may appear and be heard, and
the court may continue the proceedings or sist them. Following the decree, the Mortgage Administrator may
enter into possession and sell the property. Proceedings in Scotland are conducted by solicitors in Scotland
on the Mortgage Administrator's panel.

In both jurisdictions the time taken from the first missed payment to the sale of the property depends on the
borrower's engagement, the forbearance offered, the requirements of the pre-action rules and the Mortgage
Charter, the capacity of the courts and the condition of the local property market, and has historically been
longer in Scotland than in England and Wales. The Mortgage Administrator may accept a voluntary surrender of
possession by a borrower, and it will do so in preference to proceedings where the borrower agrees.

### Sale of repossessed properties

When the Mortgage Administrator takes possession of a property, it secures and insures the property and
instructs an asset manager on its panel to manage its sale. The asset manager obtains at least two independent
valuations, instructs local estate agents to market the property on the open market, and recommends to the
Mortgage Administrator the acceptance of offers. The Mortgage Administrator is under a duty, as mortgagee in
possession in England and Wales and as heritable creditor in Scotland, to take reasonable care to obtain the
true market value or the best price reasonably obtainable for the property at the time of sale, and it will
market every property for a period sufficient to achieve that. A property is sold by auction only where the
asset manager advises that auction is likely to achieve a better price, for example because of the condition
or the type of the property.

The proceeds of sale, after the deduction of the costs of possession and sale, are applied in discharge of the
Mortgage Loan and are credited to the Collection Account. Any shortfall is a Realised Loss, which is recorded on
the Principal Deficiency Ledger as described in Section 5. Any surplus is paid to any subsequent secured
creditor and then to the borrower. After the sale the Mortgage Administrator will pursue the borrower for any
shortfall where it considers it appropriate, in accordance with its policies and with industry guidance on
the recovery of mortgage shortfall debt, within the limitation periods under the Limitation Act 1980 in England
and Wales and the prescription periods under the Prescription and Limitation (Scotland) Act 1973 in Scotland.
Recoveries after the sale are revenue receipts of the Issuer, as described in Section 15.

### Mortgage Administrator reporting

The Mortgage Administrator will deliver to the Issuer, the Cash Manager and the Security Trustee, within the
period set out in the Mortgage Administration Agreement after the end of each month, a report on the Mortgage
Loans for that month (a "Monthly Mortgage Administration Report"). Each Monthly Mortgage Administration Report
will set out the receipts on the Mortgage Loans, split between revenue and principal; the redemptions,
repurchases and Product Switches in the month; the arrears of the Mortgage Loans by number of months in
arrears; the Mortgage Loans subject to forbearance, by form of forbearance; the properties in possession and
sold, and the Realised Losses on them; and the information needed by the Cash Manager to calculate the Arrears
Ratio and the Cumulative Loss Ratio on each Calculation Date.

The Mortgage Administrator will also provide the Cash Manager with the loan-level information on each Mortgage
Loan needed for the loan-level reports required by the transparency requirements of the Securitisation
Regulations and SECN, described in Section 17. On the basis of the Monthly Mortgage Administration Reports and
its own records, the Cash Manager will prepare a quarterly investor report (the "Investor Report") for each
Interest Payment Date, which will be made available to Noteholders as described in Section 17.

### Mortgage Administrator Termination Events

The appointment of the Mortgage Administrator may be terminated by the Issuer, with the consent of the Security
Trustee, or by the Security Trustee, by notice to the Mortgage Administrator on the occurrence of any of the
following events (each a "Mortgage Administrator Termination Event"):

(a) the Mortgage Administrator fails to pay into the Collection Account or the Transaction Account any amount
it is required to pay under the Mortgage Administration Agreement, and the failure is not remedied within five
Business Days of the earlier of the Mortgage Administrator becoming aware of it and the receipt of notice of it;

(b) the Mortgage Administrator fails to perform any other of its material obligations under the Transaction
Documents, and the failure, if capable of remedy, is not remedied within thirty days of the earlier of the
Mortgage Administrator becoming aware of it and the receipt of notice of it, and the Security Trustee
certifies that the failure is materially prejudicial to the interests of the holders of the most senior class
of Notes outstanding;

(c) an insolvency event occurs in relation to the Mortgage Administrator, including the making of a building
society insolvency order or a building society special administration order, or the exercise of a
stabilisation power under the Banking Act 2009 in a way that affects the performance of its obligations;

(d) the Mortgage Administrator ceases to hold the permissions under Part 4A of FSMA required to administer
regulated mortgage contracts; or

(e) it becomes unlawful for the Mortgage Administrator to perform its obligations under the Mortgage
Administration Agreement.

The Mortgage Administrator may also resign on not less than twelve months' notice. No termination or
resignation will take effect until a substitute mortgage administrator has been appointed that holds the
permissions required to administer regulated mortgage contracts, has experience of administering residential
mortgage loans in England, Wales and Scotland, and agrees to perform the obligations of the Mortgage
Administrator on substantially the same terms, and until the Rating Agencies have been notified. A
Mortgage Administrator Termination Event is a Perfection Event, as described in Section 12.

### Back-up arrangements

No back-up mortgage administrator is appointed on the Issue Date. If a Mortgage Administrator Termination Event
occurs, or if the long-term ratings of the Society fall below the levels specified in the Mortgage
Administration Agreement, the Issuer, with the assistance of Pellam Corporate Services Ltd (the "Corporate
Services Provider"), will use reasonable endeavours to appoint a back-up mortgage administrator within the
period set out in the Mortgage Administration Agreement. In the meantime, the Mortgage Administrator will
deliver to the Issuer, the Cash Manager and the Security Trustee a copy of the loan-level data needed to
administer the Mortgage Loans and a summary of its arrears procedures at least quarterly, and will keep its
records in a form that allows a substitute to take over the administration of the Mortgage Loans without
undue delay.

The Cash Management Agreement contains equivalent provisions for the termination of the appointment of the Cash
Manager and the appointment of a substitute cash manager, described in Section 15. The fees payable to any
substitute mortgage administrator or cash manager may be higher than those set out above; they rank in the
Revenue Priority of Payments in the same position as the fees of the Mortgage Administrator and the Cash
Manager. The risks of a change of administrator, including disruption of collections during the transfer, are
described in Section 1.
