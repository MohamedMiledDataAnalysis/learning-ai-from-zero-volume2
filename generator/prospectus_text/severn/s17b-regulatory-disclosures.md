### Transparency and Reporting

**The UK Securitisation Framework.** The sale of the Mortgage Loans to the Issuer and the issue of the Notes
constitute a "securitisation" within the meaning of the Securitisation Regulations 2024 (SI 2024/102) (the
"Securitisation Regulations"). The obligations of the parties are set out in the Securitisation Regulations,
in the securitisation sourcebook of the FCA Handbook ("SECN") and in the Securitisation Part of the PRA
Rulebook (the "PRA Securitisation Rules"), together with the templates, guidance and statements of policy
published under them (together, the "UK Securitisation Framework"). The Seller is a building society
authorised by the PRA and regulated by the FCA and the PRA, and its obligations as originator in respect of
risk retention, transparency and credit-granting are therefore set out in the PRA Securitisation Rules. The
Issuer is a securitisation special purpose entity (an "SSPE") which is not authorised by the PRA, and its
corresponding obligations are set out in SECN. The two sets of rules are in substance the same for the
purposes described in this Section 17.

**Designation of the Reporting Entity.** The Seller, as originator, and the Issuer, as SSPE, have designated
the Seller as the entity responsible for fulfilling the transparency requirements of the UK Securitisation
Framework in respect of the Notes (in that capacity, the "Reporting Entity"). The Seller will perform that
role through its treasury function, using the information produced in its capacity as Mortgage Administrator
and the calculations made in its capacity as Cash Manager. The Reporting Entity remains responsible for
compliance with the transparency requirements whether or not it delegates any task to another person.

**Information made available before pricing.** Before the pricing of the Notes the Seller made available to
potential investors:

(a) static and dynamic historical data on the arrears, default, possession, loss, recovery and prepayment
performance of the owner-occupied residential mortgage loans originated by the Seller, covering a period of
not less than five years, as referred to in Section 10;

(b) loan-level information on each Mortgage Loan in the Provisional Portfolio as at the Portfolio Reference
Date, in the underlying exposures template for residential real estate set out in the UK Securitisation
Framework;

(c) a liability cash flow model which precisely represents the contractual relationship between the Mortgage
Loans and the payments flowing between the Seller, the Issuer, the Swap Provider, the Noteholders and the
other parties to the transaction, which the Seller will continue to make available to Noteholders on an
ongoing basis and to potential investors on request; and

(d) a draft of this Offering Circular and drafts of the Mortgage Sale Agreement, the Mortgage Administration
Agreement, the Cash Management Agreement, the Swap Agreement, the Trust Deed and the Deed of Charge.

The final forms of this Offering Circular and of the Transaction Documents will be made available within the
period after the Issue Date required by the UK Securitisation Framework.

**Quarterly reporting.** On each Interest Payment Date, beginning with the first Interest Payment Date on
[[first_ipd]], the Reporting Entity will make available:

(a) the Investor Report described in Section 14, prepared by the Cash Manager in the form of the investor
report template for securitisations other than asset-backed commercial paper set out in the UK Securitisation
Framework, which will include, among other things, the Available Revenue Receipts and the Available Principal
Receipts and their application under the Revenue Priority of Payments and the Principal Priority of Payments,
the principal amount outstanding of each class of Notes, the balances of the General Reserve Fund and the
Liquidity Reserve Fund, the balance of each sub-ledger of the Principal Deficiency Ledger, the Arrears Ratio
and the Cumulative Loss Ratio, whether the Arrears Trigger, the Cumulative Loss Trigger or the Reserve Trigger
described in Section 8 has occurred, the net amounts paid or received under the Swap Agreement, and the
confirmation of the Seller that it continues to hold the randomly selected exposures described above; and

(b) loan-level information on each Mortgage Loan as at the end of the immediately preceding collection
period, in the underlying exposures template for residential real estate, in which each Mortgage Loan and each
borrower is identified by a unique identifier which does not allow the borrower to be identified directly.

The Investor Report and the loan-level information will relate to the same collection period and will be
made available no later than one month after the relevant Interest Payment Date, in accordance with the
UK Securitisation Framework.

**Inside information and significant events.** The Reporting Entity will make available without delay any
inside information relating to the Notes which the Issuer is required to make public under the UK market
abuse regime, to the extent that regime applies, and any significant event which the UK Securitisation
Framework requires to be disclosed, including:

(a) a material breach of the obligations provided for in the Transaction Documents, including any remedy,
waiver or consent subsequently provided in relation to such a breach;

(b) a change in the structural features of the transaction that can materially affect its performance;

(c) a change in the risk characteristics of the transaction or of the Mortgage Loans that can materially
affect the performance of the Notes;

(d) the occurrence of a Sequential Payment Trigger, the delivery of an enforcement notice or a Mortgage
Administrator Termination Event;

(e) the replacement of the Mortgage Administrator, the Cash Manager, the Account Bank or the Swap
Provider; and

(f) any material amendment to the Transaction Documents.

**Private securitisation; securitisation repository.** No application has been or will be made for the Notes
to be admitted to trading on any trading venue, and no prospectus is required to be published in respect of
the Notes under the UK prospectus regime. The securitisation is therefore expected to be a private
securitisation for the purposes of the UK Securitisation Framework, and the requirement that the information
on a public securitisation be made available through a securitisation repository registered with the FCA does
not apply to it. The Reporting Entity will instead make the information described above available to the
Noteholders, to the FCA and the PRA and, on request, to potential investors, through a secure investor
reporting website maintained by the Society, access to which will be given to each Noteholder and to each
potential investor which confirms that it is a professional investor. The Reporting Entity will also submit to
the regulators the notification required in respect of private securitisations, in the form and within the
period required by the UK Securitisation Framework, and will update it when the UK Securitisation Framework so
requires. The website and its contents do not form part of this Offering Circular.

If the securitisation becomes subject to a requirement to make information available through a securitisation
repository, the Reporting Entity will appoint a securitisation repository registered with the FCA, and its
fees will be borne by the Issuer as an expense. If, as has been proposed, the requirement to report through a
securitisation repository is removed from the UK Securitisation Framework, the Reporting Entity will continue to
make the information available through the website described above.

**Bank of England reporting.** The Seller may, in its discretion, make loan-level data on the Mortgage Loans
available in the form required by the Bank of England for residential mortgage-backed securities offered as
collateral in its Sterling Monetary Framework. No representation is made that any class of Notes is or will
become eligible collateral for the Bank of England or any other central bank, and the Seller is under no
obligation to make any application for that purpose or to continue to provide information in that form.

**Review of the UK Securitisation Framework.** HM Treasury, the FCA and the PRA have proposed amendments to the
UK Securitisation Framework which, among other things, would revise the underlying exposures templates for
residential real estate and the investor report requirements, remove the securitisation repository regime,
alter the notification required for private securitisations and simplify the due diligence obligations of
institutional investors. Those amendments may be made in a form, and with effect from a date, different from
those proposed. The Reporting Entity may change the form, the content, the frequency (subject to the minimum
frequency required) or the channel of the reports described in this Section 17 to the extent that an amendment
to the UK Securitisation Framework permits or requires it, without the consent of the Noteholders.

### Credit-Granting Criteria

The UK Securitisation Framework requires the originator to apply to the exposures to be securitised the same
sound and well-defined criteria for credit-granting which it applies to non-securitised exposures, to apply
the same clearly established processes for approving and, where relevant, amending, renewing and refinancing
credits, and to have effective systems in place to apply those criteria and processes. In the case of
residential mortgage loans, the pool of underlying exposures must not include any loan that was marketed and
underwritten on the premise that the loan applicant or, where applicable, an intermediary was made aware that
the information provided by the applicant might not be verified by the lender.

The Seller has confirmed to the Issuer that:

(a) each Mortgage Loan was originated in the ordinary course of its business under the Lending Criteria
described in Section 11, which are the criteria that the Seller applied, at the time of origination, to all
the owner-occupied residential mortgage loans that it originated, whether or not they were later securitised;

(b) it has effective systems in place to apply those criteria and its processes for approving, amending and
refinancing mortgage loans, including the Product Switch Conditions described in Section 11;

(c) before entering into each Mortgage Loan, it assessed the borrower's creditworthiness and the
affordability of the loan in accordance with the responsible lending requirements of MCOB 11.6 then in
force, as described in Section 11; and

(d) no Mortgage Loan was marketed or underwritten on the premise that the information provided by the
applicant might not be verified, and no Mortgage Loan is a self-certified mortgage loan.

The Mortgage Loans do not include any securitisation position, and the securitisation is not a
resecuritisation. The Loan Warranties in Section 12 include warranties on the arrears and insolvency status of
the borrowers at the Portfolio Reference Date, to which investors should refer.

### Due Diligence Requirements for Institutional Investors

**UK institutional investors.** Institutional investors subject to the UK Securitisation Framework, including
credit institutions, investment firms, insurance and reinsurance undertakings, UK alternative investment fund
managers, UK UCITS management companies and the trustees and managers of occupational pension schemes, must
comply with the due diligence requirements that apply to them before holding a securitisation position and for
as long as they hold it. For FCA-regulated institutional investors those requirements are set out in SECN 4;
for PRA-authorised persons they are set out in the PRA Securitisation Rules; and for occupational pension
schemes they are set out in the Securitisation Regulations and the rules made under them.

Before holding a Note, such an investor must, among other things, verify that the Seller grants credit on the
basis of sound and well-defined criteria and clearly established processes, verify that the Seller retains a
material net economic interest in accordance with the UK Securitisation Framework and that the retention is
disclosed, verify that the information required by the UK Securitisation Framework has been made available,
and carry out a due diligence assessment which enables it to assess the risks involved, including the risk
characteristics of the Notes and of the Mortgage Loans and all the structural features of the transaction
that can materially affect the performance of the Notes, such as the Revenue Priority of Payments and the
Principal Priority of Payments, the Sequential Payment Trigger, the Swap, the reserve funds and the
Step-Up Date. While it holds a Note, the investor must monitor its position and the performance of the
Mortgage Loans under written procedures, perform stress tests appropriate to its position, report to its
management body, and be able to demonstrate to its regulator that it understands the Notes and the Mortgage
Loans and has implemented the required policies and procedures. Failure to comply may result in additional
regulatory capital charges or other measures being imposed on the investor by its regulator.

**EEA institutional investors.** Institutional investors established in the EEA are subject to the due
diligence requirements of Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2402 (as amended, the "EU Securitisation
Regulation"). Where, as in the case of the Seller, the originator is established outside the European Union,
an EEA institutional investor must verify, among other things, that the originator retains on an ongoing basis
a material net economic interest determined in accordance with Article 6 of the EU Securitisation Regulation,
and that the information required by Article 7 of the EU Securitisation Regulation has been made available in
accordance with the frequency and modalities provided for in that Article.

The Seller intends that its retention of randomly selected exposures described above will also satisfy the
requirements of Article 6 of the EU Securitisation Regulation as in force on the Issue Date, and the Seller
is not an entity established or operating for the sole purpose of securitising exposures. However, the Seller
has undertaken to retain its interest only by reference to the UK Securitisation Framework; it has not
undertaken to comply with the EU Securitisation Regulation, and it will make the information described in
this Section 17 available in the forms required by the UK Securitisation Framework, not in the templates
adopted under the EU Securitisation Regulation and not through a securitisation repository registered in the
European Union. Amendments to the EU Securitisation Regulation, including amendments to the information that an
EEA institutional investor must verify in respect of a securitisation whose originator is established outside
the European Union, have been proposed and are in the course of the EU legislative process. Each EEA
institutional investor must make its own assessment, with its own advisers, of whether the information made
available is sufficient for it to comply with Article 5 of the EU Securitisation Regulation as in force from
time to time.

None of the Issuer, the Seller, the Arranger, the Note Trustee and the Security Trustee makes any
representation that the information in this Offering Circular, or the information to be made available after
the Issue Date, is sufficient for any investor to comply with the due diligence requirements that apply to it,
and none of them will monitor the Seller's compliance with its retention undertaking; the Note Trustee and the
Security Trustee will rely on the confirmations in the Investor Reports without further enquiry.

### Simple, Transparent and Standardised Securitisation

The securitisation has not been, and is not intended to be, designated as a simple, transparent and
standardised securitisation within the meaning of the UK Securitisation Framework ("UK STS"). No notification
has been or will be made to the FCA that the securitisation meets the UK STS requirements, and no third party
has been appointed to verify compliance with them. The securitisation is also not a simple, transparent and
standardised securitisation within the meaning of the EU Securitisation Regulation, a designation which is not
available where the originator, the sponsor and the SSPE are established outside the European Union. The
Notes will therefore not benefit from the regulatory treatment which certain investors may apply to UK STS
securitisation positions, and investors should not assume that the securitisation would meet the UK STS
criteria if a notification were made.

### Regulatory Capital and Liquidity Treatment

None of the Issuer, the Seller, the Arranger, the Note Trustee and the Security Trustee makes any
representation to any prospective investor or purchaser of the Notes as to the regulatory capital treatment of
the Notes, as to whether any class of Notes qualifies as high quality liquid assets for the purposes of the
liquidity coverage ratio rules that apply to the investor, or as to the treatment of the Notes under any other
prudential regime, on the Issue Date or at any time in the future.

Investors subject to the prudential requirements of the PRA, or to Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (as amended),
should note that the capital treatment of a securitisation position depends, among other things, on whether
the position is an STS position, on the approach to the calculation of risk-weighted exposure amounts that the
investor is permitted to use and on the investor's compliance with its due diligence requirements. Because the
securitisation is not designated as UK STS, the Notes are not expected to qualify as Level 2B assets under the
liquidity coverage ratio rules that apply to UK institutions, which require, among other conditions, that a
securitisation position be a UK STS position, nor under the equivalent EU rules. Insurance and reinsurance
undertakings subject to Solvency UK or to the EU Solvency II regime should consider the spread risk capital
requirements that apply to securitisation positions which are not STS positions. The prudential rules that
apply to securitisation positions continue to change, and investors should consult their own advisers.

### UK EMIR and the Swap

The Issuer will enter into the Swap with the Swap Provider, as described in Section 15. The Swap is an
over-the-counter derivative contract for the purposes of Regulation (EU) No 648/2012 as it forms part of
domestic law by virtue of the EUWA ("UK EMIR"). The Issuer is not a financial counterparty for the purposes of
UK EMIR and expects to be a non-financial counterparty whose positions in over-the-counter derivative contracts,
excluding contracts entered into to reduce risks directly relating to its commercial activity or treasury
financing activity, do not exceed the clearing thresholds. On that basis the Issuer does not expect to be
subject to the clearing obligation or to the requirement to exchange collateral under the UK EMIR risk
mitigation rules, but it will be subject to the other risk mitigation requirements, such as timely
confirmation and portfolio reconciliation, which the Swap Agreement provides for. As the Swap Provider is a
financial counterparty, it will be responsible under UK EMIR for reporting the Swap to a trade repository on
behalf of both parties. If the Issuer's status under UK EMIR changed, it could become subject to the clearing
obligation or to the margin requirements, which could increase its costs or require the amendment or
termination of the Swap.

### Regulated Mortgage Activities

The Mortgage Loans are regulated mortgage contracts for the purposes of FSMA. Under the Mortgage Sale
Agreement the Issuer will acquire the beneficial interest in the Mortgage Loans, and legal title will remain
with the Seller until the events described in Section 10. The Issuer is not, and does not propose to become,
authorised by the FCA to carry on regulated mortgage activities. It will rely on the exclusion which applies
where the administration of a regulated mortgage contract is carried on, under an arrangement, by a person who
has permission to administer regulated mortgage contracts. The Mortgage Administrator has that permission, and
the Mortgage Administration Agreement requires any substitute mortgage administrator to have it.

### United States Regulatory Matters

**The Volcker Rule.** Section 13 of the United States Bank Holding Company Act of 1956, as amended, and its
implementing regulations (the "Volcker Rule") generally prohibit "banking entities" from acquiring or retaining
an ownership interest in, or sponsoring, a "covered fund", subject to certain exemptions and exclusions. The
Issuer is of the view that it is not, and immediately after the issue of the Notes and the application of the
proceeds will not be, a covered fund, because it may rely on an exemption or exclusion from the definition of
"investment company" under the United States Investment Company Act of 1940, as amended, other than the
exemptions in section 3(c)(1) and section 3(c)(7) of that Act; other exclusions may also be available. No
assurance is given that any regulator or court would agree with that view, and any investor that is a banking
entity should consult its own advisers.

**United States risk retention.** The credit risk retention regulations adopted under Section 15G of the
United States Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended (the "U.S. Risk Retention Rules"), generally require
the sponsor of a securitisation transaction to retain not less than 5 per cent of the credit risk of the
securitised assets. The Seller does not intend to retain a credit risk interest for the purposes of the U.S.
Risk Retention Rules, and intends to rely on the exemption for certain foreign-related transactions. That
exemption is available if, among other conditions, the transaction is not registered under the Securities
Act, no more than 10 per cent of the value of all classes of securities issued is sold or transferred to, or
for the account or benefit of, U.S. persons as defined in the U.S. Risk Retention Rules ("Risk Retention U.S.
Persons"), neither the sponsor nor the issuer is organised under the laws of, or is a branch located in, the
United States, and no more than 25 per cent of the securitised assets were acquired from an affiliate or
branch of the sponsor or the issuer organised or located in the United States. The Notes are offered only
outside the United States in reliance on Regulation S under the Securities Act, and the Notes may not be
purchased by Risk Retention U.S. Persons without the prior written consent of the Seller.

### Benchmarks and Credit Rating Agencies

**Benchmarks.** Interest on the Class A, Class B, Class C and Class D Notes is calculated by reference to
Compounded Daily SONIA, as described in Section 3 and in the Conditions set out in Section 4, and the interest
rate on the Tracker Loans is determined by reference to the Bank of England's Bank Rate. Both are provided by
the Bank of England which, as a central bank, is outside the scope of the UK Benchmarks Regulation, as stated in
the Important Notice. The Issuer is not a supervised entity for the purposes of the requirement of the UK
Benchmarks Regulation to maintain written plans for the cessation of a benchmark. The Conditions set out the
fallback provisions which apply if SONIA ceases to be provided.

**Credit rating agencies.** Each of the Rating Agencies is established in the United Kingdom and registered under
the UK CRA Regulation. The UK CRA Regulation requires an issuer which intends to solicit a credit rating of a
structured finance instrument to appoint at least two credit rating agencies and to consider appointing at
least one credit rating agency with a small market share, and the Issuer has complied with those requirements.
The Class Z Notes will not be rated.
