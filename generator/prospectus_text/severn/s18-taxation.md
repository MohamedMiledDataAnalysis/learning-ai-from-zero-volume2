## Section 18 — United Kingdom Taxation {-}

### General

The following is a general summary of the Issuer's understanding of current United Kingdom tax law and of the
published practice of HM Revenue & Customs ("HMRC"), which may not be binding on HMRC, in relation to:

(a) the United Kingdom withholding tax treatment of payments of interest on the Notes;

(b) United Kingdom stamp duty and stamp duty reserve tax on the issue and transfer of the Notes and on the
sale of the Mortgage Loans;

(c) the United Kingdom corporation tax treatment of the Issuer under the Securitisation Company Regulations;
and

(d) certain related matters.

It does not deal with any other aspect of the United Kingdom taxation of acquiring, holding or disposing of the
Notes. It relates only to persons who are the absolute beneficial owners of their Notes and of the payments on
them, and it may not apply to certain classes of person, such as dealers in securities, persons connected with
the Issuer, and persons who hold the Notes in connection with a trade carried on in the United Kingdom through a
permanent establishment or branch, to whom special rules may apply. The law and practice described below may
change, possibly with retrospective effect. This summary is not tax advice. Prospective Noteholders who are in
any doubt as to their tax position, or who may be subject to tax in a jurisdiction other than the United
Kingdom, should consult their own professional advisers. The tax law of a Noteholder's jurisdiction and of the
United Kingdom may affect the income received from the Notes.

### Withholding Tax on Interest

**The general rule.** The Issuer is incorporated in England and Wales and is resident in the United Kingdom for
tax purposes, and interest on the Class A Notes, the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes will
have a United Kingdom source. As the Notes may remain outstanding for more than one year, that interest is
expected to be "yearly interest" for United Kingdom tax purposes. A company which pays yearly interest with a
United Kingdom source is generally required to deduct income tax at the basic rate (currently 20 per cent) from
the payment, unless an exemption or relief applies or HMRC has given a direction to the contrary under an
applicable double taxation treaty.

**No quoted Eurobond exemption.** The exemption for "quoted Eurobonds" in section 882 of the Income Tax Act
2007 (the "ITA 2007") applies only to securities which are, and continue to be, listed on a recognised stock
exchange or admitted to trading on a multilateral trading facility operated by a regulated recognised stock
exchange. No application has been or will be made for the Notes to be listed or admitted to trading, and that
exemption is therefore not available.

**The qualifying private placement exemption.** Section 888A of the ITA 2007 and the Income Tax (Qualifying
Private Placement) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/2002) (together, the "QPP Rules") provide that the duty to deduct
income tax does not apply to a payment of interest on a security which is a "qualifying private placement". In
summary, and without describing every condition, the Issuer may pay interest on a Note without deduction under
the QPP Rules where:

(a) the Note is a security representing a loan relationship to which the Issuer, a company, is party as
debtor;

(b) the Note is not listed on a recognised stock exchange;

(c) the term of the Note does not exceed 50 years, a condition which is satisfied because the Final Maturity
Date of the Notes is [[final_maturity_date]];

(d) the aggregate value of the securities issued under the arrangement is not less than £10 million;

(e) the person beneficially entitled to the interest is resident in a "qualifying territory", meaning a
territory with which the United Kingdom has a double taxation treaty containing a non-discrimination
provision, and is not connected with the Issuer;

(f) that person holds the Note for genuine commercial reasons and not as part of a tax advantage scheme, and
the Issuer has issued the Notes for genuine commercial reasons and not as part of a tax advantage scheme; and

(g) the Issuer has received from that person a certificate (a "Creditor Certificate") confirming its
beneficial entitlement to the interest, its residence in a qualifying territory and the absence of any
connection with the Issuer, and the Issuer has no reason to believe that any statement in it is incorrect.

As the Notes are held in global registered form through the clearing systems, the Issuer can apply the QPP
Rules only to interest to which a Noteholder is beneficially entitled and in respect of which a valid Creditor
Certificate has been delivered, through the procedures agreed with the paying agent and the clearing
systems, before the relevant Interest Payment Date.

**Payments to United Kingdom companies and other exempt recipients.** The Issuer may also pay interest on the
Notes without deduction where it reasonably believes, at the time of the payment, that the person beneficially
entitled to the interest is a company resident in the United Kingdom, or a non-resident company carrying on a
trade in the United Kingdom through a permanent establishment which is required to bring the interest into
account in calculating its chargeable profits, or another person listed in sections 933 to 937 of the ITA 2007.
If that belief proves to be incorrect, the Issuer may be liable to account for the tax that it should have
deducted, together with interest and, in certain circumstances, penalties.

**Treaty relief.** A Noteholder resident in a jurisdiction which has a double taxation treaty with the United
Kingdom may be entitled to receive interest without deduction, or subject to deduction at a reduced rate, if
HMRC directs the Issuer accordingly following a claim by that Noteholder in the prescribed form, or may recover
from HMRC some or all of any tax deducted. A direction does not apply to payments made before it is given.

**The Class Z Notes.** The Class Z Notes do not bear interest, and the rules on the deduction of tax from
payments of interest described above therefore do not apply to them. Payments of principal on any class of
Notes may be made without deduction for or on account of United Kingdom income tax.

**Consequences of a deduction.** If interest on the Notes is paid subject to a deduction of United Kingdom
income tax, neither the Issuer nor any other person will be obliged to pay any additional amount to the
Noteholders in respect of the amount deducted, as provided in the Conditions set out in Section 4. A change in
law which requires such a deduction may entitle the Issuer to redeem the Notes in the circumstances set out in
the Conditions.

**Payments under the Swap Agreement.** Payments made by the Swap Provider to the Issuer, and by the Issuer to the
Swap Provider, under the Swap Agreement are not expected to be payments of interest and are therefore not
expected to be subject to any deduction for or on account of United Kingdom tax. If a deduction were required as
a result of a change in law, the consequences would be those set out in the Swap Agreement and described in
Section 15.

### Provision of Information

HMRC has powers to obtain information and documents relating to securities, including information about payments
of interest on the Notes and about the persons for whom, or on whose behalf, those payments are made or
received. A paying agent or other person in the United Kingdom which pays interest to, or receives interest for
the benefit of, a Noteholder may be required to provide that information to HMRC, which may exchange it with the
tax authorities of other jurisdictions under international agreements. The Issuer may also be required to
collect and report information about Noteholders under the International Tax Compliance Regulations 2015 (SI
2015/878), which implement in the United Kingdom the Common Reporting Standard and the intergovernmental
agreement between the United Kingdom and the United States relating to the United States Foreign Account Tax
Compliance Act. Each Noteholder will be required to provide the information reasonably requested for these
purposes, as set out in the Conditions.

### Stamp Duty and Stamp Duty Reserve Tax

**The Notes.** No United Kingdom stamp duty or stamp duty reserve tax ("SDRT") should be payable on the issue of
the Notes, on their delivery into the clearing systems, or on a transfer of the Notes or of interests in them,
whether by book-entry transfer within the clearing systems or by an instrument of transfer of a Note in
definitive form. That is because the Notes are expected to constitute "loan capital" within section 78 of the
Finance Act 1986 and to fall within the exemption in section 79(4) of that Act, on the basis that:

(a) they carry no right of conversion into, or acquisition of, shares or other securities;

(b) they carry no right to interest the amount of which exceeds a reasonable commercial return on their nominal
amount, or which is determined by reference to the results of a business or the value of any property; and

(c) they carry no right on repayment to an amount which exceeds their nominal amount and is not reasonably
comparable with what is generally repayable in respect of similar loan capital listed on the Official List.

**The Mortgage Loans.** No United Kingdom stamp duty should be payable on the sale of the English Mortgage Loans
to the Issuer under the Mortgage Sale Agreement, on the Scottish Declaration of Trust, or on the grant of
security by the Issuer under the Deed of Charge, because stamp duty is now chargeable only on instruments
transferring stock or marketable securities, and the Mortgage Loans and their Related Security are neither. No
stamp duty land tax, land and buildings transaction tax or land transaction tax should be payable in respect
of the sale of the Mortgage Loans or of the transfer of legal title on perfection, because an interest held by
way of security for the payment of money is an exempt interest for the purposes of each of those taxes.

### Taxation of the Issuer

**The Securitisation Company Regulations.** The Issuer expects to be a "note-issuing company" within the meaning
of the Securitisation Company Regulations, and therefore a "securitisation company" for their purposes. A
securitisation company is subject to United Kingdom corporation tax by reference to its "retained profit", that
is, broadly, the amount of profit which the Transaction Documents provide that it is to retain for its own
benefit in each accounting period, and not by reference to the profits shown in its statutory accounts. The
amounts which the Issuer receives and pays under the Transaction Documents, including the timing differences
between its accounting profits and its cash flows, the accounting treatment of the Swap and any impairment of
the Mortgage Loans, are disregarded in computing its taxable profits except to the extent of its retained
profit. The Transaction Documents provide for the Issuer to retain a small amount of profit in each accounting
period, and the Issuer expects its liability to corporation tax to be limited to tax on that retained profit at
the prevailing rate.

**Conditions of the regime.** The application of the Securitisation Company Regulations depends on the Issuer
continuing to satisfy their conditions, including conditions relating to the nature of its business and its
assets, the issue of capital market investments as part of a capital market arrangement, the payments which it
makes and receives, and the absence of any arrangement of which the main purpose, or one of the main purposes,
is to secure a tax advantage for any person. The Issuer has covenanted in the Conditions not to take any action
which would cause it to cease to be a securitisation company. If the Issuer ceased to qualify, it could become
liable to tax on its accounting profits, which in any accounting period could exceed its cash income and its
retained profit, as described in Section 1. A change in the tax treatment of the Issuer may entitle the Issuer to
redeem the Notes in the circumstances set out in the Conditions.

**Holdings.** Holdings is not expected to be a securitisation
company. It will be subject to corporation tax on its income and gains under the general rules. Holdings is not
expected to have any significant income, and any tax payable by it will not be an obligation of the Issuer.

**Value added tax.** Supplies of services to the Issuer under the Transaction Documents may be subject to value
added tax ("VAT") at the standard rate, depending on their nature. The Issuer is not expected to be able to
recover input VAT, because its own supplies are exempt or outside the scope of VAT. Any irrecoverable VAT on the
fees of the Mortgage Administrator, the Cash Manager, the Note Trustee and Security Trustee, the Corporate
Services Provider or any other service provider will be an expense of the Issuer and will reduce the funds
available to pay the Noteholders, as described in Section 1.

### Other Tax Considerations

**Noteholders within the charge to corporation tax.** A Noteholder within the charge to United Kingdom
corporation tax will generally be taxed on the return on the Notes, including interest, profits and losses on
disposal and any fair value movements, as income under the loan relationship rules in Part 5 of the Corporation
Tax Act 2009, broadly by reference to the amounts recognised in its accounts in accordance with generally
accepted accounting practice. The Notes are offered only to professional investors, and this summary does not
describe the position of individual Noteholders.

**Noteholders not resident in the United Kingdom.** Interest on the Notes has a United Kingdom source and may
therefore be chargeable to United Kingdom tax by direct assessment even where it is paid without deduction.
However, a Noteholder which is not resident in the United Kingdom and which does not hold the Notes in connection
with a trade carried on in the United Kingdom through a permanent establishment, branch or agency will generally
have no liability to United Kingdom tax on that interest beyond any tax deducted at source.

**Summary only.** This Section 18 is a general summary. It is not a complete analysis of the tax consequences of
acquiring, holding or disposing of the Notes, it is based on the law and practice in force on the date of this
Offering Circular, and none of the Issuer, the Seller, the Arranger, the Note Trustee and the Security Trustee
accepts any responsibility for the tax consequences for any Noteholder.
