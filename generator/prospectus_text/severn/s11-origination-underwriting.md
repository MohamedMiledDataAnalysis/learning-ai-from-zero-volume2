## Section 11 — Origination and Underwriting {-}

The information in this Section 11 has been provided by the Seller and describes the lending criteria and
underwriting procedures that the Seller applied, and applies, to residential mortgage loans to owner-occupiers
(the "Lending Criteria"). The Mortgage Loans in the Provisional Portfolio were originated between 2019 and
2025, as stated in the Loan Warranties, and the Lending Criteria have been amended from time to time during
that period. This Section 11 describes the Lending Criteria as they applied during that period in all
material respects; where an amendment was material to the Mortgage Loans, it is described below. The Seller
may amend the Lending Criteria after the Issue Date, as described under "Changes to the Lending Criteria"
below, and any such amendment will apply to further advances and product switches made after the Issue Date.

This Section 11 does not contain, and should not be read as a restatement of, the warranties given by the
Seller about each Mortgage Loan. Those are the Loan Warranties set out in Section 12, and the remedies for
their breach are set out in the same Section. The characteristics of the Provisional Portfolio on the
Portfolio Reference Date are set out in Section 13.

### Distribution channels

The Seller originates residential mortgage loans through two channels.

(a) **Direct channel.** Applications in the direct channel are taken by the Seller's mortgage advisers in
its branches, by its telephone advisers in its customer contact centre in Worcester, and through its website.
Every regulated mortgage contract sold through the direct channel is sold with advice, given by a mortgage
adviser employed by the Seller who holds a mortgage advice qualification recognised by the FCA and is
assessed as competent under the Seller's training and competence scheme. The Seller does not offer
execution-only sales of new owner-occupied mortgage loans, other than in the limited circumstances permitted
by MCOB 4.8A, and it has not done so for any Mortgage Loan in the Provisional Portfolio.

(b) **Intermediary channel.** The Seller accepts applications from mortgage intermediaries that are authorised
by the FCA with permission to advise on and arrange regulated mortgage contracts, or that are appointed
representatives of such firms, and that are registered with the Seller (each, a "Registered Intermediary").
Registered Intermediaries submit applications through the Seller's intermediary portal. The Seller registers
an intermediary firm only after checking its FCA authorisation and permissions, and it monitors each firm's
submissions for the quality of applications, the incidence of misstatement and fraud, and early arrears. It
suspends or terminates the registration of any firm whose submissions fall below its standards, and it
reports any suspected fraud to the FCA and to the relevant fraud prevention agencies.

Registered Intermediaries are paid a procuration fee by the Seller on completion of each loan. The
procuration fee is a fixed proportion of the loan amount and does not vary with the interest rate, the
product or the loan-to-value ratio of the loan, so that the intermediary has no financial incentive to
recommend one of the Seller's products over another. The Seller discloses the procuration fee in the
illustration given to the borrower, as MCOB requires. The intermediary, not the Seller, gives the advice to
the borrower, but the Seller applies the same Lending Criteria and the same underwriting process to every
application regardless of the channel through which it is received.

Before the Seller receives a full application, the applicant (directly or through a Registered Intermediary)
may obtain a decision in principle. A decision in principle is based on the applicant's declared income and
commitments and on a credit search that leaves a soft footprint on the applicant's credit file. It is not an
offer of a loan and the Seller is not bound by it.

### The application

A full application is made on the Seller's application form, in paper or electronic form, and must be
signed or electronically confirmed by each applicant. The application records, for each applicant, his or her
identity and address history for at least the previous three years, nationality and residency status,
employment and income, existing credit commitments and other committed expenditure, dependants, and the
purpose of the loan; and, for the property, its address, tenure, type and purchase price or estimated value.

Every applicant must be an individual who is at least 18 years of age at the date of the application. The
loan must be repaid by the date on which the youngest applicant reaches the maximum age set in the Lending
Criteria, unless the Seller is satisfied, on evidence, that the applicant will have sufficient income in
retirement to meet the monthly payments for the part of the term that extends beyond his or her expected
retirement date. A maximum of four applicants may be party to a loan, and every person who is a legal owner of
the property must be an applicant.

The Seller lends to applicants who are United Kingdom nationals, nationals of other countries who have
indefinite leave to remain or settled status in the United Kingdom, and, subject to additional criteria,
nationals of other countries who have a right to live and work in the United Kingdom for a period that the
Seller considers sufficient. The Seller verifies the identity and address of every applicant in accordance
with the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations
2017, by electronic verification against credit reference agency data and, where that verification is not
sufficient, by examination of documents.

### Credit search and credit scoring

The Seller obtains a full credit search on every applicant from at least one credit reference agency. The
credit search records the applicant's existing credit commitments and their conduct, any county court
judgments (in England and Wales) and decrees (in Scotland), any bankruptcy, sequestration, individual voluntary
arrangement, protected trust deed or debt relief order, and the applicant's registration on the electoral
roll. The Seller declines any application in which an applicant:

(a) is an undischarged bankrupt, or has had his or her estate sequestrated and has not been discharged;

(b) has been the subject of a bankruptcy order, sequestration, individual voluntary arrangement, protected
trust deed or debt relief order within the period specified in the Lending Criteria before the date of the
application, whether or not since discharged or completed;

(c) has a county court judgment or decree registered against him or her in the period specified in the
Lending Criteria, above the value specified in them, that has not been satisfied;

(d) has had a property taken into possession by a lender, or has given up a property voluntarily to a lender,
at any time; or

(e) has missed payments on an existing mortgage, secured loan or rent obligation in the period specified in
the Lending Criteria before the application, beyond the number permitted by them.

Applications that pass these policy rules are scored by the Seller's application scorecard. The scorecard is a
statistical model, developed by the Seller's credit risk function from the performance of its own mortgage
lending and from credit reference agency data, that estimates the probability that a loan will fall into
serious arrears within a defined outcome period. It uses the applicant's credit file data, the applicant's
declared information and the characteristics of the loan. The score determines whether the application is
accepted, referred to an underwriter or declined. The scorecard is recalibrated at least every two years and
monitored quarterly by the credit risk function, and every change to it, including a change to a cut-off
score, is approved under the Seller's model governance framework. The scorecard in use from 2019 to 2021 was
replaced by a redeveloped scorecard in the second half of 2021; the redevelopment did not change the Seller's
credit risk appetite.

The Seller does not lend to applicants who are assessed as having a significant adverse credit history, and
it does not offer, and has not offered during the period in which the Mortgage Loans were originated, any
product designed for borrowers with impaired credit records. The Seller has not originated any self-certified
mortgage loan, and every Mortgage Loan in the Provisional Portfolio was originated after the verification of
income described below.

### Affordability

The Seller assesses the affordability of every loan in accordance with the responsible lending rules in
MCOB 11.6. Under those rules the Seller may not enter into a regulated mortgage contract, or vary one in a way
that increases the amount borrowed, unless it can demonstrate that the loan is affordable for the borrower,
having taken full account of the borrower's income and committed and basic essential expenditure, together
with the effect of reasonably expected future interest rate increases.

The affordability assessment has three elements.

(a) **Income.** The Seller determines the applicant's net income, after tax and national insurance
contributions, from the verified gross income described under "Verification of income" below. It includes
basic salary in full and, subject to evidence of regularity over the period required by the Lending Criteria,
a proportion of overtime, commission, bonus and allowances. It may include, subject to evidence, income from
pensions, from maintenance payments under a court order or a registered agreement, and from certain state
benefits. It does not include income from the letting of a room in the mortgaged property unless the Lending
Criteria expressly permit it.

(b) **Expenditure.** The Seller deducts from net income the applicant's committed expenditure, being the
payments due on other credit commitments, including any that the applicant has declared will be repaid on or
before completion only where the Seller has evidence that they will be; child care costs; maintenance
payments; and other contractual commitments. It then deducts basic essential expenditure and basic quality of
living costs. For those it uses the higher of the amount declared by the applicant and the amount produced by
the Seller's expenditure model, which estimates the expenditure of a household of the applicant's composition,
income and location from statistics published by the Office for National Statistics and is updated at least
once a year. The Seller does not accept a declared expenditure that is lower than its modelled expenditure
without evidence.

(c) **Interest rate stress.** The Seller calculates the monthly payment on the loan at a stressed interest
rate (the "Affordability Stress Rate") and requires the applicant's net income, after the deductions in
paragraph (b), to cover it. In accordance with MCOB 11.6.18, the Affordability Stress Rate reflects market
expectations of interest rates over at least the first five years of the loan. For a loan with an initial fixed
rate of five years or more, the Seller may apply a lower stress to the initial fixed rate, as MCOB permits. The
Affordability Stress Rate is set by the Seller's assets and liabilities committee, reviewed at least quarterly
by reference to market interest rate expectations, the Bank Rate and the Seller's standard variable rate, and
applies equally to every channel. Before August 2022 the Seller also applied the affordability test
recommended by the Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee, in addition to its own stress; that
recommendation was withdrawn with effect from August 2022 and the Seller has applied its own Affordability
Stress Rate alone since then.

In addition to the affordability assessment, the Seller limits the amount it will lend by reference to a
multiple of the applicants' gross income, which varies with the loan-to-value ratio of the loan and the level
of income. The Seller manages the proportion of its new lending at high loan-to-income multiples within the
limit on such lending that the PRA applies to mortgage lenders in accordance with the Financial Policy
Committee's recommendation, and within the lower internal limit set by its board risk committee.

Where the term of the loan extends beyond the expected retirement date of an applicant, the Seller assesses
affordability for the part of the term after retirement on the applicant's expected retirement income, as
MCOB 11.6 requires. Where the loan is on an interest-only basis, in whole or in part, the Seller assesses
affordability on a capital and interest basis, as described under "Interest-only and part and part loans" below.

### Verification of income

The Seller verifies the income of every applicant in every channel, as MCOB 11.6 requires, by evidence that
is independent of the applicant. The evidence required depends on the source of income.

(a) **Employed applicants.** The Seller requires the applicant's most recent payslips for the period specified
in the Lending Criteria, together with the most recent form P60 where variable income is included. It may
verify the income electronically, by checking credit reference agency current account turnover data or by an
open banking connection with the applicant's consent, in which case the Seller may reduce the documents it
requires. Where the applicant has been in his or her current employment for a short period or is on a fixed
term contract, the Seller requires additional evidence of continuity of income.

(b) **Self-employed applicants.** The Seller requires evidence of income for at least the two most recent
complete tax years, in the form of the tax calculations and tax year overviews issued by HMRC or accounts
certified by a qualified accountant, and it uses the average of those years or, if lower, the most recent
year. For an applicant who is a director of a limited company holding a material shareholding, the Seller
assesses salary and dividends and may, subject to the Lending Criteria, take account of the applicant's share
of retained profit.

(c) **Other income.** Pension income is verified by a pension statement or bank statements; maintenance and
benefit income by the court order, agreement or benefit award letter and bank statements.

Underwriters check the documents for consistency with each other, with the application and with the credit
search, and the Seller's fraud prevention team reviews every application that its fraud detection rules
identify for investigation. The Seller uses a national fraud database and shares data with other lenders
through it, as permitted by data protection law.

### Valuation

Every property offered as security must be valued for the Seller before the Seller makes an offer of loan.
The valuation is carried out in one of two ways.

(a) **Physical valuation.** A physical valuation is carried out by a valuer who is a member or fellow of the
Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors and a registered valuer under its valuation standards, and who is
instructed through the Seller's panel of valuation firms, managed for it by a panel management company. The
valuer inspects the property, reports on its condition, tenure, construction and suitability as security, and
gives an opinion of its market value. The Seller does not accept valuations instructed by the applicant or by
an intermediary.

(b) **Automated valuation.** For applications that meet the criteria set by the Seller's credit risk function,
the Seller may rely on an automated valuation model ("AVM") instead of a physical valuation. The Seller uses
an AVM supplied by an independent provider, which estimates the value of the property from comparable sales,
Land Registry and Registers of Scotland price data and the characteristics of the property, and returns a
confidence score. The Seller accepts an AVM valuation only where the confidence score meets the level set in
the Lending Criteria, the property is of standard construction, and the loan-to-value ratio and the loan amount
are within the limits set for AVM valuations, which are lower than those for physical valuations. AVM
valuations are used principally for remortgages and are not used for new-build properties or properties of
non-standard construction.

The Seller lends on freehold houses, leasehold houses and flats and, in Scotland, heritable property. A
leasehold property must have an unexpired lease term at the end of the mortgage term that meets the Lending
Criteria, and the ground rent and service charge must be within acceptable limits. The Seller does not lend
on properties of a construction type that its valuation panel classes as unsuitable for mortgage purposes, on
properties with agricultural restrictions, on properties used wholly or mainly for business purposes, or on
properties that are not to be occupied by the borrower as his or her main residence.

For new-build properties, the Seller requires the property to be covered by a structural warranty from a
provider that it accepts, and it applies lower maximum loan-to-value ratios to new-build flats and to
properties purchased from a developer with incentives, in accordance with the disclosure of incentives
standard used in the industry. The valuation of a new-build property is based on its market value without the
benefit of incentives.

The loan-to-value ratio of each Mortgage Loan at origination was calculated as the amount of the loan,
including any fees added to it, divided by the lower of the purchase price and the valuation of the property
(in the case of a purchase) or the valuation (in the case of a remortgage). The maximum loan-to-value ratio
permitted by the Loan Warranties is set out in Section 12, and the loan-to-value ratios of the Provisional
Portfolio are set out in Section 13.

### Underwriting and lending authorities

Every application that is not declined by the policy rules or by the scorecard is reviewed by an underwriter
before an offer is issued. Underwriters are employed by the Seller in its mortgage operations centre; the
Seller does not delegate any underwriting decision to an intermediary or to any third party. The underwriter
checks the application against the Lending Criteria, reviews the evidence of identity and income, confirms the
affordability assessment, reviews the valuation and the conveyancing requirements, and either approves the
application, refers it for a higher authority, requests further information or declines it.

Each underwriter holds a personal lending mandate (a "Lending Authority"), granted by the director of lending
and recorded on the mortgage administration system, that limits the loan amount and the loan-to-value ratio
up to which he or she may approve an application, and the types of case that he or she may approve. The
Seller has four tiers of Lending Authority, from trainee underwriters, whose decisions are all reviewed by a
senior underwriter, to the senior credit managers in the credit risk function. An application above an
underwriter's Lending Authority must be approved by an underwriter with a sufficient one. Applications for
the largest loan amounts permitted by the Lending Criteria must be approved by two holders of the highest tier
of Lending Authority, one of whom must be a member of the credit risk function.

The Seller's quality assurance team reviews a sample of the cases approved by each underwriter every month,
including every case approved by an underwriter in his or her first six months, and reports the results to
the credit risk function.

### Exceptions to the Lending Criteria

An underwriter holding the appropriate Lending Authority may approve an application that falls outside a
non-mandatory element of the Lending Criteria (an "Exception") where he or she is satisfied that the loan is
affordable and that the application presents an acceptable credit risk, and records the reasons. Exceptions
may be approved for matters such as the length of employment, the treatment of a particular source of income
or the age of a payslip. No Exception may be approved to an element of the Lending Criteria that the Seller
classifies as mandatory, which include the affordability assessment, the verification of income, the
requirements as to bankruptcy and possession, the maximum loan-to-value ratio and the requirement for owner
occupation.

The proportion of applications approved by Exception is monitored monthly by the credit risk function against
a limit set by the board risk committee, and the performance of loans approved by Exception is tracked
separately. Mortgage Loans approved by Exception are included in the Provisional Portfolio if they satisfy the
Loan Warranties.

### Offer and completion

When an application is approved the Seller issues a mortgage offer to the applicant, together with the
European Standardised Information Sheet required by MCOB 6A. An offer is valid for the period stated in it,
normally six months for a purchase. Completion is carried out by a conveyancing solicitor or licensed
conveyancer in England and Wales, or a solicitor in Scotland, who is a member of the Seller's conveyancing
panel and who acts for the Seller as well as for the borrower, unless the borrower's own conveyancer is
separately approved. The conveyancer must report on title in accordance with the standard instructions of the
Seller, and the Seller releases the loan only after a satisfactory report on title.

The Seller requires the mortgage over a property in England or Wales to be registered at HM Land Registry as a
first legal charge, and the standard security over a property in Scotland to be registered in the Land
Register of Scotland as a first-ranking standard security. The Seller requires the property to be insured
against fire and other usual risks for at least its reinstatement value, by buildings insurance arranged by
the borrower or, for a leasehold flat, under a block policy arranged by the landlord or management company. The
Seller does not require life cover as a condition of the loan.

### Products

The Seller offers its owner-occupied mortgage loans on the following interest rate bases. Each Mortgage Loan
is, at any time, on one of these bases, and the proportion of the Provisional Portfolio on each basis on the
Portfolio Reference Date is set out in Section 13.

(a) **Fixed rate products.** The loan bears interest at a fixed rate for an initial period, which for the
Mortgage Loans is two or five years (the "Fixed Rate Period"), after which it bears interest at the Seller's
Standard Variable Rate unless the borrower takes a new product. Mortgage Loans in their Fixed Rate Period are
"Fixed Rate Loans". Most fixed rate products carry an early repayment charge, expressed as a declining
proportion of the amount repaid, if the borrower repays the loan, or more than a permitted annual overpayment,
during the Fixed Rate Period. The interest rate risk that arises from Fixed Rate Loans is hedged by the Swap
described in Section 15.

(b) **Tracker products.** The loan bears interest at a margin over the Bank of England's Bank Rate (the "Bank
Rate"), either for an initial period or for the whole term (the "Tracker Loans"). The rate changes on the
first day of the month following a change in the Bank Rate, or on such other date as the offer states. Tracker
Loans are subject to a minimum rate, which is the margin over the Bank Rate set out in the offer, so that the
rate on a Tracker Loan cannot fall below that margin.

(c) **Standard Variable Rate.** The Seller's standard variable rate (the "Standard Variable Rate") is the
variable rate set by the Seller from time to time and applying to loans whose product period has ended. The
Seller may change the Standard Variable Rate in the circumstances set out in the mortgage conditions, which
include changes in the Bank Rate and in the Seller's cost of funds, and must give borrowers notice of any
change as the mortgage conditions and MCOB require. After the Issue Date the Mortgage Administrator will set
the Standard Variable Rate applicable to the Mortgage Loans on behalf of the Issuer, as described in Section 14.
Loans on the Standard Variable Rate are not subject to early repayment charges.

The Seller does not offer, and none of the Mortgage Loans is, an offset mortgage, a flexible mortgage with a
facility to redraw, a discounted variable rate product, a capped rate product or a loan in a currency other
than sterling. Overpayments are permitted on every product, subject to early repayment charges where they
apply, and reduce either the monthly payment or the term at the borrower's choice.

### Interest-only and part and part loans

The Seller offers interest-only loans and loans repayable partly on an interest-only basis and partly on a
capital and interest basis ("part and part" loans) to owner-occupiers who meet the following additional
criteria, which applied throughout the period in which the Mortgage Loans were originated:

(a) the borrower must have a repayment strategy for the interest-only part of the loan that is credible and
verified at the time of the application, as the responsible lending rules in MCOB 11.6 require for interest-only lending, and the Seller accepts as a repayment
strategy only the sale of the mortgaged property (subject to the minimum equity requirement in the Lending
Criteria), investments and pension lump sums of the types specified in the Lending Criteria, or a combination
of them;

(b) the amount on an interest-only basis, and the overall loan-to-value ratio, must be within the lower limits
set by the Lending Criteria for interest-only lending;

(c) the borrower must meet the minimum income requirement set by the Lending Criteria for interest-only
lending; and

(d) affordability is assessed on a capital and interest basis over the term of the loan, at the Affordability
Stress Rate, so that the borrower could afford to convert the loan to a capital and interest basis if the
repayment strategy failed.

The Mortgage Administrator contacts every interest-only borrower at intervals during the term, and in any case
in the years before the maturity of the loan, to ask the borrower to confirm that the repayment strategy
remains in place, in accordance with the FCA's guidance on the fair treatment of interest-only customers. The
treatment of interest-only loans that are not repaid at maturity is described in Section 14. The proportion of
the Provisional Portfolio repayable on an interest-only or part and part basis is set out in Section 13, and
the risks of such loans are described in Section 1.

### Further advances

A borrower may apply to the Seller for a further advance secured by the existing mortgage or standard security.
An application for a further advance is assessed under the Lending Criteria applying at the date of the
application, including a new affordability assessment, a credit search and, where required by the Lending
Criteria, a new valuation, which may be an AVM valuation.

The Issuer will not fund any further advance. If the Seller agrees to make a further advance on a Mortgage
Loan after the Issue Date, the Seller will repurchase that Mortgage Loan, and its Related Security, from the
Issuer before the further advance is made, at a price equal to its current balance plus accrued interest, under
the Mortgage Sale Agreement. The repurchase price will form part of the Available Principal Receipts and the
Available Revenue Receipts, as described in Section 15. As a result, no Mortgage Loan in the portfolio of the
Issuer will at any time have been increased by a further advance made after the Issue Date.

### Product switches and retention

On or before the end of a product period, the Seller may offer the borrower a new product, which may be a new
fixed rate or tracker product, or a change of the borrower's terms (a "Product Switch"). A Product Switch that
does not increase the amount borrowed does not require a new affordability assessment: the provisions of MCOB 11.6 and 11.7 allow a lender not to
carry out a full affordability assessment where an existing borrower switches product without increasing the
amount borrowed, and the Seller applies them where the borrower is up to date with payments. A Product Switch that increases the amount
borrowed is a further advance and is treated as described above.

A Mortgage Loan that is the subject of a Product Switch after the Issue Date will remain in the portfolio of
the Issuer only if the conditions set out in the Mortgage Sale Agreement are satisfied on the date of the
Product Switch (the "Product Switch Conditions"). The Product Switch Conditions include requirements that:

(a) the new product is a fixed rate product, a tracker product or a variable rate product of a type offered
by the Seller to its owner-occupied borrowers at the Issue Date, and the Mortgage Loan continues to be repayable
in monthly instalments;

(b) the final maturity date of the Mortgage Loan is not extended beyond the date permitted by the Loan
Warranties set out in Section 12;

(c) the Mortgage Loan is not, on the date of the Product Switch, in arrears beyond the level permitted by the
Mortgage Sale Agreement;

(d) the interest rate on the new product is not lower than the minimum rate specified in the Mortgage Sale
Agreement, by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA and the rates on the Notes; and

(e) no Sequential Payment Trigger has occurred and the aggregate current balance of Mortgage Loans that have
been the subject of a Product Switch to a new fixed rate product does not exceed the limit specified in the
Mortgage Sale Agreement.

If the Product Switch Conditions are not satisfied, the Seller will repurchase the Mortgage Loan on or before
the date of the Product Switch at its current balance plus accrued interest. The Seller is under no obligation
to offer a Product Switch to any borrower, and its decisions to offer products to borrowers will be made in the
ordinary course of its business, without regard to whether the Mortgage Loan has been sold to the Issuer. A
Mortgage Loan that becomes a Fixed Rate Loan as a result of a Product Switch after the Issue Date will not be
hedged by the Swap, as described in Section 15, and the resulting risk is described in Section 1.

### Changes to the Lending Criteria

The Seller may change the Lending Criteria from time to time. Every change is approved by the Seller's credit
committee, and any change that is material to the Seller's credit risk appetite is approved by its board risk
committee. A change to the Lending Criteria after the Issue Date will apply to Mortgage Loans only in relation
to further advances and Product Switches made after the change; it will not affect the Loan Warranties, which
are given on the Issue Date by reference to the Lending Criteria under which each Mortgage Loan was
originated.

The material changes to the Lending Criteria during the period in which the Mortgage Loans were originated
were:

(a) the replacement of the application scorecard in 2021, described under "Credit search and credit scoring"
above;

(b) the withdrawal of the Financial Policy Committee's affordability test in August 2022, after which the
Seller relied on its own Affordability Stress Rate alone, described under "Affordability" above;

(c) the increase in the Seller's modelled expenditure figures in 2022 and 2023, to reflect the increase in the
cost of living in those years; and

(d) the extension of AVM valuations to a wider range of remortgage applications in 2024, within the limits set
by the credit risk function.

None of these changes, in the opinion of the Seller, resulted in Mortgage Loans being originated to a credit
standard materially lower than that applied to the remainder of the Provisional Portfolio. The Seller has
applied to the Mortgage Loans the same sound and well-defined criteria for credit-granting that it applies to
comparable mortgage loans that it has not securitised, as required by the Securitisation Regulations 2024
(SI 2024/102) and SECN, as described in Section 17.
