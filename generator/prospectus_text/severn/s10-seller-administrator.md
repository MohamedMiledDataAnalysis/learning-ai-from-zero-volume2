### Constitution and legal status

The Society is a building society incorporated in England and Wales under the Building Societies Act 1986
(the "1986 Act"). Its principal office is in Worcester. The Society is a mutual organisation: it has no share
capital held by external shareholders, and it is owned by its members, who are the holders of its share
accounts and its borrowing members. The Society's purpose, as stated in its memorandum under section 5 of the
1986 Act, is making loans which are secured on residential property and are funded substantially by its
members. The rules and memorandum of the Society, together with the 1986 Act, govern the relationship between
the Society and its members, including the right of members to attend and vote at general meetings, to elect
the directors of the Society and to receive the summary financial statement and the annual business statement
required by the 1986 Act.

The 1986 Act imposes two structural limits on every building society. Under section 6 of the 1986 Act (the
"lending limit"), at least 75 per cent of the business assets of a building society, other than its liquid
assets and fixed assets, must be loans fully secured on residential property. Under section 7 of the 1986 Act
(the "funding limit"), at least 50 per cent of the funds raised by a building society, other than from its
reserves, must be raised in the form of shares held by individual members. The Society has complied with both
limits throughout each of the five financial years before the Issue Date, and the transfer of the Provisional
Portfolio to the Issuer on [[issue_date]] has been taken into account in the Society's assessment of its
compliance with them going forward. The 1986 Act also restricts the activities a building society may carry on
and the kinds of transactions it may enter into for hedging purposes; the Society has received legal advice
that its entry into the Transaction Documents falls within its powers.

### History

The Society traces its origins to the Marlowe Permanent Benefit Building Society, founded in Worcester in 1871
by a group of local tradesmen and nonconformist ministers who wished to help working families buy their own
homes. In its first fifty years the Society lent almost exclusively within the county of Worcestershire,
funding its loans from the savings of local members. Between 1928 and 1974 the Society grew by a series of
twelve transfers of engagements from smaller societies in the West Midlands, the Welsh Marches and the
Severn valley, and in 1975 it adopted its present name.

The Society's members rejected a proposal to convert the Society into a bank in 1997, when several large
building societies demutualised. The board at the time concluded, and every subsequent board has
reaffirmed, that the Society's long-term interest and that of its members is best served by remaining a
mutual. In 2009 the Society accepted a transfer of engagements from a smaller regional society based in
Gloucestershire, which added 14 branches and approximately 41,000 members. Since 2012 the Society's growth has
been organic.

The Society opened its intermediary lending channel in 2004 and began lending throughout England, Wales and
Scotland in 2011. It began lending in Scotland on the security of standard securities over Scottish
residential property in 2016, through intermediaries and, from 2019, through its branch in Edinburgh. The
Society has offered regulated mortgage contracts as defined in the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000
(Regulated Activities) Order 2001 since the regulation of mortgage lending began on 31 October 2004.

### Membership, branches and business

On 31 December 2025 the Society had approximately 712,000 members, of whom approximately 618,000 were
investing members and approximately 94,000 were borrowing members. It is, by total assets, one of the larger
regional building societies in the United Kingdom. The Group's principal business is the provision of
residential mortgage loans to owner-occupiers and the provision of savings accounts to individuals. The Group
also offers home and contents insurance as an introducer appointed representative of an insurer, and
financial advice through a referral arrangement with an independent firm. The Society does not provide
current accounts, credit cards or unsecured personal loans.

The Society operates a network of 63 branches, located principally in the West Midlands, the South West,
Wales and the Welsh Marches, together with branches in London, Bristol, Manchester and Edinburgh. Its head
office, its mortgage operations centre and its customer contact centre are in Worcester, and a second
operations site in Cardiff provides resilience for the mortgage administration functions described in
Section 14. On 31 December 2025 the Group employed approximately 2,140 people, of whom approximately 390 work
in mortgage underwriting, administration and arrears management.

The Society's lending strategy concentrates on prime owner-occupied lending to borrowers with a stable income
history, a satisfactory credit record and adequate equity in the property. The Society also lends, through a
separate brand, to landlords of residential property let on assured shorthold tenancies (in England) and
private residential tenancies (in Scotland), and offers a small range of shared ownership, retirement
interest-only and later-life products. None of those buy-to-let, shared ownership or later-life loans forms part
of the Provisional Portfolio, as set out in the warranties given by the Seller in Section 12 (the "Loan Warranties").

### Regulation

The Society is authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority (the "PRA") and regulated by the Financial
Conduct Authority (the "FCA") and the PRA under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 ("FSMA"). It holds
permissions under Part 4A of FSMA to accept deposits, to enter into regulated mortgage contracts as lender, to
administer regulated mortgage contracts and to arrange and advise on regulated mortgage contracts, among other
activities. It is a "dual-regulated firm": the PRA is responsible for its prudential supervision, including
its capital, liquidity and resolvability, and the FCA is responsible for the conduct of its business with
customers, including its compliance with the Mortgages and Home Finance: Conduct of Business sourcebook
("MCOB") and with the Consumer Duty set out in Principle 12 of the FCA's Principles for Businesses and in
PRIN 2A.

The Society's eligible deposits are protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme, up to the limits
applicable under the PRA Rulebook. The Society is subject to the special resolution regime under the Banking
Act 2009, as applied to building societies by the Building Societies Act 1986 and the related secondary
legislation, under which the Bank of England, as the United Kingdom resolution authority, may exercise
stabilisation powers in respect of the Society if the conditions for resolution are met. The consequences of
the exercise of those powers for the Transaction Documents are described in Section 1.

The Society's mortgage business is subject to the jurisdiction of the Financial Ombudsman Service, to which an
eligible borrower may refer a complaint after the Society has issued its final response or after eight weeks
from the date of the complaint. The Society reports its complaint data to the FCA twice a year and publishes
the complaint figures it is required to publish under the FCA's dispute resolution rules.

The Society is also subject to the Building Societies Act 1986 as supervised by the PRA, to the Money
Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017, to the UK
General Data Protection Regulation and the Data Protection Act 2018 in its processing of borrower data, and,
in relation to the Scottish Mortgage Loans, to the law of Scotland on heritable security. The Society has not
been the subject of any public censure, fine or enforcement action by the FCA or the PRA in the ten years
before the Issue Date.

### Mortgage book

The Society's residential mortgage book consists principally of loans to owner-occupiers secured by
first-ranking legal mortgages over residential property in England and Wales and by first-ranking standard
securities over residential property in Scotland. On 31 December 2025 the Society's owner-occupied book
comprised approximately 81,000 accounts with an aggregate balance of approximately £11.9 billion; its
buy-to-let book comprised approximately 9,600 accounts with an aggregate balance of approximately
£1.2 billion; and its shared ownership and later-life books together comprised the balance of its lending.

The owner-occupied book is concentrated in the regions in which the Society has its branches and its
strongest intermediary relationships, being the West Midlands, the South West, the South East and Wales, with
smaller exposures in London, the North of England and Scotland. The majority of loans in the owner-occupied
book bear interest at a fixed rate for an initial period of two or five years, after which they revert to the
Society's standard variable rate unless the borrower takes a new product. The remainder bear interest at a
rate that tracks the Bank of England's Bank Rate or at the Society's standard variable rate. The products are
described in Section 11.

The Society's gross new lending to owner-occupiers in 2025 was approximately £2.6 billion, of which
approximately two thirds was originated through mortgage intermediaries and one third through the Society's
branches, its contact centre and its website. Retention of existing borrowers at the end of their product
periods, by product switch, is an important source of the Society's lending; product switches are not
included in the gross new lending figure.

Arrears and possessions on the Society's owner-occupied book have historically been below the averages for
regulated first-charge mortgage lending published by the FCA and the Bank of England in their mortgage lending
statistics. The number of properties taken into possession by the Society in each of the last five financial
years has been small in absolute terms. The historical performance of the Society's owner-occupied mortgage
loans, including dynamic arrears, cumulative default and recovery data in the form required by the
Securitisation Regulations, is made available to prospective investors as described in Section 17. That
historical data relates to the Society's book as a whole and is not a forecast of the performance of the
Provisional Portfolio. Past performance is no guarantee of future performance.

### Funding and prior transactions

The Society funds its lending principally from the savings balances of its members. It supplements that
retail funding with wholesale funding, which provides diversification of maturity and of source. The
Society's wholesale funding programmes have, since 2013, included:

(a) public and retained issues of residential mortgage-backed securities, in which the Society has acted as
seller, mortgage administrator and cash manager, backed by owner-occupied residential mortgage loans
originated by the Society;

(b) a regulated covered bond programme admitted to the register of regulated covered bonds maintained by the
FCA under the Regulated Covered Bonds Regulations 2008, under which the Society has issued covered bonds
guaranteed by a limited liability partnership and secured over a portfolio of the Society's residential
mortgage loans;

(c) senior unsecured and senior non-preferred debt issued under a medium term note programme; and

(d) drawings under the Bank of England's Sterling Monetary Framework, including against portfolios of
mortgage loans pre-positioned with the Bank of England as collateral.

Every residential mortgage-backed securities transaction sponsored by the Society to date has paid interest and
principal in full when due, and in none of them has an event of default occurred or the Society, as mortgage
administrator, been replaced. The Society's previous transactions differ from the transaction described in
this offering circular in their size, the characteristics of their mortgage pools, the structure of their notes
and their credit enhancement, and their performance is not an indication of the performance of the Notes.

No Mortgage Loan in the Provisional Portfolio is, on the Portfolio Reference Date, subject to any security
interest or trust in favour of the covered bond programme referred to in paragraph (b), to any earlier
securitisation referred to in paragraph (a) or to the Bank of England under paragraph (d). Any Mortgage Loan
that was previously allocated to any such arrangement has been released from it before its selection for the
Provisional Portfolio, in accordance with the terms of that arrangement.

### Board and management

The board of the Society consists of a non-executive chair, seven independent non-executive directors and
three executive directors, being the chief executive, the chief financial officer and the chief operating
officer. The board has established an audit committee, a board risk committee, a remuneration committee and a
nominations committee, each chaired by an independent non-executive director. The Society applies the UK
Corporate Governance Code, so far as it is relevant to a building society, and reports annually on how it has
done so.

The Society's mortgage business is managed by its director of lending, who reports to the chief operating
officer. The director of lending is responsible for the Lending Criteria described in Section 11, for the
underwriting and mortgage operations functions, and for the collections and recoveries function that
administers the Mortgage Loans in arrears. The Society's credit risk function, which reports to the chief risk
officer, is independent of the lending function. It sets the credit risk appetite approved by the board risk
committee, monitors the performance of the mortgage book against it, and approves every change to the Lending
Criteria and to the Society's credit scoring models.

### The Society as Seller

On [[issue_date]], under the mortgage sale agreement between the Seller, the Issuer and the Security Trustee
(the "Mortgage Sale Agreement"), the Seller will sell to the Issuer the Mortgage Loans comprised in the
Provisional Portfolio, together with the mortgages, standard securities, insurance rights and other rights
securing or relating to them (the "Related Security"), as described in Section 12. The Mortgage Loans
were selected from the Society's owner-occupied mortgage book by a random selection process applied to the
loans that satisfied the Loan Warranties on the Portfolio Reference Date, and the Seller will keep those loans on
its balance sheet as its retention, as described in Section 17.

The sale of the English Mortgage Loans will take effect in equity only, and the sale of the Scottish
Mortgage Loans will be effected by a declaration of trust governed by Scottish law (the "Scottish Declaration
of Trust") under which the Seller holds the Scottish Mortgage Loans and their Related Security on trust for
the Issuer. Legal title to the Mortgage Loans will remain with the Seller until the occurrence of one of the
events specified in the Mortgage Sale Agreement, which include the insolvency of the Seller and the
termination of its appointment as Mortgage Administrator (each, a "Perfection Event"), as described in
Section 12. Until
then the Seller will remain the lender of record, the borrowers will not be notified of the sale, and the
Seller will continue to act as lender in all dealings with the borrowers, in its capacity as Mortgage
Administrator.

The Seller's obligations as seller include the repurchase of any Mortgage Loan for which a Loan Warranty proves
untrue and the breach cannot be remedied, as described in Section 12; the repurchase of any Mortgage Loan to
which it agrees to make a further advance, and of any Mortgage Loan that is the subject of a product switch
that does not satisfy the conditions described in Section 11; and the funding of the General Reserve Fund and
the Liquidity Reserve Fund by the Subordinated Loan, as described in Section 5.

### The Society as Mortgage Administrator

Under the mortgage administration agreement between the Mortgage Administrator, the Issuer, the Seller and the
Security Trustee (the "Mortgage Administration Agreement"), the Society will administer the Mortgage Loans on
behalf of the Issuer for the fee set out in Section 14. It will administer them with the same level of skill,
care and diligence as it applies to comparable mortgage loans that it holds for its own account, and in
accordance with its policies and procedures from time to time, the requirements of MCOB, the Consumer Duty
and the other requirements of the FCA, and the terms of the Mortgage Loans.

The Society's mortgage administration functions comprise the setting up of new accounts, the collection of
monthly payments and the application of receipts, the administration of interest rate changes and of product
maturities, the handling of redemptions, the administration of buildings insurance requirements, the
management of arrears and of borrowers in financial difficulty, and the enforcement of the Related Security.
Those functions are carried out by the Society's own staff in Worcester and Cardiff on the Society's mortgage
administration system, which the Society has used since 2017 and which is supported under a long-term
licence and maintenance agreement with its software supplier. The Society maintains business continuity and
disaster recovery arrangements for that system, which it tests at least once a year. The servicing and arrears
management of the Mortgage Loans, the termination of the Mortgage Administrator's appointment and the back-up
arrangements are described in Section 14.

The Society may sub-contract or delegate any of its functions as Mortgage Administrator, including the
printing and dispatch of statements, the provision of solicitors and of asset managers for the conduct of
possession proceedings and the sale of properties in possession, and the tracing of borrowers. The Society will
remain responsible for the performance of any function it sub-contracts or delegates, and the appointment of a
sub-contractor will not relieve it of any of its obligations under the Mortgage Administration Agreement.

### The Society as Cash Manager

Under the cash management agreement between the Cash Manager, the Issuer and the Security Trustee (the "Cash
Management Agreement"), the Society will manage the Issuer Accounts (as defined in Section 15), maintain the ledgers of the Issuer, calculate
the Available Revenue Receipts and the Available Principal Receipts, and apply them on each Interest Payment
Date in the orders set out in Sections 6 and 7, for the fee set out in Section 14. The cash management
functions are carried out by the Society's treasury operations team, which is separate from the Society's
mortgage administration functions. They are described in Section 15.

### Conflicts of interest

The Society acts in several capacities in this transaction: as Seller, Mortgage Administrator, Cash Manager,
Subordinated Loan provider and holder of the retained exposures. Its interests in
one capacity may conflict with its interests in another, or with the interests of the Noteholders. For
example, the Society may offer products to borrowers in the Provisional Portfolio that encourage them to
redeem their Mortgage Loans or to switch to products that result in the repurchase of their Mortgage Loans, and
it will determine the standard variable rate applicable to the Mortgage Loans and to the other mortgage loans
in its book. The Society has agreed in the Mortgage Administration Agreement that it will not administer the
Mortgage Loans, or set the interest rates on them, in a way that discriminates against the Issuer by reason of
the sale of the Mortgage Loans to it. The risks arising from these conflicts are described in Section 1.
