# 14. Cash management and the issuer accounts

## The Cash Management Agreement

Under a cash management agreement dated the Closing Date between Larkmead Bank plc (the "Cash
Manager"), the Issuer, the Servicer and the Trustee (the "Cash Management Agreement"), the Cash Manager will operate the
Issuer Accounts, maintain the Ledgers, calculate on each Determination Date the Available Funds and the amounts
payable on the following Payment Date, and instruct the payments to be made in accordance with the Priority of
Payments set out in section 15. The Cash Manager acts as agent of the Issuer and, after the delivery of an
enforcement notice, of the Trustee. It performs its services on the basis of the Servicer Report and the other
information delivered to it, and is not responsible for verifying that information, except that it must notify
the Issuer and the Trustee if the information is inconsistent on its face.

Larkmead Bank plc also acts as account bank of the Issuer (in this capacity, the "Account Bank") under a
bank account agreement dated the Closing Date between the Issuer, the Account Bank, the Cash Manager and
the Trustee (the "Bank Account Agreement"), and as agent bank and principal paying agent under the agency
agreement described in section 5. The fees of the Cash Manager, the Account Bank, the agent bank and the
principal paying agent, and of the Back-up Servicer Facilitator, are paid by Kestrel Motor Finance Ltd ("Kestrel")
out of the servicer fee and are not separate items of the Priority of Payments described in section 15.

The Cash Manager may resign on not less than three months' notice, and the Issuer (with the consent of the
Trustee) may terminate its appointment if it fails to perform its obligations or becomes subject to an
Insolvency Event. No resignation or termination takes effect until a successor cash manager has been
appointed and the Rating Agencies have been notified.

## The Collection Account and the declaration of trust

Borrowers pay their instalments and other amounts into the Collection Account described in section 13, which is
held by Kestrel with Larkmead Bank plc (the "Collection Account Bank") and also receives payments under Finance
Agreements which do not form part of the Portfolio. Under a declaration of trust made by Kestrel on or before the
Closing Date (the "Collection Account Declaration of Trust"), Kestrel holds all amounts standing to the credit
of the Collection Account on trust for the Issuer, as to the amounts which derive from the Loans, and for the
other beneficiaries and itself, as to the remainder. The Collection Account Declaration of Trust is intended to
ensure that collections on the Loans held in the Collection Account do not form part of Kestrel's estate on its
insolvency. The Collection Account Bank has acknowledged the trust and has waived any right of set-off or
combination against the Collection Account in respect of amounts owed to it by Kestrel.

The Servicer identifies, using the payment references and its loan management platform, the amounts received
in the Collection Account in respect of each Loan, and records them as collections of the Issuer. Collections
which cannot be identified on the day of receipt are held in a suspense account of the Servicer and allocated
within five Business Days. Amounts which are received in the Collection Account in error, and Direct Debit
indemnity claims which reverse collections already transferred to the Issuer, are reconciled by the Servicer
and deducted from the next transfer to the Issuer, with details given in the Servicer Report.

## Sweeping of collections

On each Business Day the Servicer transfers from the Collection Account to the Issuer Transaction Account all
collections on the Loans identified on the previous Business Day, including instalments, Balloon Payments,
settlement amounts, the proceeds of vehicles sold after repossession, surrender, voluntary termination or
return, insurance proceeds and any other Recoveries. The Seller pays into the Issuer Transaction Account any
Repurchase Price and any indemnity amount under the Sale Agreement, and any amount which is treated as a deemed
collection because a Borrower has exercised a right of set-off against Kestrel or because the amount owed by a
Borrower has been reduced by way of redress, on or before the Business Day before the next Payment Date. The
daily sweep limits to one or two Business Days' collections the amount held by Kestrel for the Issuer at any
time; the commingling risk which remains is described in section 2.

If the Collection Account Bank ceases to have the Account Bank Required Ratings, Kestrel must, within 30
calendar days, open a replacement collection account with a bank which has them, transfer the Collection
Account Declaration of Trust to it and instruct Borrowers paying by Direct Debit, card or standing order to pay
into the replacement account. After a Perfection Event, Borrowers will be instructed to pay directly into an
account of the Issuer (see section 12).

## The Issuer Accounts

The Issuer maintains with the Account Bank the following accounts (together, the "Issuer Accounts"):

(a) a transaction account (the "Issuer Transaction Account"), into which all collections, Repurchase Prices,
deemed collections and other amounts received by the Issuer in respect of the Loans are paid, from which
payments are made on each Payment Date in accordance with the Priority of Payments, and into which the
proceeds of the issue of the Notes were paid on the Closing Date before being applied to the Initial Purchase
Price; and

(b) a reserve account (the "Reserve Account"), in which the reserve fund described in section 16 (the
"Reserve Fund") is held.

The Issuer also maintains an account with a bank in the United Kingdom for its share capital and the profits
retained by it, which does not form part of the Issuer Accounts and from which the Issuer pays its corporation
tax and other corporate costs. Each Issuer Account is subject to the security created by the Issuer in favour of
the Trustee under the deed of charge dated the Closing Date (the "Deed of Charge"), and notice of that security
has been given to the Account Bank, which has acknowledged it and waived any right of set-off,
combination or counterclaim against the Issuer Accounts.

No withdrawal may be made from the Issuer Accounts except on the instructions of the Cash Manager in accordance
with the Cash Management Agreement. Between Payment Dates, the Cash Manager may make withdrawals only to pay
amounts received in error, to make payments due to third parties which the Issuer is obliged to make before the
next Payment Date and which are permitted by the Transaction Documents, and to make Authorised Investments.

## The Reserve Account

On the Closing Date, Kestrel will fund the Reserve Fund by a payment into the Reserve Account of the amount
described in section 16. On each Payment Date the Cash Manager transfers from the Reserve Account to the Issuer
Transaction Account the amount (if any) by which the Available Funds are insufficient to pay in full the senior
fees and the interest on each class of Notes, that is, items 1 to 5 of the Priority of Payments described in
section 15, and, after the application of the Available Funds, credits to the Reserve Account the amount paid
under item 6 to restore the Reserve Fund to its required amount. The Reserve Fund is not used to pay principal
or Deferred Consideration. The required amount of the Reserve Fund, its release on the redemption of the Notes
and the trigger event relating to it are described in section 16.

## Ledgers

The Cash Manager maintains the following ledgers (the "Ledgers") in its records for the Issuer:

(a) the "Interest Collections Ledger", to which it credits all interest collections, including the interest
element of instalments, settlement amounts and Repurchase Prices, fees and charges paid by Borrowers and the
interest and investment income earned on the Issuer Accounts;

(b) the "Principal Collections Ledger", to which it credits all principal received from instalments, Balloon
Payments, prepayments and settlement amounts, and the principal element of Repurchase Prices and indemnity
amounts;

(c) the "Recoveries Ledger", to which it credits all Recoveries received in respect of Defaulted Loans;

(d) the "Reserve Fund Ledger", which records the balance of the Reserve Fund, each drawing made under the
previous paragraph and each amount credited to restore it; and

(e) the "Principal Deficiency Ledger", which records the principal deficiency described in section 15 on each
Payment Date, that is, the amount by which the total balance of the Notes exceeds the Pool Balance after the
application of the Available Funds, and its reduction on later Payment Dates.

Each Ledger is debited with the amounts applied from it on each Payment Date. The Ledgers are maintained for
the purposes of calculation and reporting only; the amounts credited to the Interest Collections Ledger, the
Principal Collections Ledger and the Recoveries Ledger are applied together as Available Funds, and the
classification of a receipt as interest or principal does not affect its application.

## Account bank ratings and replacement

The Account Bank must at all times have the minimum short-term and long-term ratings required by each
Rating Agency for an account bank in a transaction with the ratings of the Notes, as set out in the Bank
Account Agreement (the "Account Bank Required Ratings"). If the Account Bank ceases to have the Account
Bank Required Ratings, it must notify the Issuer, the Cash Manager and the Trustee, and the Issuer (or the Cash
Manager on its behalf) must, within 30 calendar days after the downgrade, either:

(a) transfer the Issuer Accounts, and the balances standing to their credit, to a bank which has the Account
Bank Required Ratings and which accedes to the Bank Account Agreement and the Deed of Charge; or

(b) obtain an unconditional and irrevocable guarantee of the obligations of the Account Bank from a
financial institution which has the Account Bank Required Ratings,

or take any other action which the Rating Agencies confirm will not result in the downgrade of the Notes. The
costs of the replacement are borne by the outgoing Account Bank. The Account Bank may also resign
on not less than 60 days' notice, and its appointment may be terminated by the Issuer, with the consent of the
Trustee, on an Insolvency Event, but no resignation or termination takes effect until a replacement account
bank has been appointed and the balances transferred. A replacement may take time to complete, and the Issuer's
cash may be exposed to the credit risk of a downgraded bank during that period, as described in section 2.

## Investment of funds

The Cash Manager may, on behalf of the Issuer, invest the balances standing to the credit of the Issuer
Transaction Account and the Reserve Account between Payment Dates in authorised investments ("Authorised
Investments"), which are:

(a) sterling demand or time deposits with a bank which has the Account Bank Required Ratings; and

(b) sterling money market funds which have the highest rating of each Rating Agency which rates them,

in each case which mature, or can be redeemed without penalty, on or before the Business Day before the next
Determination Date, which are not securitisation positions and which are held in the name of the Issuer subject
to the security of the Deed of Charge. The Cash Manager will not invest in any derivative instrument and will not
make an investment which would expose the Issuer to a currency or a market risk. The income from Authorised
Investments is credited to the Interest Collections Ledger. Any loss on an Authorised Investment is borne by the
Issuer, and the Cash Manager is not liable for it unless it results from its negligence, wilful default or
fraud. The Cash Manager expects to hold the balances of the Issuer Accounts as deposits with the Issuer Account
Bank.

## Calculation and determination of Available Funds

On the fourth Business Day before each Payment Date (each a "Determination Date") the Cash Manager will calculate,
on the basis of the Servicer Report for the Collection Period ending before that Payment Date:

(a) the Available Funds for the Collection Period, being the interest collections, principal received from
instalments (including Balloon Payments), prepayments and Recoveries received during the Collection Period, as
defined in section 11, together with any deemed collections, Repurchase Prices and indemnity amounts received in
respect of that Collection Period and the income credited to the Interest Collections Ledger;

(b) the Pool Balance at the end of the Collection Period, the defaulted balances of the Loans which became
Defaulted Loans during it, the cumulative default ratio and the 90+ day arrears ratio, each as defined in
section 11;

(c) the amount of each item payable under the Priority of Payments described in section 15, including the senior
fees, the interest payable on each class of Notes for the interest period ending on the Payment Date at the rate
determined by the agent bank in accordance with the Conditions in section 5, the amount required to restore the
Reserve Fund and the principal payable on the Notes;

(d) any drawing on the Reserve Fund required under section 16;

(e) whether a trigger event described in section 16 has occurred, and consequently whether principal is to be
paid pro rata or sequentially as described in section 4; and

(f) the balance of the Principal Deficiency Ledger after the application of the Available Funds, and the
Deferred Consideration payable to Kestrel.

The Cash Manager notifies the results to the Issuer, the Trustee, the Servicer and the principal paying agent
no later than the Business Day after the Determination Date, and on the Payment Date instructs the Issuer Account
Bank to make the payments from the Issuer Transaction Account and the Reserve Account. The amounts of interest
and principal to be paid on each class of Notes are notified to the Noteholders in accordance with the
Conditions and are shown in the investor report described in section 16. Each determination of the Cash Manager
is, in the absence of manifest error, final and binding on the Issuer, the Trustee and the Noteholders.

If the Servicer Report for a Collection Period has not been received by the Determination Date, the Cash
Manager will estimate the Available Funds by reference to the amounts received in the Issuer Transaction Account
during the Collection Period and, for the allocation between interest and principal, the average of the three
most recent Servicer Reports. Where the Cash Manager makes such an estimate, it will make no payment of principal
beyond the amount which it is reasonably satisfied is available and will pay no Deferred Consideration on that
Payment Date. Any difference between the estimated and the actual amounts is reconciled on the next Payment Date
for which a Servicer Report is received, by adjusting the Available Funds for that Payment Date. The failure of
the Servicer to deliver a Servicer Report may be a Servicer Termination Event (see section 13).
