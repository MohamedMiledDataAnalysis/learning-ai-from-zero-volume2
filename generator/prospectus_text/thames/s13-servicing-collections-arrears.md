# 13. Servicing, collections and arrears

## Appointment of the Servicer

Under the Servicing Agreement, the Issuer and, after the delivery of an enforcement notice, the Trustee appoint
Kestrel Motor Finance Ltd ("Kestrel", in this capacity the "Servicer") to administer the Loans and the Ancillary
Rights, to collect all amounts payable in respect of them, to manage arrears, defaults and recoveries and to
report to the Issuer, the Cash Manager and the Trustee. The Servicer acts as agent of the Issuer and holds the
Loan Files, and any collections which it receives, for the Issuer. In performing its services the Servicer must
apply the Credit and Collection Policies described in section 9 and this section, with the degree of skill and
care which it applies to Finance Agreements which it services for its own account and which a reasonable and
prudent lender of motor finance in the United Kingdom would apply, and must comply with the CCA, FSMA, CONC,
the Consumer Duty and data protection law. The Servicer may subcontract specific services, such as the
collection of vehicles, the auction of vehicles, tracing and the printing and dispatch of statements, to third
parties, but remains liable for their performance.

The Servicer may amend the Credit and Collection Policies only where the amendment is required by law or by the
FCA, or where it would be made by a reasonable and prudent lender and it applies the amendment equally to
Finance Agreements which it services for its own account; it must give prior notice to the Issuer, the Trustee
and the Rating Agencies of any amendment which would be likely to have a material adverse effect on the Loans.
The Servicer may not vary the terms of a Loan except as permitted by the Servicing Agreement. Permitted
variations are a change of payment date or payment method, a forbearance arrangement agreed under the
procedures described below, a settlement or a voluntary termination exercised under the CCA, and any variation
required by law, by the FCA, by the Financial Ombudsman Service or by the redress scheme described in section 8.
No variation may reduce the interest rate of a Loan (other than as required by law or by such redress) or extend
its final scheduled payment beyond the date falling two years before the Final Maturity Date. A variation which
is not permitted obliges the Seller to repurchase the Loan (see section 12).

**Servicer fee.** The servicer fee is [[servicer_fee]] per year of the pool balance, paid to the Servicer
monthly as a senior fee in the Priority of Payments (section 15). The Servicer bears from that fee all its costs of servicing, other than the costs of
repossession, transport, storage and sale of vehicles and legal costs of enforcement, which are deducted from
the proceeds of the relevant Loan before those proceeds are paid to the Issuer.

## Payment methods and Direct Debits

Each Borrower is required by the Finance Agreement to pay instalments monthly, and nearly all Borrowers pay by
direct debit under the Bacs Direct Debit scheme ("Direct Debit") on a collection date chosen by the Borrower at
origination. Borrowers may also pay by debit card, by standing order or by bank transfer, and may make
additional payments or settle the agreement early by any of these methods. The Servicer submits the Direct
Debit files on the working day before each collection date, and the funds are credited to the Collection Account
on the collection date. Under the Direct Debit Guarantee, a Borrower may require its bank to refund a payment
which was taken in error; the bank then reclaims the amount from the Servicer through an indemnity claim. Amounts
so reclaimed after they have been transferred to the Issuer are netted against later collections, as described
in section 14. Where a Direct Debit is returned unpaid, the Servicer re-presents it once, on a date agreed with
the Borrower where possible, and the account enters the arrears process described below.

A Borrower may settle a Finance Agreement early at any time under section 94 of the CCA by paying the settlement
amount calculated in accordance with the Consumer Credit (Early Settlement) Regulations 2004, which includes a
statutory rebate of interest. A Borrower may also make partial early settlements, which the Servicer applies
to reduce the term or the instalment as the Borrower chooses. Settlement amounts are principal received in
respect of the outstanding principal of the Loan and interest collections in respect of the remainder, and
are part of the Available Funds defined in section 11.

## Collection accounts

All payments by Borrowers under the Loans are paid into an account of the Servicer held with Larkmead Bank plc
(the "Collection Account Bank"), into which payments under other Finance Agreements serviced by Kestrel are also
paid (the "Collection Account"). Kestrel has declared a trust over the Collection Account in favour of the
Issuer, the beneficiaries under the other financing arrangements of Kestrel and Kestrel itself, according to
their respective interests, and the Servicer identifies the collections on the Loans each day. Collections on
the Loans are transferred to the Issuer Transaction Account on the Business Day after the day on which they are
identified. The declaration of trust, the daily sweep and the accounts of the Issuer are described in section
14.

## Arrears management

An account is in arrears when a contractual instalment has not been paid in full on its due date. The Servicer's
arrears management is organised in stages which reflect the time since the first missed instalment, the
amount in arrears and the circumstances of the Borrower. Throughout, the Servicer's objective, consistent with
the Consumer Duty and CONC 7, is to understand the reason for the arrears, to agree an affordable and
sustainable way for the Borrower to repay, and to avoid foreseeable harm to the Borrower, including the harm of
unaffordable arrangements or of an accumulation of charges. The Servicer charges a single fee for a returned
Direct Debit and does not charge fees for collection letters or calls; any fee charged in arrears reflects the
Servicer's reasonable costs as required by CONC 7.7.

*Early arrears.* When an instalment is missed, the Servicer sends a text message, an email or both on the
following day, re-presents the Direct Debit and writes to the Borrower. The early arrears team contacts the
Borrower by telephone and through Kestrel's digital channels, including an online portal in which the Borrower
may make a payment, propose an arrangement or complete an income and expenditure assessment. The Servicer gives
the Borrower information about free and impartial debt advice, including the Money and Pensions Service's debt
advice services, in every arrears letter.

*Statutory notices.* Where a Borrower has missed two instalments, the Servicer sends a notice of sums in arrears
in the form required by section 86B of the CCA within the period required by that section, accompanied by the
arrears information sheet published by the FCA, and repeats the notice at the intervals required while the
arrears continue. The Servicer also sends the annual statements required by section 77A of the CCA and any
notice of default sums required by section 86C.

*Late arrears.* Accounts which are not brought up to date or placed on an arrangement move to the late arrears
team, whose staff are trained to assess income and expenditure using a standard financial statement and to
identify Borrowers who are vulnerable. The team may offer the forbearance options described below, may refer the
Borrower to a debt advice charity with the Borrower's consent, and, where an arrangement cannot be agreed,
explains to the Borrower the options of voluntary termination and voluntary surrender of the vehicle.

*Default notice and termination.* Where the arrears remain unresolved and no arrangement is in place, the
Servicer may serve a default notice under section 87 of the CCA, in the form required by the Consumer Credit
(Enforcement, Default and Termination Notices) Regulations 1983, giving the Borrower not less than 14 days after
service to remedy the breach. If the breach is not remedied, the Servicer may terminate the Finance Agreement
and demand the balance due, subject to the restrictions described under "Repossession and sale of vehicles".
The decision to serve a default notice and to terminate is taken by an arrears manager after a review of the
account, and is not taken where the Borrower is engaging with the Servicer on an affordable arrangement or
where the Servicer has identified a vulnerability which requires a different approach.

*After a default.* Once a Loan is in default as set out in section 11, all amounts received in respect of it are
Recoveries, whether or not a default notice has been served or the Finance Agreement has been terminated. The
Servicer Report shows, for each such Loan, the balance at the date of default and that date. A Loan stays in
default if the Borrower later clears the arrears.

## Forbearance and the Consumer Duty

The Servicer offers forbearance in accordance with the FCA's rules on customers in arrears or default in CONC 7,
which since 2024 incorporate the tailored support for customers in financial difficulty previously set out in
guidance, and with the Consumer Duty. The forbearance options available include:

(a) an arrangement to pay the arrears over a period in addition to the contractual instalment;

(b) a temporary reduction of the monthly payment to an amount the Borrower can afford, for an agreed period
after which the arrangement is reviewed;

(c) a short deferral of instalments where the Borrower's difficulty is expected to be temporary, for example
pending a return to work;

(d) the suspension, reduction or waiver of interest or charges, where this is appropriate to the Borrower's
circumstances;

(e) an extension of the term of the Finance Agreement, within the limit described under "Appointment of the
Servicer"; and

(f) the acceptance of a token payment or a period of no payment where the Borrower's circumstances, including a
serious illness or bereavement, make any payment inappropriate.

The option chosen depends on an assessment of the Borrower's income and expenditure, and the Servicer must not
pressure the Borrower to pay more than the Borrower can sustainably afford. A Loan under a forbearance arrangement
continues to be measured for arrears by reference to its contractual instalments unless its terms have been
varied, and the Servicer Report states the number and balance of Loans subject to each type of arrangement.
Forbearance may reduce or delay the collections available to the Issuer, as described in section 2, but the
Issuer may not direct the Servicer to withhold forbearance which the law or the FCA requires.

## Repossession and sale of vehicles

The vehicle financed under each HP Agreement or PCP Agreement remains the property of the Seller (held on the
Vehicle Trust for the Issuer, see section 12) until the Borrower exercises the option to purchase. After
termination of a Finance Agreement the Servicer may recover the vehicle, subject to the restrictions of the CCA.
Where the Borrower has paid at least one-third of the total price of the vehicle, the vehicle is protected goods
under section 90 of the CCA and may be recovered only with the Borrower's consent or under a court order; the
Servicer may not in any case enter premises to recover a vehicle without a court order under section 92 of the
CCA. In practice, most vehicles are recovered through voluntary surrender by the Borrower or under a return of
goods order or a time order made by the county court (or the sheriff court in Scotland), which may allow the
Borrower further time to pay. Where a vehicle cannot be located, the Servicer uses tracing agents and the
industry vehicle finance register, and makes a claim against any insurer or dealer liable for the loss.

Recovered and returned vehicles are collected by the Servicer's appointed collection agents, inspected and
graded, and sold through national vehicle auction houses or online trade auctions, with reserve prices set by the
Servicer's remarketing team by reference to current trade valuations, the condition of the vehicle and recent
sale prices. The Servicer may sell a vehicle to a dealer where that is expected to achieve a better price than
auction. The net sale proceeds, after the costs of collection, storage, preparation and sale, are applied to the
amount owed by the Borrower, and the Borrower is notified of the sale price and any remaining balance. Where the
proceeds exceed the amount owed, the surplus is repaid to the Borrower.

## Voluntary terminations and PCP maturities

A Borrower may terminate a Finance Agreement at any time before the final payment falls due by giving notice
under section 99 of the CCA and returning the vehicle. On a voluntary termination, the Borrower's liability is
limited under section 100 of the CCA to any arrears, the amount (if any) needed to bring total payments up to
one-half of the total amount payable under the agreement, and compensation for damage to the vehicle beyond fair
wear and tear. The Servicer sells the returned vehicle in the manner described above. The proceeds of sale,
together with amounts paid by the Borrower, are principal received and interest collections of the Issuer; a
Loan which has been voluntarily terminated ceases to form part of the Pool Balance when the vehicle has been sold
and the termination has been settled, and any shortfall between the outstanding principal and those amounts is a
loss borne by the Issuer (a "Voluntary Termination Loss"). The Servicer informs Borrowers of their right to
voluntary termination where required by CONC 7 and does not discourage its exercise.

At the end of a PCP Agreement, the Servicer writes to the Borrower in advance of the Balloon Payment date to
explain the options of paying the Balloon Payment, returning the vehicle or part-exchanging it. Where the
vehicle is returned, it is inspected against the mileage and condition terms of the agreement, any excess mileage
and damage charges are invoiced to the Borrower, and the vehicle is sold. Any amount by which the net sale
proceeds and those charges fall short of the Balloon Payment is a loss borne by the Issuer (a "Residual Value
Loss"). The Servicer reports Voluntary Termination Losses and Residual Value Losses separately from Defaulted
Loans and Recoveries. The Servicer may offer to refinance the Balloon Payment under a new agreement; a new
agreement is not part of the Portfolio, and the Issuer receives the Balloon Payment in full from the refinancing.

## Recoveries and write-off

After a Loan has become a Defaulted Loan, the recoveries team pursues the outstanding balance through the sale of
the vehicle, payment arrangements with the Borrower, claims against insurers and dealers and, where appropriate
and proportionate, court proceedings. The Servicer does not sell Defaulted Loans to debt purchasers without the
consent of the Issuer and the Trustee. The Servicer will write off a Defaulted Loan when, in accordance with the
Credit and Collection Policies, it considers that no further amounts are likely to be recovered at a
reasonable cost, and in any case on the death of a Borrower whose estate is insolvent or on the Borrower's
bankruptcy or sequestration once the Servicer has submitted its claim. Amounts received after a write-off remain
Recoveries. The Recoveries which the Servicer expects for the Transaction are described in section 11; actual
Recoveries depend on the condition of the vehicles, used car prices and the timing of sales and court
proceedings, and may be lower or later than expected.

## Servicer reporting

The Servicer will deliver to the Issuer, the Cash Manager and the Trustee, no later than the fifth Business Day
of each month, a report for the Collection Period ending on the last day of the previous month (the "Servicer
Report"). The Servicer Report sets out, among other things, the interest collections, principal received from
instalments and Balloon Payments, prepayments and Recoveries received during the Collection Period; the Pool
Balance at the start and the end of the Collection Period; the number and balance of Loans in each arrears band;
the Loans which became Defaulted Loans and their defaulted balances; Voluntary Termination Losses and Residual
Value Losses; the Loans subject to forbearance arrangements; any Loans repurchased and the Repurchase Prices
received; any deemed collections and Direct Debit indemnity claims; and the data needed by the Cash Manager to
calculate the cumulative default ratio and the 90+ day arrears ratio defined in section 11. With each Servicer
Report the Servicer delivers loan-level data on each Loan in a form which enables the Issuer to meet its
reporting obligations described in section 18. The Cash Manager uses the Servicer Report to prepare the
calculations described in section 14 and the investor report described in section 16.

The Servicer will also provide to the Issuer and the Trustee, within 120 days after the end of each financial
year, a certificate signed by a director confirming that it has complied with the Servicing Agreement, and will
give them access, on reasonable notice, to the Loan Files and its systems for the purpose of audits. The
Servicer must notify the Issuer and the Trustee promptly of any Servicer Termination Event or of any event which
would, with the giving of notice or the passing of time, become one.

## Servicer Termination Events

Each of the following is a "Servicer Termination Event":

(a) the Servicer fails to pay into the Issuer Transaction Account any amount due from it within three Business
Days after its due date, unless the failure results from a technical error which is remedied within a further
two Business Days;

(b) the Servicer fails to deliver a Servicer Report within five Business Days after its due date;

(c) the Servicer fails to perform any other obligation under the Servicing Agreement, and the failure has a
material adverse effect on the interests of the Noteholders and, if capable of remedy, is not remedied within
30 days after notice from the Issuer or the Trustee;

(d) an Insolvency Event occurs in relation to the Servicer;

(e) the Servicer ceases to hold any authorisation under FSMA required to service the Loans, or the FCA imposes a
requirement or restriction which prevents the Servicer from performing its services; or

(f) the Servicer ceases to carry on all or substantially all of its business of servicing motor finance
agreements.

On a Servicer Termination Event, the Issuer (with the consent of the Trustee) or the Trustee may terminate the
appointment of the Servicer by notice. The Servicer may also resign on not less than six months' notice. No
termination or resignation takes effect until a successor servicer, which holds the authorisations required
under FSMA and has experience of servicing UK motor finance agreements, has been appointed on terms
substantially similar to the Servicing Agreement and the Rating Agencies have been notified. On termination, the
Servicer must transfer the Loan Files, the data on its systems relating to the Loans and the collections it holds
to the successor servicer and must co-operate in the transfer of the servicing, including the redirection of
Direct Debits under the Bacs scheme rules.

## The Back-up Servicer Facilitator

Pellam Corporate Services Ltd (the "Back-up Servicer Facilitator") has agreed in the Servicing Agreement that,
if the appointment of the Servicer is terminated, it will use reasonable endeavours to identify, on behalf of
the Issuer, a suitably authorised and experienced successor servicer and to assist the Issuer in negotiating the
terms of its appointment, within 60 days after the termination notice. The Back-up Servicer Facilitator does not
itself act as servicer and has no obligation to service the Loans, to fund any amount or to guarantee that a
successor servicer will be found. To assist a transfer, the Servicer must maintain an up-to-date description of
its servicing procedures and systems, deliver to the Back-up Servicer Facilitator each month a copy of the
loan-level data and give it access to its systems once a year for a review of the data. The fee of a successor
servicer may be higher than the fee of the Servicer and would rank among the senior fees in the Priority of
Payments described in section 15. A change of servicer may lead to a temporary disruption of collections and
a rise in arrears, as described in section 2.
