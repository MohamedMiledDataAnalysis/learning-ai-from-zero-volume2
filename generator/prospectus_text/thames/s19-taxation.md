# 19. Taxation

## General

The following is a general summary of the Issuer's understanding of current United Kingdom tax law as applied
in England and Wales and of the published practice of HM Revenue & Customs ("HMRC"), which may not be binding
on HMRC, relating only to the United Kingdom withholding tax treatment of payments of interest on the Notes,
to United Kingdom stamp duty and stamp duty reserve tax in relation to the Notes, and to the United Kingdom
corporation tax treatment of the Issuer. It does not deal with any other United Kingdom tax aspect of
acquiring, holding or disposing of the Notes, it is not exhaustive, and it is not tax advice. It relates only to
persons who are the absolute beneficial owners of their Notes and of the interest on them, and it may not apply
to certain classes of person, such as dealers in securities, persons connected with the Issuer, persons who
hold the Notes as part of a tax avoidance arrangement or in connection with a trade carried on through a
permanent establishment or branch in the United Kingdom, and persons to whom special rules apply. The law and
practice described below may change, possibly with retrospective effect.

Prospective Noteholders who are in any doubt as to their tax position, or who may be subject to tax in a
jurisdiction other than the United Kingdom, should consult their own professional advisers. The tax treatment
of a Noteholder depends on its individual circumstances, and the tax legislation of the Noteholder's
jurisdiction of residence and of the Issuer's jurisdiction of incorporation may have an effect on the income
received from the Notes.

## United Kingdom withholding tax on interest

### The general rule

The Issuer is incorporated and resident for tax purposes in the United Kingdom, and interest on the Notes will
have a United Kingdom source. Because the Notes may remain outstanding for more than one year, interest on the
Notes is expected to be "yearly interest" for United Kingdom tax purposes. A company that pays yearly interest
with a United Kingdom source is in general obliged to deduct United Kingdom income tax at the basic rate
(currently 20%) from the payment, unless an exemption or relief applies or HMRC has given a direction to the
contrary under an applicable double taxation treaty.

The exemption for "quoted Eurobonds" in section 882 of the Income Tax Act 2007 (the "ITA 2007") applies only
to securities that are, and continue to be, listed on a recognised stock exchange or admitted to trading on a
multilateral trading facility operated by a regulated recognised stock exchange. The Notes are not listed and
will not be admitted to trading on any trading venue, so that exemption is not available. The Issuer will
instead rely, where their conditions are met, on the exemptions described below, as provided in Condition 8(c)
(*United Kingdom withholding*).

### The qualifying private placement exemption

Section 888A of the ITA 2007 and the Income Tax (Qualifying Private Placement) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/2002)
(together, the "QPP Rules") provide that the duty to deduct income tax does not apply to a payment of interest
on a security that is a "qualifying private placement". In summary, and without describing every condition,
the Issuer may pay interest on a Note without deduction under the QPP Rules where:

(a) the Note is a security that represents a loan relationship to which the Issuer, a company, is party as
debtor;

(b) the Note is not listed on a recognised stock exchange;

(c) the term of the Note does not exceed 50 years, which is satisfied because the final maturity date of the
Notes is [[final_maturity_date]];

(d) the Notes are issued under an arrangement under which the aggregate value of the securities issued is not
less than £10 million;

(e) the person beneficially entitled to the interest is resident in a "qualifying territory", that is, a
territory with which the United Kingdom has a double taxation treaty that contains a non-discrimination
provision, and is not connected with the Issuer;

(f) the person beneficially entitled to the interest holds the Note for genuine commercial reasons and not as
part of a tax advantage scheme, and the Issuer has entered into the arrangements for genuine commercial
reasons and not as part of a tax advantage scheme; and

(g) the Issuer has received from that person a certificate (a "Creditor Certificate") confirming, among other
things, that it is beneficially entitled to the interest for genuine commercial reasons, that it is resident
in a qualifying territory and that it is not connected with the Issuer, and the Issuer has no reason to
believe that any statement in the Creditor Certificate is incorrect.

Because the Notes are held in global registered form through the clearing systems, the Issuer can apply the
exemption only in respect of the interest to which a Noteholder is beneficially entitled and for which a valid
Creditor Certificate has been delivered, through the procedures established by the Principal Paying Agent and
the clearing systems, before the relevant payment date. A Noteholder that is not resident in a qualifying
territory, or that does not deliver a Creditor Certificate, cannot benefit from the QPP Rules and must rely on
another exemption or relief.

### Other exemptions and reliefs

The Issuer may also pay interest on the Notes without deduction where it reasonably believes, at the time of the
payment, that the person beneficially entitled to the interest is a company resident in the United Kingdom, or
a non-resident company that carries on a trade in the United Kingdom through a permanent establishment and must
bring the interest into account in computing its chargeable profits, or another person listed in sections 933
to 937 of the ITA 2007, such as certain partnerships of such companies and certain exempt bodies. If that belief
proves to be incorrect, the Issuer may be liable to account for the tax that it should have deducted, together
with interest and penalties, unless it took reasonable care.

A Noteholder that is resident for tax purposes in a jurisdiction which has a double taxation treaty with the
United Kingdom may be entitled to receive interest without deduction, or subject to deduction at a reduced rate,
if HMRC directs the Issuer accordingly following a claim by that Noteholder in the prescribed form, or may
recover from HMRC some or all of the tax deducted. A claim under a treaty takes time to process, and a direction
does not have retrospective effect for payments already made.

### Consequences of a deduction

If interest on the Notes is paid after the deduction of United Kingdom income tax, the Issuer will not pay any
additional amount to the Noteholders in respect of the amount deducted (Condition 8(b) (*No gross-up*)). A
change in United Kingdom tax law which requires the Issuer to make a deduction from payments on the Notes may
entitle the Issuer to redeem the Notes under Condition 6(e) (*Optional redemption for tax reasons*). Payments of
principal on the Notes, and payments in respect of the Notes which are not interest, may be made without any
deduction for or on account of United Kingdom income tax.

### Provision of information

HMRC has powers to obtain information and documents relating to securities, including information about
payments of interest on the Notes and about the persons for whom, or on whose behalf, those payments are made or
received. Any paying agent or other person in the United Kingdom that pays interest to, or receives interest for
the benefit of, a Noteholder may be required to provide that information to HMRC, and HMRC may exchange it with
the tax authorities of other jurisdictions under international agreements, including the arrangements known as
the Common Reporting Standard implemented in the United Kingdom by the International Tax Compliance
Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/878). Condition 8(d) (*Tax information*) requires each Noteholder to provide the
information reasonably requested for these purposes.

## United Kingdom stamp duty and stamp duty reserve tax

No United Kingdom stamp duty or stamp duty reserve tax ("SDRT") should be payable on the issue of the Notes, on
their delivery into the clearing systems, or on a transfer of the Notes or of an interest in them, whether by
book-entry transfer within the clearing systems or by an instrument of transfer of a Note in definitive form.
That is because the Notes are expected to constitute "loan capital" for the purposes of section 78 of the
Finance Act 1986 and to fall within the exemption in section 79(4) of that Act: they do not carry a right of
conversion into shares or other securities, or to the acquisition of shares or other securities; they do not
carry a right to interest the amount of which exceeds a reasonable commercial return on their nominal amount
or which is determined by reference to the results of a business or the value of any property; and they do not
carry a right on repayment to an amount which exceeds their nominal amount and is not reasonably comparable
with what is generally repayable in respect of similar loan capital listed on the Official List of the FCA.

No United Kingdom stamp duty should be payable on the sale of the Loans by the Seller to the Issuer, on the
assignment of the related rights to the Issuer or on the grant of security by the Issuer to the Trustee,
because stamp duty is now chargeable only on instruments transferring stock or marketable securities, and the
Loans and the related rights are neither. No stamp duty land tax, land and buildings transaction tax or land
transaction tax arises, because no interest in land is transferred.

## Taxation of the Issuer

### The securitisation company regime

The Issuer expects to be a "note-issuing company" within the meaning of the Taxation of Securitisation
Companies Regulations 2006 (SI 2006/3296) (the "Securitisation Company Regulations"), and therefore a
"securitisation company" for the purposes of those regulations. A securitisation company is taxed on the basis
of its "retained profit", that is, broadly, the amount of profit which the Transaction Documents provide that
it is to retain for its own benefit in each accounting period, and not on the profits shown in its accounts.
The amounts which the Issuer receives and pays under the Transaction Documents, including timing differences
between its accounting profits and its cash flows and any impairment of the Loans, are disregarded in computing
its taxable profits, except to the extent of its retained profit. The Transaction Documents provide for the
Issuer to retain a small amount of profit in each accounting period, and the Issuer expects that its liability
to United Kingdom corporation tax will be limited to tax on that retained profit at the prevailing rate.

The application of the Securitisation Company Regulations depends on the Issuer continuing to satisfy their
conditions, which include conditions as to the nature of its business and its assets, the issue of capital
market investments as part of a capital market arrangement, the payments which it makes and receives, and the
absence of any arrangement of which the main purpose, or one of the main purposes, is to secure a tax advantage
for any person. The Issuer has covenanted in Condition 4 (*Issuer covenants*) not to take any action that would
cause it to cease to be a securitisation company. If the Issuer ceased to be a securitisation company, it could
be taxed on its accounting profits, which in a given accounting period could exceed its cash income and its
retained profit, and the tax payable would reduce the amounts available to pay the Noteholders. Such an event
may entitle the Issuer to redeem the Notes under Condition 6(e).

### Holdings

Holdings is not expected to be a securitisation company. It will be subject to United Kingdom corporation tax on
its income and gains under the general rules. Holdings is not expected to have any significant income, and any
tax payable by it will not be an obligation of the Issuer.

### Value added tax

Supplies of services to the Issuer under the Transaction Documents may be subject to United Kingdom value added
tax ("VAT") at the standard rate, depending on their nature. The Issuer is not expected to be able to recover
input VAT, because its supplies are exempt or outside the scope of VAT. Any irrecoverable VAT on the fees of the
Servicer, the Cash Manager, the Trustee or any other service provider will be an expense of the Issuer and will
reduce the funds available to pay the Noteholders. A change in the VAT treatment of the servicing of the Loans
could increase that expense.

## Other tax considerations

### Noteholders resident in the United Kingdom

A Noteholder within the charge to United Kingdom corporation tax will generally be taxed on the return on the
Notes, including interest, profits on disposal and exchange gains or losses, as income in accordance with the
loan relationship rules in Part 5 of the Corporation Tax Act 2009, broadly by reference to the amounts
recognised in its accounts under generally accepted accounting practice. The Notes are offered only to
professional investors, and this summary does not describe the position of individual Noteholders.

### Noteholders not resident in the United Kingdom

Interest on the Notes has a United Kingdom source and may therefore be chargeable to United Kingdom tax by direct
assessment even where it is paid without deduction. However, a Noteholder that is not resident in the United
Kingdom and does not hold the Notes in connection with a trade carried on in the United Kingdom through a
permanent establishment, branch or agency will generally have no liability to United Kingdom tax on the interest
beyond any tax deducted at source.

### The proposed financial transaction tax

The European Commission's proposal for a financial transaction tax to be adopted by certain participating member
states of the European Union under the enhanced co-operation procedure has not been adopted. The United Kingdom
is not a participating state. Prospective Noteholders should seek their own advice on any financial transaction
tax that may apply to them in any other jurisdiction.

and none of the Issuer, the Originator, the Arranger, the Lead Manager and the Trustee accepts responsibility for
the tax consequences for any Noteholder.
