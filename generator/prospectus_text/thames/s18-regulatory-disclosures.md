# 18. Regulatory disclosures

## The UK securitisation framework

The securitisation of the Loans by the Issuer is a "securitisation" within the meaning of the Securitisation
Regulations 2024 (SI 2024/102) (the "Securitisation Regulations"), made by HM Treasury under the Financial
Services and Markets Act 2000 ("FSMA 2000"). Since 1 November 2024 the Securitisation Regulations, together
with the rules made under them by the FCA in the securitisation sourcebook of the FCA Handbook ("SECN") and by
the PRA in the Securitisation Part of the PRA Rulebook (the "PRA Securitisation Rules"), have replaced the
version of Regulation (EU) 2017/2402 that formed part of domestic law by virtue of the EUWA. In this
Prospectus the Securitisation Regulations, SECN and the PRA Securitisation Rules, together with any guidance,
templates and statements of policy published by the FCA or the PRA under them, are referred to as the "UK
Securitisation Framework". The FCA rules apply to originators, original lenders, sponsors and securitisation
special purpose entities ("SSPEs") that are not PRA-authorised persons, and to institutional investors that
are FCA-regulated; the PRA rules apply to PRA-authorised persons acting in those capacities. The Originator is
authorised by the FCA and is not a PRA-authorised person, and the Issuer is an SSPE; their obligations as
originator and SSPE are therefore set out in SECN.

The UK Securitisation Framework imposes obligations on three groups of persons: on the originator, the sponsor,
the original lender and the SSPE (together, the "Sell-Side Parties"), principally in relation to risk
retention, transparency and credit-granting; on institutional investors, in relation to due diligence before
and while they hold a securitisation position; and on any person who wishes a securitisation to be treated as
a simple, transparent and standardised securitisation. This section describes how the Transaction is intended
to address each of these obligations. It does not purport to be a complete description of the UK
Securitisation Framework, and it is not legal or regulatory advice. Each prospective investor is responsible
for determining, on the basis of its own analysis and with its own advisers, whether the information in this
Prospectus and the information that will be made available after the Closing Date is sufficient to allow it
to comply with the requirements that apply to it.

The UK Securitisation Framework is subject to review. HM Treasury, the FCA and the PRA have proposed
amendments to it which, among other things, would revise the transparency templates, remove the requirement
for public securitisations to report to a securitisation repository, simplify the due diligence obligations of
institutional investors and add further options to the risk retention rules. Those proposals may be adopted in
a form that differs from the form proposed, and they may take effect on dates that are not yet known. The
Originator and the Issuer will comply with the UK Securitisation Framework as it applies to the Transaction
from time to time, and the Originator may change the form, the content or the channel of the reports described
in this section to the extent that an amendment to the UK Securitisation Framework permits or requires it,
without the consent of the Noteholders. None of the Issuer, the Originator, the Arranger, the Lead Manager and
the Trustee gives any assurance as to the content, the timing or the effect of any amendment.

## Risk retention

Kestrel Motor Finance Ltd (the "Retention Holder") will undertake to the Issuer and the Trustee in the
Subscription Agreement and in the Servicing Agreement (the "Retention Undertaking") that, for so long as any
Note is outstanding, it will retain on an ongoing basis a material net economic interest in the securitisation
of not less than 5% of the nominal value of each class of Notes, in accordance with SECN 5, by holding a
*vertical slice*, as described in section 1 (*Transaction overview*). On the Closing Date the Retention Holder
will subscribe for not less than 5% of the principal amount of each of the Class A Notes, the Class B Notes,
the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes (the "Retained Notes"). Because principal is applied to every class of
Notes in the manner described in section 4 and section 15, and the Retained Notes rank *pari passu* with the
other Notes of the same class, the Retained Notes will continue to represent not less than 5% of the principal
amount outstanding of each class throughout the life of the Transaction.

Under the Retention Undertaking the Retention Holder will also undertake that:

(a) it will not sell, transfer or otherwise surrender all or part of the rights, benefits or obligations
arising from the Retained Notes, and will not subject them to any credit risk mitigation, short position or
other hedge, except to the extent permitted by SECN 5;

(b) it will not change the method by which it retains its net economic interest, except in the circumstances,
and in the manner, permitted by SECN 5;

(c) it will confirm in each Investor Report (as defined below) that it continues to hold the Retained Notes,
and it will notify the Cash Manager and the Trustee promptly if it ceases to do so; and

(d) it will provide to the Issuer, the Trustee and the Noteholders such further information as is reasonably
required to enable an institutional investor to verify compliance with the Retention Undertaking, subject to
any applicable law on confidentiality and data protection.

The Retention Holder is the originator of the Loans for the purposes of the UK Securitisation Framework. It
originated every Loan in its own name under the Finance Agreements described in section 9. It has not been
established, and does not operate, for the sole purpose of securitising exposures: it is an established motor
finance company with a strategy and a capacity to meet payment obligations consistent with a broader business,
and its principal source of revenue is the income from the motor finance agreements that it originates and
holds or funds, not the sale of exposures to securitisations. The Retention Holder has not selected the Loans
to be sold to the Issuer with the aim of rendering losses on the Loans higher than the losses over the same
period on comparable exposures held on its own balance sheet. Each Loan was selected from the Retention
Holder's portfolio of loans that satisfied the eligibility criteria in section 11 on the cut-off date, without
any further selection by reference to credit quality.

None of the Issuer, the Arranger, the Lead Manager and the Trustee makes any representation that the Retention
Undertaking is sufficient to satisfy the requirements that apply to any investor, and none of them will
monitor compliance by the Retention Holder with the Retention Undertaking; the Trustee will rely on the
confirmations in the Investor Reports without further enquiry.

## Transparency and reporting

### Reporting entity

For the purposes of SECN 6, the Originator, the Issuer and the other Sell-Side Parties have designated the
Originator as the entity responsible for fulfilling the transparency requirements of the UK Securitisation
Framework in respect of the Transaction (in that capacity, the "Reporting Entity"). The Reporting Entity has
appointed the Cash Manager under the Cash Management Agreement to prepare, and to make available on its behalf,
the Investor Reports and the loan-level data, and has appointed the Servicer to provide the Cash Manager with
the information on the Loans that it needs for that purpose. The appointment of the Cash Manager does not
relieve the Reporting Entity of its responsibility for compliance with SECN 6.

### Information made available before pricing

Before pricing, the Originator made available to potential investors, on request:

(a) static and dynamic historical data on the default, arrears, voluntary termination, prepayment and
recovery performance of the hire purchase and personal contract purchase agreements originated by the
Originator, covering a period of not less than five years and broken down by product type, as referred to in
section 8;

(b) a liability cash flow model which precisely represents the contractual relationship between the Loans and
the payments flowing between the Originator, the Issuer, the Noteholders and the other parties to the
Transaction, which the Originator will continue to make available to investors on an ongoing basis and to
potential investors on request;

(c) loan-level information on the Loans in the portfolio at the cut-off date, in the form of the underlying
exposures template for automobile loans and leases set out in SECN 11; and

(d) a draft of this Prospectus and the drafts of the principal Transaction Documents, including the
agreement under which the Seller sells the Loans to the Issuer, the Servicing Agreement, the Cash Management
Agreement, the Trust Deed and the Deed of Charge.

The final Transaction Documents and this Prospectus in final form will be made available within the period
after the Closing Date required by SECN 6.

### Ongoing reporting

On each payment date, the Cash Manager will prepare, on behalf of the Reporting Entity and from the information
provided by the Servicer, a report on the Transaction for the preceding monthly period (each, an "Investor
Report"). Each Investor Report will be made available within 15 days after the payment date to which it
relates, as stated in section 16, and will contain the information required by SECN 6, in the form of the
investor report template for non-asset-backed commercial paper securitisations in SECN 11, together with:

(a) the collections, the available funds and their application in the priority of payments in section 15;

(b) the principal amount outstanding of each class of Notes, the interest rate for each class and the amount
of interest and principal paid;

(c) the balance of the reserve fund and the amounts drawn from and credited to it;

(d) the performance of the Loans, including defaults, arrears by band, voluntary terminations, prepayments,
vehicle recoveries and losses;

(e) the result of each trigger event test in section 16 and whether a trigger event has occurred; and

(f) the confirmation of the Retention Holder described above under *Risk retention*.

At the same time, the Cash Manager will make available on behalf of the Reporting Entity the loan-level
information on the Loans as at the end of the relevant monthly period, in the form of the underlying exposures
template for automobile loans and leases in SECN 11. SECN 6 requires this information and the investor report
to be made available at least quarterly; in respect of the Transaction both will be made available monthly.
Each data submission will use the same reporting date as the Investor Report to which it relates, and will
identify the Loans by a unique identifier that does not allow a borrower to be identified directly.

### Inside information and significant events

Without delay, the Reporting Entity will make available any inside information relating to the Transaction
which it is obliged to make public under Regulation (EU) No 596/2014 as it forms part of domestic law by
virtue of the EUWA, to the extent that regulation applies, and any significant event that SECN 6 requires to
be disclosed. A significant event includes a material breach of the obligations in the Transaction Documents,
a change in the structural features of the Transaction that can materially affect its performance, a change
in the risk characteristics of the Transaction or of the Loans, the occurrence of a trigger event described
in section 16, the replacement of the Servicer, the Cash Manager or the Account Bank, and any material
amendment to the Transaction Documents. Each such notification will also be made to the Noteholders in
accordance with the notice provisions of the Conditions.

### Public or private securitisation, and the securitisation repository

The Notes are not, and will not be, admitted to trading on any trading venue, and no prospectus is required to
be published in respect of them under the UK prospectus regime. The Transaction is therefore expected to be a
private securitisation for the purposes of the UK Securitisation Framework, even though the Issuer has
prepared this Prospectus for the information of investors. The requirement in SECN 6 that the information
on a public securitisation be made available by means of a securitisation repository registered with the FCA
therefore does not apply to the Transaction. Instead, the Reporting Entity will make the information described
in this section available to the Noteholders, to the FCA and, on request, to potential investors, by means of
a secure website maintained by the Cash Manager, access to which will be given to each Noteholder and to each
potential investor that confirms its status as a professional investor. The Reporting Entity will also submit
to the FCA the notification required for private securitisations, in the form and within the period required
by the UK Securitisation Framework, and will update it when the UK Securitisation Framework requires.

If, as a result of a change in law or in the regulatory treatment of the Transaction, the Transaction becomes
subject to a requirement to report through a securitisation repository, the Reporting Entity will appoint a
securitisation repository registered with the FCA and the Issuer will bear its costs as an expense of the
Issuer. Conversely, if the requirement for public securitisations to report to a securitisation repository is
removed, as has been proposed, this will not affect the obligation of the Reporting Entity to make the
information available on the website described above. The website and its contents do not form part of this Prospectus.

## Credit-granting criteria

SECN 8 requires originators, sponsors and original lenders to apply to exposures to be securitised the same
sound and well-defined criteria for credit-granting that they apply to non-securitised exposures, to apply
the same clearly established processes for approving and, where relevant, amending, renewing and refinancing
credits, and to have effective systems in place to apply those criteria and processes. Where the underlying
exposures are consumer credit agreements, the originator must also have assessed the creditworthiness of the
borrower on the basis required by the rules that apply to the originator.

The Originator has confirmed to the Issuer that each Loan was originated in the ordinary course of its business
under the underwriting criteria and processes described in section 9, which are the same criteria and
processes that it applies to motor finance agreements which it does not securitise; that it has effective
systems to apply those criteria and processes; and that, before entering into each Loan, it carried out the
creditworthiness and affordability assessment required by CONC 5.2A, as described in section 9. The
Originator has also confirmed that no Loan was marketed and underwritten on the premise that the applicant or,
where applicable, an intermediary was made aware that the information provided by the applicant might not be
verified by the Originator. The Originator will disclose in the Investor Reports any material change to its
underwriting criteria made after the Closing Date which applies to its new lending, to the extent SECN
requires such disclosure; such a change will not affect the Loans, which were originated before the cut-off
date.

The Loans are not, and will not include, any securitisation position, so the Transaction is not a
resecuritisation. No Loan was in arrears on the cut-off date, as required by the eligibility criteria in
section 11, and the Originator has confirmed that, to the best of its knowledge, no Loan was owed on the
cut-off date by a borrower who had been declared insolvent or had entered into a debt restructuring process
with the Originator in the three years before the cut-off date.

## Due diligence requirements for institutional investors

### UK institutional investors

Institutional investors that are subject to the UK Securitisation Framework, including credit institutions
and investment firms, insurance and reinsurance undertakings, UK alternative investment fund managers, UK
UCITS management companies and the trustees and managers of occupational pension schemes, must comply with
the due diligence requirements that apply to them before holding a securitisation position and for so long
as they hold it. Those requirements are set out, for FCA-regulated institutional investors, in SECN 4, for
PRA-authorised persons, in the PRA Securitisation Rules and, for the trustees and managers of occupational
pension schemes, in the Securitisation Regulations and the rules made under them.

Before holding a position in the Notes, such an institutional investor must, among other things, verify that
the Originator grants credit on the basis of sound and well-defined criteria and clearly established
processes; verify that the Retention Holder retains a material net economic interest in accordance with the
UK Securitisation Framework and that the retention is disclosed; verify that the information required by
SECN 6 has been made available, or, where the Transaction were not subject to SECN 6, that sufficient
information has been made available to allow it to carry out its assessment; and carry out a due diligence
assessment which enables it to assess the risks involved, including the risk characteristics of the Notes and
of the Loans and all the structural features of the Transaction that can materially affect the performance of
the Notes. While it holds the Notes it must have written procedures to monitor its position and the
performance of the Loans, perform stress tests appropriate to its position, report to its management body on
the risks, and be able to demonstrate to its regulator that it has a comprehensive and thorough understanding
of the Notes and of the Loans and that it has implemented the required policies and procedures. An institutional investor that has
delegated the management of its investments should consider which person is responsible for compliance under
the rules that apply to it.

A failure by an institutional investor to comply with the requirements that apply to it may result in
additional regulatory capital charges or other measures being imposed on it by its regulator. Each of the
Issuer, the Originator, the Arranger, the Lead Manager and the Trustee makes no representation that the
information in this Prospectus, or the information to be made available after the Closing Date, is sufficient
for any investor to comply with its due diligence requirements, and none of them will have any liability to
an investor that fails to do so.

### EEA institutional investors

Institutional investors established in the EEA are subject to the due diligence requirements of Article 5 of
the EU Securitisation Regulation. Where the originator, sponsor or original lender is established outside the
European Union, as the Originator is, Article 5 requires an EEA institutional investor to verify, among other
things, that the originator retains on an ongoing basis a material net economic interest determined in
accordance with Article 6 of the EU Securitisation Regulation, and that the originator, sponsor or SSPE has
made available the information required by Article 7 of the EU Securitisation Regulation in accordance with
the frequency and modalities provided for in that Article.

The Retention Holder intends that the vertical slice described above will also constitute the retention of a
material net economic interest of not less than 5% of the nominal value of each class of Notes within the
meaning of Article 6 of the EU Securitisation Regulation, as in force on the Closing Date, and it does not
intend to change its method of retention after the Closing Date. However, the Retention Holder has given the
Retention Undertaking only by reference to the UK Securitisation Framework; it has not undertaken to comply
with the EU Securitisation Regulation, and it will not be in breach of the Retention Undertaking if the EU and
UK requirements diverge. The information described in this section will be prepared in the form required by
the UK Securitisation Framework and not in the templates adopted under Article 7 of the EU Securitisation
Regulation, and no party has undertaken to make information available through a securitisation repository
registered in the European Union.

Legislative amendments to the EU Securitisation Regulation, including amendments to Article 5 which would
alter the information that an EEA institutional investor must verify in respect of a securitisation whose
originator is established outside the European Union, have been proposed and are in the course of the EU
legislative process; they had not been adopted on the date of this Prospectus. Each EEA institutional
investor should therefore make its own assessment, with its own advisers, of whether the information made
available under the UK Securitisation Framework is sufficient for it to comply with Article 5 of the EU
Securitisation Regulation as in force from time to time. None of the Issuer, the Originator, the Arranger,
the Lead Manager and the Trustee makes any representation in that respect.

## Simple, transparent and standardised securitisation

The Transaction has not been, and is not intended to be, designated as a simple, transparent and standardised
securitisation within the meaning of the UK Securitisation Framework ("UK STS"). No notification has been or
will be made to the FCA that the Transaction meets the UK STS requirements, and no third party has been
appointed to verify compliance with them. The Transaction is also not a simple, transparent and standardised
securitisation within the meaning of the EU Securitisation Regulation; that designation is not available to a
securitisation whose originator, sponsor and SSPE are established outside the European Union.

As a result, the Notes will not benefit from the preferential regulatory treatment which certain investors may
apply to UK STS securitisation positions, including the treatment described below under *Regulatory capital
and liquidity treatment*.

## Regulatory capital and liquidity treatment

None of the Issuer, the Originator, the Arranger, the Lead Manager and the Trustee makes any representation to
any prospective investor or purchaser of the Notes as to the regulatory capital treatment of the Notes, as to
whether the Notes qualify as high quality liquid assets for the purposes of the liquidity coverage ratio rules
that apply to an investor, or as to the treatment of the Notes under any other prudential regime, on the
Closing Date or at any time in the future.

Credit institutions and investment firms subject to the prudential requirements of the PRA, and their EEA
equivalents subject to Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (as amended), should note that the capital treatment of a
securitisation position depends, among other things, on whether the position is a UK STS (or EU STS) position,
on the approach to the calculation of risk-weighted exposure amounts that the investor is permitted to use,
and on the investor's compliance with the due diligence requirements. Because the Transaction is not
designated as UK STS, the Notes are not expected to qualify as Level 2B assets under the liquidity coverage
ratio rules applicable to UK institutions, which require, among other things, that a securitisation position be
a UK STS position, nor under the equivalent EU rules. Insurance and reinsurance undertakings subject to the
UK Solvency II regime (Solvency UK) should consider the spread risk charge applicable to securitisation positions that are
not STS positions. The prudential rules that apply to securitisation positions
in the United Kingdom continue to change, and investors should consult their own advisers on the consequences
for the Notes.

## United States regulatory matters

### The Volcker Rule

Section 13 of the United States Bank Holding Company Act of 1956, as amended, and its implementing regulations
(together, the "Volcker Rule") generally prohibit "banking entities" (as defined in the Volcker Rule) from
acquiring or retaining an ownership interest in, or sponsoring, a "covered fund", subject to certain
exemptions and exclusions. The Issuer is of the view that it is not, and immediately after the issue of the
Notes and the application of the proceeds will not be, a covered fund. In reaching that view the Issuer
intends to rely on an exemption or exclusion from the definition of "investment company" under the United
States Investment Company Act of 1940, as amended, other than the exemptions in section 3(c)(1) and section
3(c)(7) of that Act, so that the Issuer falls outside the definition of covered fund; other exclusions may also
be available. The Notes are offered only outside the United States to persons that are not U.S. persons in
reliance on Regulation S under the Securities Act, as described in the Important notices and in section 20. No assurance is given that a regulator or a
court would agree with that view, and any prospective investor that is a banking entity should consult its own
advisers.

### United States risk retention

The credit risk retention regulations adopted under Section 15G of the United States Securities Exchange Act
of 1934, as amended (the "U.S. Risk Retention Rules"), generally require the sponsor of a securitisation
transaction to retain not less than 5% of the credit risk of the assets collateralising the issued securities.
The Transaction is not intended to involve the retention by a sponsor of a credit risk interest for the
purposes of the U.S. Risk Retention Rules, and the Originator intends to rely on the exemption for certain
foreign-related transactions in the U.S. Risk Retention Rules. That exemption is available if, among other
conditions, the transaction is not required to be and is not registered under the Securities Act, no more than
10% of the value of all classes of securities issued is sold or transferred to, or for the account or benefit
of, U.S. persons (as defined in the U.S. Risk Retention Rules, "Risk Retention U.S. Persons"), neither the
sponsor nor the issuer is organised under the laws of, or is a branch located in, the United States, and no
more than 25% of the assets were acquired from an affiliate or branch of the sponsor or the issuer organised
or located in the United States. Accordingly, the Notes may not be purchased by Risk Retention U.S. Persons
without the prior written consent of the Originator, and each purchaser of Notes will be deemed to represent
that it is not a Risk Retention U.S. Person. Failure of the Transaction to comply with the U.S. Risk Retention
Rules could adversely affect the market value and the liquidity of the Notes.

## Benchmarks

The rate of interest on each class of Notes is determined by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA, as described
in section 4 and in the Conditions in section 5. SONIA is administered by the Bank of England, which, as a
central bank, falls outside the scope of the UK Benchmarks Regulation, as explained in the Important notices.
The Issuer is not a supervised entity for the purposes of Article 28(2) of the UK Benchmarks Regulation.
The Conditions set out the fallback provisions which apply if SONIA ceases to be provided or if its methodology
changes materially, and the risks associated with the use of SONIA, including the absence of a hedge of the
basis between the fixed rates on the Loans and the floating rate on the Notes, are described in section 2.

The Issuer has appointed the Rating Agencies, each of which is established in the United Kingdom and registered

rating agencies, and to consider appointing at least one credit rating agency with a small market share; the
