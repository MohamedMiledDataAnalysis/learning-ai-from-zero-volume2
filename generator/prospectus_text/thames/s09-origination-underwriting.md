# 9. Origination and underwriting

The information in this section has been provided by Kestrel Motor Finance Ltd ("Kestrel"). It summarises the
origination and underwriting policies and procedures (the "Credit and Collection Policies") which applied to the
Loans in the Portfolio when they were originated and which Kestrel applies to new Finance Agreements at the date
of this Prospectus. The Credit and Collection Policies are reviewed at least annually by the Credit Committee
and may be amended from time to time; the restrictions on changes to the Credit and Collection Policies after
the Closing Date are described in section 13. Each Loan was originated in accordance with the Credit and
Collection Policies in force at the date of its origination, as warranted by Kestrel under the Sale Agreement
(see section 12). The criteria which a Loan had to satisfy to be included in the Portfolio are the Eligibility
Criteria set out in section 11; they are narrower than the lending criteria described in this section, and
not every Finance Agreement which Kestrel originates is eligible for the Transaction.

## Governance of credit risk

Kestrel's credit risk appetite is set by its board on the recommendation of the risk committee. Within that
appetite, the credit committee of Kestrel (the "Credit Committee"), chaired by the Chief Risk Officer and
including the Chief Financial Officer, the Chief Operating Officer and the heads of underwriting, credit
analytics and residual values, approves the scorecards, the policy rules, the cut-off scores, the affordability
model, the maximum advance and loan-to-value limits by product and vehicle age, the residual value curves and
the delegated lending authorities described below. The Credit Committee meets monthly and reviews management
information on application volumes, approval rates, early arrears, defaults, losses on repossessed and returned
vehicles and the performance of each dealer segment. A change to a scorecard, a cut-off score or the
affordability model is validated by the credit analytics team, which is independent of the sales function,
and is monitored after implementation by comparison of actual and expected performance.

Kestrel's commercial teams have no lending authority. Sales and dealer support staff are not remunerated by
reference to the volume or value of business approved, and no element of the remuneration of any underwriter
depends on the number of applications approved.

## Dealer onboarding and monitoring

Kestrel accepts applications only from Introducing Dealers. Before Kestrel enters into a dealer agreement, its
dealer onboarding team carries out due diligence on the prospective dealer, which includes:

(a) confirmation that the dealer is authorised by the FCA for credit broking, or is an appointed representative
of a principal firm so authorised, and that the scope of its permission or appointment covers the introduction
of regulated credit agreements;

(b) verification of the identity of the dealer's legal entity, its directors and its beneficial owners, and
screening of each of them against sanctions, politically exposed persons and adverse media lists, in accordance
with the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017;

(c) a review of the dealer's financial position, using filed accounts and commercial credit reference agency
data, and of any county court judgments, insolvency events or disqualifications affecting the dealer or its
directors;

(d) a site visit or a remote inspection of the dealer's premises, stock and sales processes, and a review of
its website and advertising for compliance with the financial promotion rules in CONC 3;

(e) a review of the dealer's complaints handling procedures and of any complaints about the dealer referred to
the Financial Ombudsman Service; and

(f) confirmation that the dealer's sales staff who will introduce applications have completed Kestrel's
training on its products, the disclosure of commission, the treatment of vulnerable customers and the
prevention of fraud.

Each dealer agreement requires the Introducing Dealer to comply with CONC and with the Consumer Duty, to
disclose to the customer the existence and nature of any commission payable by Kestrel in accordance with the
FCA's rules, to provide the customer with the pre-contract information and explanations required by the CCA and
CONC 4, to verify the identity of the customer and to deliver to the customer a vehicle which conforms to its
description and to the Consumer Rights Act 2015. Commission is paid to Introducing Dealers on a fixed basis by
reference to the amount of credit and the product; Kestrel has not permitted any dealer to vary the interest
rate charged to a customer since the FCA's prohibition of discretionary commission arrangements took effect.
The Introducing Dealer gives warranties as to the vehicle and the information supplied, and indemnifies Kestrel
against losses arising from a breach of the dealer agreement; Kestrel may claw back commission on agreements
that are cancelled, withdrawn or terminated early.

Kestrel monitors each Introducing Dealer through monthly management information on the volume and quality of
its applications, approval and decline rates, cancellations and withdrawals, early payment default (a missed
instalment in the first months of an agreement), complaints, fraud referrals and losses on vehicles. A dealer
whose indicators deviate from those of its peer group is placed on a watch list and reviewed by the dealer risk
team, which may require additional documents on every application from that dealer, suspend its ability to
introduce business or terminate the dealer agreement. Each Introducing Dealer is subject to an annual review of
its FCA status and, for dealers above a volume threshold set by the Credit Committee, a periodic audit of a
sample of files. Kestrel checks the FCA status of all Introducing Dealers against the Financial Services Register
each month.

## The application process

A customer who wishes to finance a vehicle through an Introducing Dealer completes an application, either with
the dealer at its premises or remotely through Kestrel's online application journey. The application captures
the customer's identity, date of birth, address history for the previous three years, marital and residential
status, employment status, employer and length of employment, gross and net income, the number of dependants,
the customer's declared regular expenditure and existing credit commitments, the vehicle (including its
registration or chassis number, mileage, age and the dealer's cash price), the deposit (in cash, by
part-exchange or from a manufacturer or dealer deposit contribution), the requested term and, for a PCP
Agreement, the annual mileage which the customer expects to drive. The customer's consent is obtained before
any search is made with a credit reference agency, and a quotation search is used until the customer proceeds
with a full application.

The dealer submits the application through the dealer portal. The decision engine verifies the vehicle against
an industry vehicle data service, which confirms its identity, its first registration date, whether it has been
recorded as stolen, written off or subject to outstanding finance, and its current trade valuation. The engine
then retrieves the customer's credit file from one or more credit reference agencies and applies the policy
rules, the scorecard, the affordability assessment and the fraud checks described below. It returns one of
three outcomes to the dealer: an acceptance, which may be conditional on proof of income, proof of address or
another document; a referral to an underwriter; or a decline. Where an application is declined, the customer is
told that the decision has been made and, where the decision was based on information from a credit reference
agency, is given the name and contact details of that agency as required by section 157 of the CCA.

When an application has been accepted, the dealer presents the pre-contract credit information in the Standard
European Consumer Credit Information form prescribed under the Consumer Credit (Disclosure of Information)
Regulations 2010 and the adequate explanations required by CONC 4.2, and the customer signs the Finance
Agreement, either on paper or electronically through Kestrel's e-signature process. Kestrel pays the dealer the
amount financed only after it has received the signed Finance Agreement and the supporting documents, confirmed
that any conditions of acceptance have been met and recorded the vehicle as subject to its finance agreement
with the industry vehicle finance register. The customer may withdraw from the Finance Agreement within
fourteen days under section 66A of the CCA, in which case the customer must repay the credit with accrued
interest; no Loan was within that period at the Cut-off Date, because each Loan had the seasoning required by
the Eligibility Criteria in section 11.

## Credit scoring

Kestrel uses application scorecards developed by its credit analytics team on its own historical performance
data, which combine credit reference agency data (including payment history, current indebtedness, recent
credit searches, county court judgments, insolvency records and electoral roll data) with application data and
the characteristics of the vehicle and the proposed agreement. Separate scorecards are used for new and used
vehicles and for applicants with limited credit histories. The scorecard produces a score which is mapped to a
risk grade. Applications are subject to minimum scores and to policy rules set by the Credit Committee, which
decline, among others, applicants under 18 years of age, applicants not resident in the United Kingdom,
applicants who are subject to a current bankruptcy order, sequestration, individual voluntary arrangement,
debt relief order or trust deed, and applicants with unsatisfied county court judgments above a threshold amount
or recent defaults. The credit score recorded in the data tape and used in section 10 is the score of the
credit reference agency at origination and not Kestrel's internal score.

Each scorecard is monitored monthly for its stability and its power to rank applicants by risk. A scorecard
whose performance deteriorates beyond the tolerance set by the Credit Committee is recalibrated or redeveloped.
The interest rate charged on a Finance Agreement depends on the product, the vehicle and the risk grade, within
a rate card approved by the Credit Committee; the Introducing Dealer has no discretion over the rate.

## Creditworthiness and affordability

Kestrel carries out a creditworthiness assessment of every applicant in accordance with CONC 5.2A before it
enters into a Finance Agreement or significantly increases the amount of credit. The assessment considers both
the risk to Kestrel that the customer will not make repayments (credit risk) and the risk to the customer of
not being able to make repayments without undue difficulty, that is, without failing to meet other reasonable
commitments, without having to borrow further to meet the repayments and without a significant adverse impact
on the customer's financial situation (affordability risk). The assessment is proportionate to the amount and
the term of the credit and to the customer's circumstances, and is based on sufficient information obtained
from the customer and from credit reference agencies.

Kestrel verifies income in every case. For most applicants, income is verified through a credit reference agency
service which compares declared income with the turnover recorded on the applicant's current account; where
that check does not confirm income within a tolerance, the applicant is asked for payslips, bank statements or,
for a self-employed applicant, tax calculations or accounts. Committed expenditure, including existing credit
repayments and housing costs, is taken from the credit file and the application; essential living costs are
estimated using statistical data published by the Office for National Statistics, adjusted for household size
and region, and the higher of the declared and the estimated figure is used. The application is declined
unless the customer's verified net income after committed expenditure, essential living costs and a buffer for
unexpected costs is sufficient to meet the proposed instalment. The assessment uses the scheduled instalments
and, for a PCP Agreement, takes into account that the customer will have to pay, refinance or settle the
balloon payment through the return of the vehicle at the end of the term.

Kestrel does not take into account the value of the vehicle as a substitute for the customer's ability to repay.
The customer is asked about any foreseeable changes in income or expenditure during the term of the agreement,
such as retirement or the end of a fixed-term contract, and the assessment is adjusted accordingly.

## Fraud prevention

Each application is checked against the fraud prevention databases to which Kestrel subscribes, the internal
records of previous applications to Kestrel and the credit reference agency identity and address verification
services. The decision engine identifies inconsistencies in the application data, the reuse of contact details
across unrelated applications, mismatches between the applicant's address and the dealer's location and other
indicators of application fraud, impersonation and first-party fraud. Applications which trigger a fraud rule
are referred to Kestrel's fraud team, which may contact the applicant, request original documents or conduct a
video identity check. Confirmed frauds are recorded on the fraud prevention databases, and Introducing Dealers
associated with repeated fraud are reviewed under the dealer monitoring process. Kestrel also verifies, before
paying out, that the vehicle is not recorded as stolen or as subject to existing finance and that the dealer is
entitled to sell it, and checks that the amount financed is consistent with the trade valuation of the vehicle.

## Loan-to-value and the amount of credit

The amount of credit under each Finance Agreement is the cash price of the vehicle, plus any optional products
financed with Kestrel's approval, less the deposit. Kestrel sets maximum loan-to-value ratios by product, by
vehicle age and by risk grade, by reference to the trade valuation of the vehicle at origination supplied by the
vehicle data service; for the purposes of section 10, the original loan-to-value of a Loan is the amount of
credit divided by that valuation. Kestrel requires a minimum deposit, set by the Credit Committee, on all PCP
Agreements and on HP Agreements for higher risk grades. Kestrel finances only cars and light commercial vehicles
under a maximum age at the end of the agreement and a maximum mileage at origination. Agreements for light
commercial vehicles and agreements with an original loan-to-value above 100% are excluded from the Portfolio by
the Eligibility Criteria in section 11. The original term of an HP Agreement or a PCP Agreement
is between 12 and 60 months.

## Personal contract purchase and the setting of residual values

Under a PCP Agreement the customer pays a deposit, a series of monthly instalments and a final optional payment
(the "Balloon Payment"). The Balloon Payment is equal to the guaranteed minimum future value of the vehicle at the
end of the agreement (the "GMFV"), set by Kestrel at origination for the term and the annual mileage agreed with
the customer. At the end of the term the customer may pay the Balloon Payment and acquire title to the vehicle,
or return the vehicle to Kestrel in full satisfaction of the Balloon Payment, provided that the vehicle is
within the agreed mileage and is in a condition consistent with fair wear and tear; if it is not, the customer
pays an excess mileage charge set out in the agreement and the cost of repairing damage. A customer may also
part-exchange the vehicle at a dealer, in which case the Balloon Payment is settled from the sale proceeds. In
addition, a customer under any Finance Agreement may terminate it voluntarily under sections 99 and 100 of the
CCA, as described in section 13.

Kestrel's residual value function sets the GMFV for each make, model, derivative, fuel type, term and mileage
band. The GMFV is derived from the forecast future trade values of an independent vehicle valuation provider,
adjusted by Kestrel for its own historical experience of returned vehicles and for the residual value appetite
set by the Credit Committee. Kestrel applies a prudence margin below the forecast future trade value for every
vehicle; the margin is larger for vehicles with less established resale markets, including battery electric
vehicles, for which separate residual value curves and lower maximum GMFVs have applied since 2022. The residual
value function reviews the forecasts monthly and, where the forecast values for a segment fall, may reduce
the GMFVs offered on new agreements in that segment. The GMFV of an existing PCP Agreement is not changed after
origination.

Kestrel measures its residual value exposure monthly by comparing the Balloon Payment of each live PCP Agreement
with the current forecast trade value of the vehicle at maturity, and reports the results to the Credit
Committee and the risk committee. The Issuer bears the risk that the value of a returned vehicle is lower than the
Balloon Payment it replaces, as described in section 2 and section 13.

## Approval authorities and referrals

Applications which pass the policy rules, the scorecard, the affordability assessment and the fraud checks, and
which are within the automatic approval limits for the risk grade, are approved by the decision engine. All
other applications which are not declined automatically are referred to an underwriter. Underwriters exercise
lending authority delegated by the Credit Committee to named individuals according to their grade, as follows.
The amounts are Kestrel's internal limits on the amount of credit and are not terms of the Notes.

| Authority | Maximum amount of credit | May approve exceptions to policy |
|---|---:|---|
| Underwriter | £30,000 | No |
| Senior underwriter | £50,000 | Minor exceptions only |
| Underwriting manager | £75,000 | Yes, within the limits of the policy |
| Head of underwriting or Chief Risk Officer | Above £75,000 | Yes |

An underwriter who refers an application considers the information on the credit file, the affordability
assessment and any documents supplied, and may approve it, approve it on amended terms (for example with a
higher deposit, a shorter term or a different vehicle), request further information or decline it. The reasons
for each decision are recorded on the loan management platform. An underwriter may not approve an application
introduced by a dealer with which the underwriter has a personal connection, and a sample of decisions by each
underwriter is reviewed each month by the quality assurance team.

## Exceptions to policy

An exception is an approval of an application which does not meet one or more of the policy rules, other than
the rules which give effect to legal or regulatory requirements, which may not be waived. Exceptions may be
approved only by an underwriter with authority to approve them and only where the underwriter records a reason
which mitigates the risk, such as a strong payment history with Kestrel, verified income substantially in excess
of the minimum required, or a larger deposit. Exceptions are reported to the Credit Committee each month, and
the number of exceptions is limited by the Credit Committee to a small proportion of approvals. The performance
of agreements approved as exceptions is monitored separately. No exception may be made to the affordability
assessment.

## Quality assurance and the underwriting audit

Kestrel's quality assurance team, which reports to the Chief Risk Officer, reviews each month a sample of
applications approved automatically and of applications decided by underwriters, and checks that the documents
required by the Credit and Collection Policies were obtained, that the affordability assessment was completed
correctly and that the Finance Agreement was executed in the prescribed form. Findings are reported to the
Credit Committee, and remedial training is given where necessary. The Kestrel Group's internal audit function
reviews the underwriting process at least every two years.

## Documentation and retention of records

Each Finance Agreement is executed in the form required by the Consumer Credit (Agreements) Regulations 2010 and
contains the prescribed terms required for its enforceability under the CCA. Kestrel maintains a standard
form of HP Agreement and of PCP Agreement which is reviewed by its legal and compliance functions whenever the
law or the FCA's rules change; the form of each Finance Agreement is confirmed by Kestrel's warranty in the Sale
Agreement (see section 12). Agreements signed electronically are executed through an e-signature platform which
records the identity checks performed, the time of signature and a tamper-evident copy of the signed document;
the customer is provided with a copy of the executed agreement in a durable medium.

Kestrel holds the original or electronic signed Finance Agreement, the application, the credit file at
origination, the affordability assessment, the identity and income verification documents, the dealer's
invoice and the vehicle data check (together, the "Loan Files") in electronic form in its document management
system, with paper originals held in a secure archive operated by a third party. Under the Servicing Agreement,
Kestrel holds the Loan Files relating to the Loans as agent of the Issuer and must make them available to the
Issuer, the Trustee and any successor servicer on request. Kestrel retains Loan Files for at least six years
after the end of the relevant Finance Agreement, in accordance with its record-keeping obligations under the
FCA Handbook, the limitation periods applicable under the Limitation Act 1980 and its policies under the UK
General Data Protection Regulation and the Data Protection Act 2018, after which they are securely destroyed
unless they relate to a complaint, a claim or a regulatory review which is still open.
