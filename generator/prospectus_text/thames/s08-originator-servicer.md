## Introduction

Kestrel Motor Finance Ltd ("Kestrel" and, in its respective capacities in the Transaction, the "Originator", the
"Seller" and the "Servicer") is a private limited company incorporated in England and Wales on 12 March 2004
under the Companies Act 1985 with registered number 05087341. Kestrel is a wholly owned subsidiary of Kestrel
Group plc (the "Parent" and, together with its subsidiaries, the "Kestrel Group"), a public limited company
incorporated in England and Wales which acts as the holding company of the Kestrel Group and has no operating
business of its own. The shares of the Parent are not admitted to trading on any market; they are held by the
founding shareholders, members of senior management under an employee share plan and a small number of
institutional investors, none of which holds a controlling interest.

Kestrel is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (the "FCA") under the Financial Services
and Markets Act 2000 ("FSMA") for the regulated activities of entering into regulated credit agreements as
lender, exercising or having the right to exercise the lender's rights and duties under regulated credit
agreements, credit broking, debt collecting and debt administration. Kestrel is entered on the Financial
Services Register maintained by the FCA. Kestrel is also registered with the Information Commissioner's Office
as a controller of personal data and is a member of the principal UK trade association for consumer and motor
finance providers, whose lending code it has adopted.

Kestrel's business consists of the provision of point-of-sale finance to individual consumers for the purchase
of new and used cars and light commercial vehicles supplied by franchised and independent motor dealers in
England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. Its two principal products are hire purchase agreements ("HP
Agreements") and personal contract purchase agreements ("PCP Agreements" and, together with HP Agreements, the
"Finance Agreements"), each of which is a fixed-sum credit agreement regulated by the Consumer Credit Act 1974
(the "CCA") under which title to the financed vehicle remains with Kestrel until the borrower has paid all
amounts due and exercised the option to purchase. Kestrel does not offer leasing products, does not lend on an
unsecured basis and does not lend to businesses other than sole traders and small partnerships whose agreements
are regulated by the CCA; agreements with sole traders and partnerships are not included in the Portfolio.

## History and development

Kestrel was founded in 2004 by a group of former managers of the motor finance division of a UK clearing bank,
with the intention of offering independent and medium-sized franchised dealers in the Midlands and the north of
England a responsive alternative to the captive finance companies of the vehicle manufacturers. During its first
three years Kestrel operated from a single office in Leicester and financed its lending through a revolving
credit facility provided by its founding shareholders and a bilateral bank facility.

From 2008 Kestrel extended its dealer network to the south of England and Wales and, in 2011, opened a regional
office in Glasgow to serve dealers in Scotland. In 2013 Kestrel introduced its PCP product, which had until then
been offered principally by captive lenders, and established a residual value function to set and monitor
guaranteed future values (see section 9). In 2016 Kestrel began to finance dealers in Northern Ireland through
a team based in Belfast. In 2018 the Parent was incorporated as the new holding company of the group, and
Kestrel's head office moved to its present location in Leicester, where its underwriting, servicing,
collections and finance functions are located. Kestrel has no operations outside the United Kingdom.

In 2019 Kestrel completed the migration of its loan origination and administration to the systems described
below under "Systems". In 2021 it launched an online application journey through which a consumer who has
selected a vehicle at a dealer may complete the application, receive a decision and sign the Finance Agreement
electronically. Since 2022 Kestrel has offered dedicated finance for battery electric and plug-in hybrid
vehicles, for which it applies separate residual value curves.

## Business volumes

Kestrel's new business is generated exclusively through motor dealers that have entered into a dealer
agreement with Kestrel and that are themselves authorised by the FCA for credit broking or are appointed
representatives of a firm so authorised (each an "Introducing Dealer"). Kestrel does not lend directly to
consumers who approach it without an Introducing Dealer, other than through refinancing of the balloon payment
of an existing PCP Agreement, and agreements arising from such refinancing are not included in the Portfolio.

At 30 September 2026 Kestrel had dealer agreements with approximately 2,300 Introducing Dealers, of which
approximately 40% by number were franchised dealers and the remainder independent dealers. No single dealer
group introduced more than 4% of Kestrel's new business by amount advanced in the year ended 31 December 2025.

At 30 September 2026 Kestrel administered a portfolio of approximately 141,000 live Finance Agreements with an
aggregate outstanding principal balance of approximately £1.62 billion, all of which were originated by Kestrel.
The Kestrel Group's consolidated audited financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025 reported

Kestrel finances its lending through equity, subordinated loans from the Parent, a senior revolving warehouse
facility provided by a UK bank and secured on a portfolio of Finance Agreements held by a special purpose
company within the Kestrel Group, and bilateral term facilities. Certain of the Loans in the Portfolio were,
until the Closing Date, held by that special purpose company and will be released from the security of the
warehouse facility and transferred back to Kestrel immediately before their sale to the Issuer on the Closing
Date. The Transaction is part of Kestrel's strategy of diversifying its funding by accessing the term
securitisation market.

## Systems

Kestrel uses a single loan management platform, licensed from an established vendor of consumer lending
software and hosted in two UK data centres operated by a third party, for the administration of every Finance
Agreement from proposal to settlement. The platform holds the customer and vehicle records, the contractual
payment schedule, the balloon payment and guaranteed future value of each PCP Agreement, the payment history,
arrears status, forbearance arrangements and recovery history, and generates the statutory notices and
statements required by the CCA. Applications are received from Introducing Dealers through a web-based dealer
portal and are routed to an automated decision engine which retrieves credit reference agency data, applies
Kestrel's scorecards and policy rules and returns a decision or refers the application to an underwriter (see
section 9).

Collections are managed through a dialler and workflow system integrated with the loan management platform,
which assigns accounts in arrears to collection strategies and records each contact with the customer.
Payments by Direct Debit are submitted through the Bacs scheme by Kestrel as a service user; card payments are
processed through a payment service provider. Data on the loan management platform are replicated in real time
to the secondary data centre, and a full backup is taken daily and held off-site. Kestrel tests its business
continuity and disaster recovery arrangements at least annually; the most recent test, completed in June 2026,
restored the loan management platform and the payment files at the secondary site within the recovery time
objective set by the board. Kestrel's systems will produce the loan-level data required for the reports
described in section 13 and section 18 in the templates required by the Securitisation Regulations 2024 (SI
2024/102) and the FCA's securitisation rules.

The internal audit function of the Kestrel Group, which reports to the chair of the audit committee, reviews
the underwriting, servicing and collections processes on a risk-based cycle. The external auditors of Kestrel
have not been asked to report on the systems or controls described in this section. An independent firm of
accountants has, at the request of the Arranger, performed agreed-upon procedures on a sample of the Loans in
the Portfolio, comparing the data in the loan management platform with the underlying Finance Agreements and
with the data tape from which section 10 was prepared; no material exceptions were noted that have not been
corrected or the related Loans removed from the Portfolio.

## Previous securitisations

The Transaction is the first public securitisation of Kestrel and the first transaction in which Finance
Agreements originated by Kestrel have been securitised in a transaction rated by any rating agency. Before the
Closing Date Kestrel's securitisation experience consisted of the private warehouse facility described above
under "Business volumes", for the administration of which Kestrel has acted as servicer since 2017 and under
which it has delivered monthly loan-level and portfolio reports to the warehouse lender. Kestrel has
therefore not previously acted as servicer of a rated securitisation, and investors have no history of
investor reports for Kestrel-originated receivables. Historical static and dynamic performance data on
Kestrel's originations, covering a period of at least five years, have been made available to prospective
investors before pricing as described in section 18.

Kestrel has confirmed to the Issuer that it has expertise in originating and servicing exposures of a similar
nature to the Loans, through its management body and senior staff with professional experience in the
origination and servicing of motor finance receivables of more than five years, and that it has
well-documented and adequate policies, procedures and risk management controls relating to the servicing of
such exposures. Kestrel will act as the originator for the purposes of the Securitisation Regulations 2024 (SI
2024/102) and will retain a material net economic interest in the Transaction by way of the vertical slice
described in section 1 and section 18.

## Regulatory matters and the FCA review of motor finance commission

Kestrel is subject to the FCA's Principles for Businesses, including the Consumer Duty set out in Principle 12
and PRIN 2A, to the Consumer Credit sourcebook ("CONC") and to the other parts of the FCA Handbook that apply to
consumer credit firms. Kestrel has a compliance function independent of its business lines and reports to the
FCA through the regular regulatory returns required of consumer credit firms. Kestrel has not been the subject
of any FCA enforcement action or of any requirement imposed under section 55L of FSMA, and is not aware of any
investigation by the FCA into its business other than the matters described in this paragraph and the
following paragraphs.

Like other UK motor finance providers, Kestrel is affected by the FCA's review of historical commission
arrangements between lenders and motor dealers, by the related litigation in the English courts, including the
judgment of the Supreme Court in 2025 on the duties owed by motor dealers to customers and the disclosure of
commission, and by the industry-wide consumer redress scheme which the FCA has consulted on and is
implementing in respect of motor finance agreements entered into during the period covered by the scheme.
Before the FCA's prohibition of discretionary commission arrangements took effect in January 2021, a
proportion of Kestrel's agreements were introduced by dealers who were remunerated under arrangements which
allowed the dealer to adjust the interest rate within a range set by Kestrel. Kestrel has also reviewed the
disclosure of commission made to customers under the fixed commission arrangements which it has used since then.

Kestrel has made provisions in its financial statements for the estimated cost of redress and of the
administration of complaints relating to commission, has paused final responses to such complaints where
permitted by the FCA and is preparing to implement the redress scheme in the form in which it is finally
adopted. The amount of any redress, and the number of agreements affected, cannot yet be determined with
certainty and may differ materially from the amount provided. Some of the Loans in the Portfolio were entered
into during the period covered by the redress scheme. Under the Sale Agreement, Kestrel has agreed that it
remains liable for, and will indemnify the Issuer against, any redress, compensation, interest or costs
payable to a Borrower in connection with commission paid or payable to an Introducing Dealer in respect of a
Loan, and that any reduction of the amount owed by a Borrower which is made by way of redress will be treated
as a deemed collection which Kestrel must pay to the Issuer (see section 12). Investors should nevertheless
consider the risk factor in section 2 relating to the FCA review of motor finance commission, because the
Issuer's protection depends on the continued solvency of Kestrel and because a Borrower may in some
circumstances seek to set off a claim against amounts owed under a Loan.

Kestrel is not, and has not been during the twelve months before the date of this Prospectus, involved in any
governmental, legal or arbitration proceedings, other than the matters described above and complaints and
claims arising in the ordinary course of its business, which may have, or have had in the recent past,
significant effects on its financial position or its ability to perform its obligations under the
Transaction Documents. Complaints by Borrowers which Kestrel does not resolve may be referred to the Financial
Ombudsman Service; the number of complaints referred to, and upheld by, the Financial Ombudsman Service in
relation to Kestrel is published by the Financial Ombudsman Service in accordance with its usual practice.

## Kestrel as Servicer

Kestrel will service the Loans on behalf of the Issuer under the servicing agreement dated the Closing Date
between Pellam Corporate Services Ltd (the "Back-up Servicer Facilitator"), the Issuer, Kestrel and the Trustee
(the "Servicing Agreement"), in accordance with the same policies and procedures that it applies to Finance
Agreements which it owns for its own account. Kestrel will continue to service the Loans from its head office in
Leicester using the systems and staff described above, and Borrowers will continue to deal with Kestrel as
before; they will not be notified of the sale of the Loans unless a Perfection Event occurs (see section 12).
The servicing, collection and arrears procedures, the reports which the Servicer will deliver and the
circumstances in which its appointment may be terminated are described in section 13. The fee payable to the
Servicer is set out in section 15.
