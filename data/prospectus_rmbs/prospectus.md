### Risks relating to the Notes and the structure of the transaction

**The Notes are obligations of the Issuer only and are limited recourse obligations.**

The Notes will be obligations solely of the Issuer. They will not be obligations of, and will not be guaranteed by, Holdings, the Seller, the Mortgage Administrator, the Cash Manager, the Account Bank, the Swap Provider, the Arranger, the Corporate Services Provider, the Note Trustee, the Security Trustee or any other person. None of those persons accepts any liability whatsoever in respect of any failure by the Issuer to make any payment of any amount due on the Notes, and no such person has any obligation to provide funds to the Issuer, other than the Seller and the Swap Provider in the limited circumstances, and to the limited extent, expressly set out in the Transaction Documents.

The Notes are limited recourse obligations. The Issuer's obligation to pay any amount in respect of the Notes is limited to the amounts that the Issuer actually receives or recovers in respect of the Portfolio and its other assets, and that are available for that purpose under the Revenue Priority of Payments set out in Section 6, the Principal Priority of Payments set out in Section 7 or, following the delivery of a notice of enforcement by the Note Trustee to the Issuer (an "Enforcement Notice"), the post-enforcement priority of payments set out in the Conditions. If, once the Security Trustee has realised the security created by the Deed of Charge and distributed the proceeds in accordance with the applicable priority of payments, the amounts available are insufficient to pay in full all amounts due on a Class of Notes, the Noteholders of that Class will have no further claim against the Issuer in respect of the unpaid amounts, which will be extinguished.

**The Issuer has no significant assets other than the Portfolio and its rights under the Transaction Documents, and its ability to pay the Notes depends on the receipts it obtains from those assets.**

The Issuer is a special purpose company incorporated in England and Wales for the purpose of issuing the Notes and acquiring the Portfolio, as described in Section 9. The Issuer has no business operations other than those incidental to the transaction, no employees and no subsidiaries. Its only material assets will be the Portfolio, its rights under the Mortgage Sale Agreement, the Mortgage Administration Agreement, the Swap Agreement and the other Transaction Documents, and the balances standing to the credit of its accounts held with the Account Bank, including the General Reserve Fund and the Liquidity Reserve Fund described in Section 5.

The ability of the Issuer to meet its obligations under the Notes will depend primarily on the receipt by it, in a timely manner, of payments of interest and principal by the Borrowers under the Loans, of the proceeds of enforcement of the Related Security, of net payments under the Swap Agreement, of interest on its bank accounts and of any repurchase price payable by the Seller under the Mortgage Sale Agreement. If there is a shortfall in those receipts, the Issuer will have no other source of funds, apart from the reserve funds and the use of Available Principal Receipts to meet certain revenue shortfalls described below, from which to make good the shortfall. Other than the Subordinated Loan made on the Issue Date to fund the reserve funds and certain initial expenses, the Issuer will not have the benefit of any liquidity facility, guarantee or other form of credit support from any third party.

**The Notes of each Class are subordinated to the Notes of each more senior Class, and the Noteholders of the junior Classes bear losses on the Portfolio before the Noteholders of the senior Classes.**

The Notes of each Class rank in order of seniority, the Class A Notes ranking ahead of the Class B Notes, the Class B Notes ahead of the Class C Notes, the Class C Notes ahead of the Class D Notes and the Class D Notes ahead of the Class Z Notes, both as to payments of interest (the Class Z Notes bearing no interest) and as to payments of principal, in each case as set out in the Revenue Priority of Payments in Section 6, the Principal Priority of Payments in Section 7 and the post-enforcement priority of payments set out in the Conditions. The level of subordination available to each Class of Notes on the Issue Date is described in Section 5.

The subordination of the junior Classes is the principal form of credit enhancement available to the senior Classes. If the Portfolio suffers Realised Losses, those losses will in effect be borne first by the Class Z Noteholders, then by the Class D Noteholders, then by the Class C Noteholders, then by the Class B Noteholders and finally by the Class A Noteholders, in each case to the extent that the losses exceed the amounts available from excess Available Revenue Receipts and from the General Reserve Fund to make good the corresponding debit balances on the Principal Deficiency Ledger. There is no assurance that the subordination provided to any Class will protect the Noteholders of that Class from all risk of loss.

**Losses and the use of principal to pay revenue items are recorded on the Principal Deficiency Ledger, and a debit balance on a sub-ledger which is not cleared will result in a loss of principal on the corresponding Class of Notes.**

The Issuer will maintain a principal deficiency ledger comprising one sub-ledger for each Class of Notes (together, the "Principal Deficiency Ledger"). Realised Losses on the Loans, and any Available Principal Receipts applied under item (a) of the Principal Priority of Payments in Section 7 to meet a revenue shortfall, will be debited to the Principal Deficiency Ledger, starting with the Class Z sub-ledger and moving up through the Class D, Class C and Class B sub-ledgers to the Class A sub-ledger, in each case up to the principal amount outstanding of the corresponding Class. Amounts will be credited to the sub-ledgers out of Available Revenue Receipts, in order of seniority, at the levels in the Revenue Priority of Payments set out in Section 6.

The Principal Deficiency Ledger is designed so that excess revenue from the Portfolio, which would otherwise be paid down the Revenue Priority of Payments to the Subordinated Loan and as deferred consideration to the Seller, is converted into principal and applied to redeem the Notes in an amount equal to the losses suffered. Whether a debit balance can be cleared will depend on the amount of excess revenue generated by the Portfolio after payment of senior expenses, net amounts due to the Swap Provider and interest on the Rated Notes. That excess revenue will itself be reduced by the factors that generate losses: a Borrower who is in arrears pays no interest, a Loan which is enforced ceases to generate revenue, and the costs of enforcement are deducted from recoveries. Excess revenue will also be reduced if the weighted average interest rate of the Portfolio falls, for example because higher-yielding Loans prepay, because Borrowers switch to lower-rate products or because the standard variable rate is reduced.

A debit balance on a sub-ledger which has not been cleared by the time the Notes of the relevant Class fall to be redeemed in full, whether on the Final Maturity Date, on an earlier redemption date or on enforcement, will mean that the Available Principal Receipts are insufficient to redeem that Class in full, and the Noteholders of that Class will suffer a corresponding loss. In addition, while a debit balance is outstanding on the sub-ledger of a Class, the Available Revenue Receipts applied to clear it will not be available to pay amounts ranking below it in the Revenue Priority of Payments, including the interest on the more junior Classes of Notes.

**The Class Z Notes bear no interest, rank last for principal, absorb Realised Losses first and are not protected by the General Reserve Fund.**

The Class Z Notes do not bear interest, and Class Z Noteholders will receive no return on their investment other than the repayment of principal, if any. Principal on the Class Z Notes is payable at item (f) of the Principal Priority of Payments in Section 7, only after the Class A Notes, the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes have been paid the amounts due to them at the higher levels of that priority. Following the occurrence of a Sequential Payment Trigger, and in any event following the delivery of an Enforcement Notice, no principal will be paid on the Class Z Notes until all of the Rated Notes have been redeemed in full.

Realised Losses are debited first to the Class Z sub-ledger of the Principal Deficiency Ledger. The Class Z sub-ledger is cleared at item (n) of the Revenue Priority of Payments in Section 6, below the top-up of the General Reserve Fund, and the General Reserve Fund may not be applied to clear a debit balance on the Class Z sub-ledger. Accordingly, the Class Z Notes are exposed to the first Realised Losses on the Portfolio, and any recovery of those losses depends entirely on there being Available Revenue Receipts remaining after every item of higher priority, including the replenishment of the General Reserve Fund, has been paid in full.

The Class Z Notes will not be rated. The Class Z Notes are a highly leveraged exposure to the credit performance of the Portfolio, and an investor in the Class Z Notes could lose the whole of its investment even in circumstances in which the Rated Notes are redeemed in full. Prospective investors in the Class Z Notes should have the knowledge, experience and resources to evaluate the performance of a pool of residential mortgage loans over the whole life of the transaction.

**Principal on the Rated Notes is initially paid pro rata, which reduces the absolute amount of credit enhancement available to the Class A Notes, and the switch to sequential payment depends on the occurrence of a Sequential Payment Trigger.**

As described in Section 7, until a Sequential Payment Trigger occurs, and provided that the condition relating to the credit enhancement of the Class A Notes described in that Section is satisfied, Available Principal Receipts applied under items (b) to (e) of the Principal Priority of Payments will be paid pro rata to the principal amount outstanding of the Class A Notes, the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes. While principal is paid pro rata, the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes will amortise at the same rate as the Class A Notes, and the subordination which those Classes provide to the Class A Notes will fall in absolute terms in line with the reduction in the balance of the Portfolio.

The Sequential Payment Trigger is described in Section 8. It is triggered by the Arrears Trigger, the Cumulative Loss Trigger and the Reserve Trigger, each measured as set out in that Section. These measures are backward-looking, are calculated on the Calculation Date or Interest Payment Date stated in Section 8, and depend on information provided by the Mortgage Administrator. A deterioration in the Portfolio may therefore have occurred, and losses may already be in prospect, before a Sequential Payment Trigger occurs. Conversely, the junior Noteholders should note that once a Sequential Payment Trigger has occurred the switch to sequential payment is permanent, so that no principal will thereafter be paid on any Class until each more senior Class has been redeemed in full, even if the performance of the Portfolio subsequently recovers. This will extend the weighted average lives of the junior Classes, possibly significantly, and will increase the period during which they are exposed to the risk of loss.

**Shortfalls in interest on Classes of Notes other than the Most Senior Class may be deferred.**

The Conditions set out in Section 4 provide for the consequences of a shortfall in the Available Revenue Receipts available to pay interest on a Class of Notes. Failure to pay interest when due on the Most Senior Class will constitute an Event of Default under the Conditions. The "Most Senior Class" means, at any time, the Class A Notes or, if no Class A Notes are then outstanding, the most senior Class of Rated Notes then outstanding or, if no Rated Notes are then outstanding, the Class Z Notes. By contrast, as more fully described in the Conditions, a shortfall in the amount available to pay interest on any other Class of Rated Notes will not constitute an Event of Default, and the unpaid amount will instead be deferred to the next Interest Payment Date on which funds are available for that purpose in accordance with the Revenue Priority of Payments.

Interest so deferred may remain unpaid for a prolonged period and, if it has not been paid by the time the relevant Class is redeemed in full or by the Final Maturity Date, may never be paid. Noteholders of the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes should therefore be aware that the timely payment of interest on their Notes is not assured, and that the market value of their Notes may be adversely affected by any deferral, whether or not the deferred interest is ultimately paid. The Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes will become the Most Senior Class only once every more senior Class has been redeemed in full.

**The reserve funds are of limited size, the Liquidity Reserve Fund may only be used for senior expenses and interest on the Class A Notes and the Class B Notes, and the use of Available Principal Receipts to pay revenue shortfalls reduces the principal available to redeem the Notes.**

The Issuer will have two sources of liquidity to meet a shortfall in the Available Revenue Receipts. First, the General Reserve Fund, funded on the Issue Date from the proceeds of the Subordinated Loan, is available to meet shortfalls of senior expenses and interest on the Class A Notes, the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes and to clear debit balances on the Class A to D sub-ledgers of the Principal Deficiency Ledger. Secondly, the Liquidity Reserve Fund, also funded on the Issue Date from the proceeds of the Subordinated Loan, is available to meet shortfalls of senior expenses and interest on the Class A Notes and the Class B Notes only. The required amounts of the two reserve funds, and the order in which they are drawn, are set out in Sections 5 and 6. Neither reserve fund covers the Class Z Notes.

The amounts standing to the credit of the reserve funds are finite. Each draw reduces the amount available on subsequent Interest Payment Dates, and the reserve funds are replenished only from Available Revenue Receipts at the levels shown in the Revenue Priority of Payments in Section 6, which are reached only after the payment of all amounts of higher priority. In a period of sustained arrears or a disruption to collections, for example following an insolvency of the Mortgage Administrator or a failure of the payment systems through which Borrowers pay, the reserve funds could be exhausted before the shortfall is corrected. A fall in the balance of the General Reserve Fund below the level described in Section 8 will itself cause a Sequential Payment Trigger.

In addition, the Cash Manager may apply Available Principal Receipts under item (a) of the Principal Priority of Payments to pay a shortfall of senior expenses or of interest on the Most Senior Class. Any Available Principal Receipts so applied will be debited to the Principal Deficiency Ledger and will not be available on that Interest Payment Date to redeem the Notes. Unless the resulting debit balance is subsequently cleared from excess Available Revenue Receipts, this will result in a shortfall in the principal available to redeem the Notes on their final redemption, borne in the first instance by the most junior Classes outstanding. Available Principal Receipts are not available to meet shortfalls of interest on any Class other than the Most Senior Class.

**The Note Trustee and the Security Trustee will have regard to the interests of the Most Senior Class where there is a conflict between the interests of the Classes.**

The Trust Deed and the Deed of Charge will provide that, in connection with the exercise of their powers, trusts, authorities, duties, rights and discretions, the Note Trustee and the Security Trustee will have regard to the interests of the Noteholders of each Class as a class. **The ratings of the Rated Notes address only certain risks, are not recommendations to buy, sell or hold the Notes, and may be lowered, suspended or withdrawn at any time.**

The ratings expected to be assigned to the Rated Notes on the Issue Date are set out in Section 3. A rating addresses the likelihood of full and timely payment of interest due on the Most Senior Class on each Interest Payment Date, and the likelihood of payment of interest on the other Classes of Rated Notes and of principal on every Class of Rated Notes by no later than the Final Maturity Date, in each case in accordance with the methodology of the relevant Rating Agency. **Interest on the Rated Notes is calculated by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA, the amount of which will not be known until shortly before each Interest Payment Date and the market conventions for which may evolve.**

Interest on the Rated Notes will be calculated by reference to the compounded daily Sterling Overnight Index Average ("Compounded Daily SONIA") plus the applicable margin, as set out in Section 3 and in the Conditions. SONIA is administered by the Bank of England and reflects the average of the interest rates which banks pay to borrow sterling overnight from other financial institutions and other institutional investors. Compounded Daily SONIA is calculated in arrear, by compounding the daily rates over an observation period that ends shortly before the end of each interest period. As a result, the amount of interest payable on an Interest Payment Date will not be known until shortly before that date, and Noteholders may find it difficult to estimate reliably the amount of interest which they will receive. Some investors may be unable or unwilling to trade the Notes without changes to their information technology or operational systems, which could adversely affect the liquidity of the Notes.

**Changes to SONIA, or its discontinuance, may adversely affect the Notes.**

Benchmarks such as SONIA are the subject of regulatory guidance and reform in the United Kingdom and internationally, including under the Benchmarks Regulation as it forms part of the domestic law of the United Kingdom. These reforms may cause a benchmark to perform differently than in the past, to disappear entirely or to have other consequences which cannot be predicted. The Conditions provide fallback arrangements for the determination of the rate of interest if SONIA is not published on any day, including by reference to the Bank of England's Bank Rate and to the spread between Bank Rate and SONIA over a preceding period, or to any rate recommended by the Bank of England or the Financial Conduct Authority (the "FCA") as a replacement. The application of these fallbacks may result in interest on the Rated Notes being calculated at a rate that differs from that which would have applied had SONIA continued to be published, and the fallbacks may not operate as intended in circumstances of market disruption.

The Conditions also permit the Issuer, at the direction of the Cash Manager and subject to the conditions set out there, to require the Note Trustee to agree, without the consent of the Noteholders, to a modification replacing SONIA with an alternative base rate (a "Base Rate Modification") in circumstances such as the permanent discontinuance of SONIA, a public statement by its administrator or supervisor that it is no longer representative, or a change in market practice in sterling securitisations. A Base Rate Modification will not require the consent of the Noteholders unless the holders of a specified proportion of the Most Senior Class object within the period set out in the Conditions. Any alternative base rate, together with any adjustment spread, may produce a lower return than SONIA would have produced and may not match the reference rate under the Swap Agreement or the rates payable on the Loans. Any such mismatch, and the costs of implementing a Base Rate Modification, which will be borne by the Issuer, may reduce the amounts available to pay the Notes.

**The Swap Agreement hedges the interest rate risk on the Fixed Rate Loans only during their fixed-rate periods, and the hedge may not match the actual balance of the Fixed Rate Loans.**

The Notes bear interest at a floating rate calculated by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA, whereas most of the Loans bear interest at a fixed rate for an initial period of two or five years (the "Fixed Rate Loans"), as shown in Section 13. To mitigate the exposure arising from this mismatch, the Issuer will enter into the Swap Agreement with the Swap Provider on the Issue Date. Under the Swap Agreement, as described in Section 15, the Issuer will pay to the Swap Provider amounts calculated by reference to a fixed rate, and the Swap Provider will pay to the Issuer amounts calculated by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA, in each case on a notional amount determined by reference to the balance of the Fixed Rate Loans in their fixed-rate periods. Net amounts payable by the Issuer to the Swap Provider rank at item (c) of the Revenue Priority of Payments in Section 6, ahead of interest on the Class A Notes.

The swap covers the fixed-rate period only. When the fixed-rate period of a Fixed Rate Loan ends, the Loan will, unless the Borrower takes a new product, revert to the Standard Variable Rate, and the Issuer will thereafter be exposed, without any hedge, to the difference between the Standard Variable Rate and Compounded Daily SONIA. That exposure is described under "Reversion of the Fixed Rate Loans to the Standard Variable Rate, and the setting of the Standard Variable Rate, expose the Issuer to basis risk" below.

During the fixed-rate periods, the notional amount of the swap may not correspond with the actual balance of the Fixed Rate Loans. If the Fixed Rate Loans prepay, default or are repurchased more quickly than was assumed in setting the notional amount, the Issuer will be over-hedged: it will continue to pay the fixed rate on a notional amount greater than the balance of the Loans from which it receives fixed-rate interest, which will reduce the Available Revenue Receipts when SONIA is below the swap fixed rate. If the Fixed Rate Loans amortise more slowly than assumed, the Issuer will be under-hedged, and a rise in SONIA will reduce the excess spread on the unhedged balance. Fixed Rate Loans that are in arrears or in the process of enforcement continue to be included in the balance on which the swap is calculated, but generate no or reduced interest. Product Switches to new fixed-rate products after the Issue Date, if the relevant Loans remain in the Portfolio, will not be hedged unless the Swap Agreement is amended to include them, as described in Section 15.

**The Issuer may be required to make a termination payment under the Swap Agreement, which may rank ahead of payments on the Notes.**

The Swap Agreement may be terminated in the circumstances set out in it, including a failure by either party to make a payment when due, the insolvency of either party, the occurrence of certain tax or illegality events, the delivery of an Enforcement Notice, the redemption of the Notes in full, and a failure by the Swap Provider to take the remedial action required following a downgrade of its credit rating, as described under "Risks relating to the counterparties" below. On termination, a termination payment may be due from the Issuer to the Swap Provider, or from the Swap Provider to the Issuer, depending on the market value of the swap at the time. The amount of any termination payment will be calculated by reference to market quotations or the loss of the non-defaulting party and could be substantial, particularly if interest rates have moved materially since the Issue Date.

Any termination payment due from the Issuer will rank, under the Revenue Priority of Payments and the post-enforcement priority of payments, at item (c) together with the other net amounts due to the Swap Provider, ahead of all payments on the Notes, unless the termination results from an event of default in respect of which the Swap Provider is the defaulting party or from a downgrade termination event, in which case it will be paid as a subordinated swap termination payment at item (o), below all payments on the Notes. A senior termination payment would reduce, possibly significantly, the amounts available to pay interest and principal on the Notes.

**The margins on the Rated Notes increase from the Step-Up Date, but there is no assurance that the Issuer will redeem the Notes on or after that date.**

As described in Section 3, on the Interest Payment Date falling on 20 July 2031 (the "Step-Up Date") and on any Interest Payment Date thereafter, the Issuer may redeem all (but not some) of the Notes at their principal amount outstanding together with accrued interest, subject to the conditions set out in the Conditions, including the requirement that the Issuer certifies to the Note Trustee that it will have sufficient funds to discharge all amounts due on the Notes and all amounts ranking in priority to or equally with them. ### Risks relating to the Loans and the Properties

**Payments on the Notes depend on Borrowers paying their Loans, and losses not covered by excess revenue, the reserve funds and subordination will reduce the payments on the Notes.**

The Issuer's ability to pay interest and principal on the Notes depends on the Borrowers making the monthly payments due under their Loans. A Borrower's ability and willingness to pay may be adversely affected by a large number of factors, many of which are personal to the Borrower and outside the control of the Seller, the Mortgage Administrator and the Issuer. These include loss of employment or of self-employed income, a reduction in working hours or overtime, illness, disability or death, relationship breakdown, the birth of children, the need to care for a relative, an increase in other financial commitments, and an increase in the interest rate payable on the Borrower's Loan. Borrowers who become unable to pay may fall into arrears, may be offered forbearance under which payments are reduced or deferred, and may ultimately lose their Properties through possession and sale.

The Loans were underwritten in accordance with the Seller's lending criteria, described in Sections 11 and 12, which require an affordability assessment, a credit search, verification of income and a valuation of the Property by an approved valuer. The lending criteria are designed to assess whether a Borrower is able to repay the Loan at the time of origination, including under a stressed interest rate, but they cannot anticipate changes in the Borrower's circumstances after origination. The Loans were originated over a period which included the coronavirus pandemic, the rise in Bank Rate from its historical low and a sustained period of high consumer price inflation, and some Borrowers may have obtained their Loans on the basis of incomes or expenditure that have since changed materially.

If a Borrower defaults, the Issuer may suffer a loss on the Loan equal to the amount by which the balance of the Loan, together with accrued interest and the costs of enforcement, exceeds the net proceeds of sale of the Property. Such a loss is a Realised Loss. Realised Losses that are not covered by excess Available Revenue Receipts, by the General Reserve Fund or by the subordination of the junior Classes will reduce the payments on the Notes. The level of arrears, defaults and Realised Losses on the Portfolio may differ, possibly significantly, from the historical experience of the Seller described in Section 10 and from the experience of other United Kingdom residential mortgage portfolios.

**A fall in house prices raises loan-to-value ratios, may result in negative equity, and may increase Realised Losses.**

The recovery of amounts due on a defaulted Loan depends substantially on the value of the Property at the time of its sale. A fall in residential property values, whether nationally or in the region in which a Property is situated, raises the loan-to-value ratio of the Loan secured on it and may result in the balance of the Loan exceeding the value of the Property, a position commonly called negative equity. A Borrower in negative equity cannot repay the Loan by selling the Property or by remortgaging with another lender, is less able to move home for employment or family reasons, and may have less incentive to continue paying. Where such a Borrower defaults, the net proceeds of sale of the Property will be insufficient to repay the Loan in full, and a Realised Loss will result.

Residential property prices in the United Kingdom have fallen significantly on several occasions in the past, including in the early 1990s and between 2007 and 2009, and recent periods of rising interest rates have been accompanied by falls in prices in some regions and in some segments of the market. The distribution of the Loans by current and by indexed loan-to-value ratio on the Portfolio Reference Date is set out in Section 13.

**Original valuations and indexed loan-to-value ratios may not accurately reflect the value obtainable on a sale of the Properties.**

Each Property was valued at origination by a valuer approved by the Seller, or, where the Seller's lending criteria permitted, by means of an automated valuation model or a desktop valuation, as described in Section 11. A valuation is an opinion of the price that a property would achieve in the market at a particular time on the assumptions stated in it, and is not a guarantee that the property can be sold at that price. Valuations may be inaccurate as a result of error, changes in market conditions after the valuation date, defects in the property not apparent on inspection or, in rare cases, fraud.

The indexed loan-to-value ratios shown in Section 13 have been calculated by applying a regional house price index to the original valuation of each Property. A house price index measures the average movement of prices across a region and does not reflect the movement in the price of any individual property, which may be greater or smaller than the regional average, or in a direction opposite to it. Indexed values are therefore an estimate only, and the actual value of any Property, and the actual loan-to-value ratio of any Loan, may differ materially from those shown. Neither the Issuer nor the Seller has obtained new valuations of the Properties for the purposes of the issue of the Notes.

**The Properties are concentrated in certain regions of the United Kingdom, so that an adverse change in the economic or housing conditions of those regions may have a disproportionate effect on the Portfolio.**

The geographical distribution of the Properties on the Portfolio Reference Date is shown in Section 13. A material proportion of the Properties, by current balance, are located in London and the South East of England, where property values and the average balances of the Loans are higher than in other regions of the United Kingdom. Economic and housing market conditions vary between regions, and a downturn in the economy of a particular region, or in the industries on which it depends, such as financial and professional services in London and the South East, may result in higher levels of arrears and Realised Losses on the Loans secured on Properties in that region than on the Portfolio as a whole.

House prices in London and the South East have historically shown greater volatility than those in other regions, rising more quickly in periods of growth and falling more quickly in downturns. They are also more sensitive to the affordability constraints created by high price-to-income ratios, to changes in the tax treatment of higher-value properties and to changes in demand from buyers outside the United Kingdom. The concentration of the Portfolio in those regions therefore increases the sensitivity of the Notes to a regional fall in prices. The Portfolio may become more concentrated over time, because Loans in some regions may prepay or default at different rates from Loans in others.

**Interest-only Loans and Part and Part Loans expose the Issuer to the risk that Borrowers will be unable to repay the capital at maturity.**

A proportion of the Loans, shown in Section 13, are repayable on an interest-only basis (the "Interest-only Loans"), under which the Borrower pays only interest during the term and must repay the whole of the principal at the end of the term, or on a part and part basis (the "Part and Part Loans"), under which part of the principal is repaid in monthly instalments and the remainder is repayable at the end of the term. The Seller's lending criteria, described in Section 11, required each Borrower of an Interest-only Loan or of the interest-only part of a Part and Part Loan to have a credible strategy for repaying the capital at maturity, such as the sale of another property, the proceeds of an investment plan or pension, or the downsizing of the Property itself, and the Seller reviews those strategies during the term in accordance with the rules of the FCA.

The ability of a Borrower to repay the capital at maturity depends on the performance of the repayment strategy, which may not be the subject of any security in favour of the Issuer and which may produce less than expected or be applied by the Borrower for another purpose. Where the Borrower's strategy is to sell the Property, the amount realised will depend on house prices at maturity. A Borrower who is unable to repay the capital at maturity may be offered a term extension, a conversion to a capital and interest repayment basis or another form of forbearance, any of which will delay the receipt of principal by the Issuer; if no satisfactory arrangement can be made, the Mortgage Administrator may be obliged to take enforcement action, possibly against Borrowers who are elderly or vulnerable, which may be lengthy and costly.

**Reversion of the Fixed Rate Loans to the Standard Variable Rate, and the setting of the Standard Variable Rate, expose the Issuer to basis risk and may increase arrears and prepayments.**

Fixed-rate loans reset to the standard variable rate after their initial period, and the swap covers the fixed-rate period only. At the end of the initial fixed-rate period of each Fixed Rate Loan, unless the Borrower has taken a new product, the Loan will bear interest at the Seller's standard variable rate, as set from time to time by the Mortgage Administrator on behalf of the Issuer under the Mortgage Administration Agreement (the "Standard Variable Rate"). A proportion of the Loans, shown in Section 13, already bore interest at the Standard Variable Rate on the Portfolio Reference Date. The Standard Variable Rate is not linked to SONIA or to Bank Rate, and the Issuer's receipts from Loans paying the Standard Variable Rate are not hedged under the Swap Agreement.

The Issuer is therefore exposed to the risk that the Standard Variable Rate, less the costs and expenses of the Issuer, will be insufficient to pay interest on the Rated Notes at Compounded Daily SONIA plus the relevant margins. The Mortgage Administration Agreement requires the Mortgage Administrator to set the Standard Variable Rate applicable to the Loans in the Portfolio at a level which is not lower than the Seller's own standard variable rate for comparable loans retained on its balance sheet, and which is sufficient, when taken together with the other receipts of the Issuer, to meet the Issuer's obligations, subject in each case to the Mortgage Administrator's obligation to comply with applicable law and the rules of the FCA. Those rules, together with the law on unfair terms described below, constrain the discretion to set the Standard Variable Rate, and there may be circumstances in which the Mortgage Administrator is unable lawfully to set the Standard Variable Rate at the level required.

A Fixed Rate Loan that reverts to the Standard Variable Rate may become more expensive for the Borrower, sometimes materially, and the resulting increase in the monthly payment, commonly called payment shock, may lead to arrears, particularly where the Borrower is unable to remortgage elsewhere because of a fall in the value of the Property or a change in personal circumstances. Conversely, Borrowers who are able to do so will tend to refinance or take a Product Switch at the end of their fixed-rate period, which affects the rate of prepayment as described below. The proportion of Loans paying the Standard Variable Rate will therefore depend on the behaviour of Borrowers at the end of their fixed-rate periods, and the excess spread of the transaction will vary accordingly.

**The Tracker Loans bear interest by reference to Bank Rate, which is not hedged against Compounded Daily SONIA.**

A proportion of the Loans, shown in Section 13, bear interest at a margin over Bank Rate (the "Tracker Loans"). Bank Rate is set by the Monetary Policy Committee of the Bank of England. Although SONIA generally follows Bank Rate closely, the two rates are not the same: SONIA is a market rate determined by transactions in the sterling overnight unsecured market, and it may diverge from Bank Rate as a result of changes in money market conditions, in the supply of central bank reserves or in the Bank of England's operational framework. In addition, the rate payable on a Tracker Loan changes only from the date specified in the Loan's terms following a change in Bank Rate, whereas Compounded Daily SONIA reflects changes in SONIA on a daily basis.

The Issuer has not hedged the basis risk between Bank Rate and Compounded Daily SONIA. If SONIA rises relative to Bank Rate, or if the timing of rate changes on the Tracker Loans lags the corresponding change in SONIA, the excess spread of the transaction will be reduced. Some Tracker Loans may also contain a minimum rate or other terms which affect the rate payable by the Borrower. Borrowers of Tracker Loans are exposed to increases in Bank Rate immediately, and a rise in Bank Rate may increase the incidence of arrears on those Loans more quickly than on the Fixed Rate Loans.

**Borrowers may repay their Loans early, in particular at the end of their fixed-rate periods, which changes the timing of principal payments on the Notes and may reduce the excess spread.**

Borrowers may repay their Loans early, for example when their fixed-rate period ends, which changes the timing of principal payments on the Notes. A Borrower may prepay the whole or part of a Loan at any time, subject, during the fixed-rate period of a Fixed Rate Loan and in certain other cases, to the payment of an early repayment charge. Most Borrowers who prepay in full do so on the sale of the Property, on refinancing with another lender or on taking a new product. Prepayment rates in the United Kingdom are influenced by, among other things, the level of interest rates and the products available from other lenders, the end of fixed-rate periods, house prices and the volume of housing transactions, the availability of credit, government schemes, changes in stamp duty land tax and land and buildings transaction tax, and Borrowers' personal circumstances. Early repayment charges may not be enforceable in all cases, and their amount is constrained by the rules of the FCA.

Because the Loans in the Portfolio are concentrated in two- and five-year fixed-rate products, the end of those fixed-rate periods may result in concentrations of prepayments in particular quarters, followed by periods of low prepayment. A high rate of prepayment will shorten the weighted average lives of the Notes and may result in Noteholders receiving principal at a time when they are unable to reinvest it at an equivalent return; a low rate of prepayment will extend them. Prepayments may also be concentrated among the more creditworthy Borrowers, who are best able to refinance, leaving in the Portfolio a higher proportion of Borrowers who have been unable to do so, with a corresponding effect on the credit quality of the remaining Loans.

**Product Switches and Further Advances may change the composition of the Portfolio or result in the repurchase of Loans.**

The Seller may offer a Borrower, at the end of a fixed-rate period or at any other time, a new interest rate product, such as a new fixed rate or a tracker rate (a "Product Switch"), and may agree to make further advances to a Borrower secured on the same Property (a "Further Advance"). The Seller is subject to the rules of the FCA which permit it, in defined circumstances, to offer a Product Switch to an existing Borrower who is up to date with payments without carrying out a full affordability assessment, and the Seller has committed under the Mortgage Charter, described below, to offer Product Switches to eligible Borrowers on that basis.

As more fully described in Section 14, a Loan which becomes subject to a Product Switch may remain in the Portfolio only if the conditions set out in the Mortgage Sale Agreement are satisfied on the date of the switch, including conditions relating to the type of new product, the absence of material arrears, the maturity date of the Loan and, for a new fixed-rate product, the arrangements for hedging it under the Swap Agreement. If those conditions are not satisfied, the Seller will be required to repurchase the Loan. A Loan which becomes subject to a Further Advance will be repurchased by the Seller. Any repurchase will result in the Issuer receiving principal in respect of the Loan earlier than it would otherwise have done, with the effects on the weighted average lives of the Notes described above.

**The Scottish Loans are secured by standard securities and are subject to Scots law, under which the remedies available on default and the transfer of the Loans differ from those applicable to the English Loans.**

A proportion of the Properties, shown in Section 13, are situated in Scotland, and the Scottish Loans are secured by standard securities governed by Scots law. The Scottish Loans and their Related Security, and the Scottish Declaration of Trust described below, are governed by Scots law, while the other Transaction Documents are governed by English law. Under Scots law, a standard security is a statutory form of heritable security created under the Conveyancing and Feudal Reform (Scotland) Act 1970 and is constituted by registration in the Land Register of Scotland. The rights and remedies of a heritable creditor are those set out in that Act and in the standard conditions which it incorporates, as varied by the terms of the security.

The enforcement of a standard security over residential property in Scotland is subject to the Home Owner and Debtor Protection (Scotland) Act 2010, which requires the heritable creditor to comply with pre-action requirements, including the provision of information to the debtor, the making of reasonable efforts to agree proposals for the payment of arrears and the consideration of whether the debtor has taken steps to deal with the default, and to obtain a decree from the sheriff court before exercising a power of sale or entering into possession of residential property, even where the debtor consents. The court has power to continue proceedings or to make an order with conditions, having regard to the factors listed in the legislation, including the nature of and reasons for the default and the ability of the debtor to fulfil the obligations within a reasonable period. As a result, enforcement of a Scottish Loan may take longer than enforcement of an English Loan, and the costs incurred may be higher.

**Possession and enforcement in England and Wales are subject to statutory and procedural requirements which may delay recoveries and increase their cost.**

Before the Mortgage Administrator may take possession of a Property in England or Wales, it must, in almost every case involving an owner-occupier, obtain a possession order from the county court. Before issuing proceedings it must comply with the Pre-Action Protocol for Possession Claims based on Mortgage or Home Purchase Plan Arrears in Respect of Residential Property, which requires the lender to provide information to the Borrower, to consider reasonable proposals for the payment of arrears, to consider whether the Borrower has made a claim for benefits or is selling the Property, and to treat possession proceedings as a last resort. Under section 36 of the Administration of Justice Act 1970 and section 8 of the Administration of Justice Act 1973, the court has a wide discretion to adjourn proceedings, or to suspend or postpone the date for delivery of possession, if it appears that the Borrower is likely to be able within a reasonable period to pay the sums due, and it is common for the court to grant suspended orders conditional on the Borrower paying the current instalment plus an amount towards the arrears.

The rules of the FCA in the Mortgages and Home Finance: Conduct of Business sourcebook ("MCOB"), described below, and the Mortgage Charter impose further requirements before possession is sought. In addition, the period between the first missed payment and the eventual sale of a Property depends on the capacity of the courts and of the enforcement agents; court backlogs have in the past caused lengthy delays, and possession proceedings were suspended entirely for a period during the coronavirus pandemic. Delays in obtaining possession and selling the Properties will delay recoveries and increase the costs of enforcement and the interest which accrues on the Loans, which may increase the Realised Losses and the period during which the Issuer has insufficient revenue to meet its obligations.

**The Properties may not be adequately insured, and the Issuer's interest in buildings insurance may be limited.**

Each Borrower is required under the terms of the Loan to insure the Property against fire and other usual risks for its full reinstatement value with an insurer acceptable to the Seller. The Seller does not, however, monitor whether each Borrower maintains such insurance throughout the term, and no assurance can be given that a Property will be insured, or insured for an adequate amount, when damage occurs. Where a Property is leasehold, buildings insurance will usually be maintained by the landlord or the management company, over whose arrangements the Seller has little control. The Issuer will have the benefit, by way of assignment or declaration of trust, of the Seller's interest in any buildings insurance policy in respect of a Property to the extent that this interest is capable of transfer, and the Seller will maintain, for the benefit of the Issuer, a contingency insurance policy covering certain losses arising where a Borrower's insurance is not in force. Such a policy will be subject to its own exclusions and limits, and there is no assurance that claims under it will be paid in full or in time.

Some Properties may be exposed to risks which are expensive or difficult to insure, such as flooding, subsidence and coastal erosion. Insurance for flood risk is available for many existing homes through the Flood Re scheme, which applies to qualifying residential properties built before 1 January 2009 and which is intended to operate until 2039. Properties built after that date are outside the scheme, and the availability and cost of flood insurance for them depend on the commercial insurance market. Damage to a Property that is uninsured or underinsured will reduce its value and may increase the Realised Loss if the Borrower defaults.

**Leasehold Properties, flats and properties affected by building safety issues present additional risks.**

A proportion of the Properties are held on long leases, in particular flats in London and other urban areas. The value of a leasehold property is affected by the unexpired term of the lease, the level of ground rent and service charges and the terms of the lease, and a lease may in certain circumstances be forfeited by the landlord for breach of covenant by the leaseholder, although statutory protections restrict forfeiture of long residential leases. Reforms of the law of leasehold, including the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 and any further legislation, may affect the value of leasehold properties and the rights of landlords and leaseholders in ways which cannot be predicted.

Following the Grenfell Tower fire in 2017, the identification of unsafe cladding and other fire safety defects in residential buildings has affected the value and saleability of flats in affected buildings, and some lenders have restricted lending on such properties pending remediation or the provision of an external wall fire review. The Building Safety Act 2022 protects qualifying leaseholders from some or all of the costs of remediating relevant defects, but the protections are not complete, and a Borrower who owns a flat in an affected building may be unable to sell or remortgage it, may face service charges for interim measures, and may be more likely to fall into arrears. In the event of enforcement, a Property in an affected building may realise a substantially lower price than its original valuation.

**The insolvency of a Borrower, statutory debt solutions and debt respite schemes may delay or reduce recoveries.**

A Borrower who is in financial difficulty may enter into a formal insolvency procedure or debt solution. In England and Wales these include bankruptcy, individual voluntary arrangements and debt relief orders; in Scotland they include sequestration, protected trust deeds and the debt arrangement scheme. The Loan Warranties set out in Section 12 address the position of Borrowers on the Portfolio Reference Date, but a Borrower may enter such a procedure at any time after that date. Although the Related Security will generally survive the insolvency of the Borrower, so that the Issuer remains entitled to enforce it against the Property, the insolvency may delay enforcement, may result in the Borrower's interest in the Property vesting in a trustee in bankruptcy or trustee in sequestration with whom the Mortgage Administrator must deal, and will in practice prevent the recovery of any shortfall from the Borrower personally after the sale of the Property.

The Debt Respite Scheme (Breathing Space Moratorium and Mental Health Crisis Moratorium) (England and Wales) Regulations 2020 allow an eligible individual in England and Wales to obtain a moratorium, generally for sixty days or, for a person receiving mental health crisis treatment, for the duration of that treatment plus thirty days, during which creditors may not take enforcement action or charge interest, fees or penalties in respect of qualifying debts. Arrears on a mortgage loan are qualifying debts, although the ongoing monthly payments continue to be due. Comparable moratorium arrangements apply in Scotland under the Bankruptcy (Scotland) Act 2016. The use of these schemes by Borrowers may delay the collection of arrears and the commencement of enforcement proceedings and reduce the interest and charges recoverable on the Loans.

**Borrowers may be entitled to set off claims they have against the Seller against the amounts due on their Loans.**

The Seller is a building society which accepts deposits from its members, and some Borrowers may hold savings accounts with the Seller. In addition, a Borrower may have, or may come to have, claims against the Seller, for example for damages for breach of contract, for misrepresentation or for breach of the rules of the FCA, which a Borrower may bring under section 138D of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 ("FSMA"). As described under "The sale of the English Loans takes effect in equity only, and the Issuer will not obtain legal title to the English Loans unless a Perfection Event occurs" below, the sale of the English Loans to the Issuer will take effect as an equitable assignment, and notice of the assignment will not be given to the Borrowers unless a Perfection Event occurs. Until notice is given, a Borrower may set off against the amounts due under the Loan any claim which it has against the Seller, whether or not connected with the Loan, and after notice is given, a Borrower may continue to set off claims which arose before the notice and claims which arise out of the same transaction as, or are closely connected with, the Loan.

Where the Seller becomes insolvent, the insolvency set-off rules may apply to mutual debts between the Seller and a Borrower, including a deposit held by the Borrower with the Seller, so that the Borrower's liability under the Loan may be reduced by the amount of the deposit. Although deposits held with the Seller are protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme up to the applicable limit, and a Borrower who received compensation may have less incentive to assert a set-off, the interaction between the Financial Services Compensation Scheme and the rights of set-off of borrowers in the insolvency of a deposit-taker is complex and has not been fully tested. The Mortgage Sale Agreement will entitle the Issuer to recover from the Seller any amount by which a Borrower reduces its payments by set-off, but this right will be of limited value if the Seller is insolvent. The exercise of rights of set-off by Borrowers may therefore reduce the amounts received by the Issuer and the amounts available to pay the Notes.

**Borrowers may cease to occupy their Properties or may let them without consent, which may change the risk profile of the Loans.**

The Loan Warranties set out in Section 12 require that each Borrower occupied the Property as his or her main residence at origination. After origination, a Borrower may move out of the Property, let it to a tenant or allow it to be occupied by others, with or without the consent of the Seller. A Property which is let may be subject to rights of occupation in favour of the tenant, and the Renters' Rights Act 2025 has changed the regime for residential tenancies in England, including by abolishing assured shorthold tenancies and the "no fault" eviction procedure under section 21 of the Housing Act 1988. The existence of a tenancy, whether or not consented to, may make it more difficult to obtain vacant possession of the Property on enforcement and may reduce its sale value.

**The Issuer's remedy for a breach of the Loan Warranties is limited to the repurchase of the affected Loan by the Seller, and no independent investigation of the Loans has been made.**

The Seller will give the Loan Warranties set out in Section 12 in respect of each Loan on the Issue Date. Neither the Issuer, the Note Trustee, the Security Trustee nor the Arranger has undertaken, or will undertake, any investigation of the Loans or the Properties, other than the review of a sample of the Loans carried out by an appropriate and independent party as required under the UK Securitisation Framework and described in Section 17. Each of them will rely entirely on the Loan Warranties. If a Loan Warranty proves to have been untrue in respect of a Loan and the breach cannot be remedied, the Seller will be obliged to repurchase the Loan at its current balance plus accrued interest. This is the sole remedy of the Issuer for such a breach.

The Seller's obligation to repurchase is an unsecured obligation, and its value depends on the financial condition of the Seller at the time of the claim. If the Seller is insolvent or is otherwise unable or unwilling to repurchase a Loan, the Issuer will bear any loss on that Loan. Breaches of the Loan Warranties may come to light only after a Loan has defaulted, possibly many years after the Issue Date, when the Seller's financial position may have changed. The Loan Warranties are given as at the Issue Date (or, where so stated, as at the Portfolio Reference Date) and do not cover events which occur thereafter.

### Legal and regulatory risks

**The Loans are regulated mortgage contracts under FSMA, and a failure to comply with the applicable rules may make them unenforceable or give rise to claims by Borrowers.**

Each Loan is a "regulated mortgage contract" for the purposes of FSMA and the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Regulated Activities) Order 2001, and the activities of entering into, administering, arranging and advising on regulated mortgage contracts are regulated activities which may only be carried on by persons authorised by the FCA. The Seller is so authorised. The Issuer will not itself carry on the regulated activity of administering regulated mortgage contracts, because the Loans will be administered by the Mortgage Administrator, which holds the relevant permission. If the Mortgage Administrator's appointment is terminated, the Issuer will be required to appoint a replacement mortgage administrator with the requisite permission within one month; if it fails to do so, it may itself be in breach of the general prohibition in FSMA. A regulated mortgage contract entered into by an unauthorised lender is unenforceable against the borrower except with the approval of the FCA or the court.

The Seller's lending, administration and arrears management are subject to the rules of the FCA in MCOB and in the other parts of the FCA Handbook, which cover, among other things, financial promotions, the disclosure of information before and after the contract is made, responsible lending and affordability assessment, interest-only lending, the charging of fees and early repayment charges, and the treatment of borrowers in arrears or in payment difficulty. A Borrower who suffers loss as a result of a contravention of the rules of the FCA by the Seller may bring an action for damages under section 138D of FSMA, and may set off the amount of the claim against amounts due under the Loan. The FCA may also require the Seller to carry out a review of past business and to pay redress to affected Borrowers. Any such claims or redress may reduce the amounts received by the Issuer in respect of the Loans, and the Issuer's right to require the Seller to indemnify it or to repurchase affected Loans may be of limited value if the Seller is insolvent.

**The FCA's Consumer Duty imposes outcome-based obligations on the Seller and the Mortgage Administrator which may affect the setting of rates and charges and the treatment of Borrowers.**

Since 31 July 2023, firms authorised by the FCA have been subject to the Consumer Duty, set out in Principle 12 and in PRIN 2A of the FCA Handbook, in relation to products and services which remain open to retail customers, and the Consumer Duty applies to closed products and services from 31 July 2024. The Consumer Duty requires firms to act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers, to act in good faith, to avoid causing foreseeable harm and to enable and support customers in pursuing their financial objectives, and it sets out specific outcomes relating to products and services, price and value, consumer understanding and consumer support. The Consumer Duty applies to the Seller and the Mortgage Administrator in respect of the Loans throughout their term, including in the setting of the Standard Variable Rate, the treatment of Borrowers at the end of their fixed-rate periods, the charging of arrears and early repayment fees and the management of arrears. The Consumer Duty may therefore constrain the ability of the Mortgage Administrator to set the Standard Variable Rate at the level required to maintain the excess spread of the transaction, to charge fees or to enforce the Loans, and the FCA may require the Seller to pay redress if it considers that the Seller has failed to comply, which may reduce the amounts available to the Issuer.

**The rules of the FCA on arrears management and forbearance, and the Mortgage Charter, may delay enforcement and reduce or defer payments by Borrowers.**

MCOB 13 requires a lender to deal fairly with any borrower who is in arrears or in payment difficulty, to establish and implement clear, effective and appropriate policies and procedures for the fair treatment of such borrowers, and to repossess a property only where all other reasonable attempts to resolve the position have failed. Lenders must consider a range of forbearance options, including an extension of the term, a change in the type of the loan or a deferral of interest payments, a capitalisation of arrears in defined circumstances, a temporary reduction in payments and the use of government schemes, and must give appropriate support to customers in vulnerable circumstances. The FCA made rules which took effect in November 2024 strengthening these protections and incorporating into MCOB a number of the measures previously contained in temporary guidance, including requirements to consider forbearance tailored to the customer's circumstances and to reduce or waive charges and interest where appropriate.

In June 2023 a group of lenders, His Majesty's Treasury and the FCA agreed the Mortgage Charter (the "Mortgage Charter"), under which participating lenders committed, among other things, that a borrower will not be forced to leave his or her home without consent within twelve months of the first missed payment, that a borrower who is up to date with payments may switch to interest-only payments for six months or extend the term of the loan to reduce the monthly payment, with an option to revert to the original term within six months, without a new affordability assessment and without affecting the borrower's credit file, and that a borrower nearing the end of a fixed-rate deal may lock in a new product up to six months in advance. The Seller applies the commitments of the Mortgage Charter in the administration of its mortgage book, including the Loans, as described in Section 14.

The application of these rules and commitments may result in Borrowers paying less than their contractual monthly instalments for a period, in the extension of the term of some Loans, in the conversion of some Loans to interest-only payments for a period and in a delay of twelve months or more in the commencement of possession proceedings. Loans subject to such forbearance may not be reported as in arrears, or may be reported as in arrears at a lower level, than would otherwise have been the case, so that the Arrears Ratio may not fully reflect the stress in the Portfolio. Each of these measures will reduce or delay the amounts received by the Issuer. The FCA and the government may introduce further measures of a similar kind in future, including in response to an economic shock, as they did during the coronavirus pandemic when lenders were required to offer payment deferrals of up to six months.

**Terms of the Loans may be unfair and therefore unenforceable against Borrowers.**

Part 2 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 provides that a term of a consumer contract which is unfair is not binding on the consumer. A term is unfair if, contrary to the requirement of good faith, it causes a significant imbalance in the parties' rights and obligations to the detriment of the consumer. Terms which specify the main subject matter of the contract or the price payable are exempt from assessment for fairness only if they are transparent and prominent. The FCA and the Competition and Markets Authority each have powers to challenge unfair terms, and the FCA has published guidance on the fairness of variation terms in financial services contracts, which is relevant to the terms under which the Standard Variable Rate and the rates on other variable Loans may be changed.

If a term of a Loan, such as a term permitting the Mortgage Administrator to vary the Standard Variable Rate, to charge early repayment or arrears fees or to call in the Loan, were found to be unfair, the term would not be binding on the Borrower, and the Issuer might be required to repay any amounts collected under it. A finding of unfairness in respect of a term in common use could affect a large number of Loans. The Seller will give Loan Warranties, set out in Section 12, as to the origination of the Loans in accordance with its lending criteria, but there can be no assurance that a court or regulator will not find a term of the Loans to be unfair, and any such finding may reduce the amounts available to pay the Notes.

**Complaints to the Financial Ombudsman Service may result in awards against the Seller or the Issuer which are not consistent with the strict legal position.**

A Borrower may refer a complaint about the Seller or the Mortgage Administrator to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which determines complaints by reference to what is, in its opinion, fair and reasonable in all the circumstances of the case, taking into account the law, regulations, regulators' rules and guidance and good industry practice. The Financial Ombudsman Service may therefore make a determination which differs from that which a court would make, and may order the payment of compensation up to the applicable award limit, or direct that the terms of a Loan be varied. Individual determinations do not set binding precedents, but a determination may be followed in similar complaints and may have an effect on a large number of Loans. Any compensation awarded against the Seller in respect of a Loan, if not paid by the Seller, may be set off by the Borrower against the Loan, reducing the amounts received by the Issuer.

**The Seller is a building society, and its insolvency or resolution may affect the Issuer.**

The Seller is a building society incorporated under the Building Societies Act 1986 and authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority (the "PRA") and regulated by the FCA and the PRA. If the Seller were to become insolvent, the insolvency regimes applicable to building societies, which apply the provisions of the Insolvency Act 1986 with modifications, and the building society special administration and insolvency procedures under the Banking Act 2009, as applied to building societies, would apply. In addition, the special resolution regime under the Banking Act 2009 applies to building societies, and gives the Bank of England, His Majesty's Treasury and the PRA powers to deal with a failing building society, including powers to transfer all or part of its business to a private sector purchaser or a bridge institution, to apply the bail-in tool as modified for building societies, and to suspend or override contractual rights.

The exercise of those powers in relation to the Seller could affect its ability to perform its obligations as Seller, Mortgage Administrator and Cash Manager, including its obligation to repurchase Loans in breach of the Loan Warranties and to transfer collections to the Issuer. A partial property transfer could separate the Seller's obligations under the Transaction Documents from the assets and personnel needed to perform them. Secondary legislation made under the Banking Act 2009 contains safeguards protecting capital market arrangements, such as the transaction, from partial property transfers, and restricts the termination of contracts by reason of the exercise of resolution powers. However, these safeguards are subject to exceptions, and the authorities may make further secondary legislation varying their scope. There can be no assurance that the exercise of resolution powers, or the insolvency of the Seller, will not adversely affect the Issuer and the Noteholders.

**The sale of the Loans to the Issuer may be recharacterised or challenged on the insolvency of the Seller.**

The Seller will sell the Loans to the Issuer under the Mortgage Sale Agreement. Hartwell Quinn LLP and Grantley Moss LLP, as legal advisers, will deliver opinions on the Issue Date to the effect, subject to the assumptions and qualifications set out in those opinions, that the sale of the English Loans constitutes an effective equitable assignment and that the Scottish Declaration of Trust creates an effective trust under Scots law, in each case which would not be recharacterised as a secured loan on the insolvency of the Seller. A legal opinion is an expression of professional judgement and is not a guarantee of the outcome of any dispute. English courts have generally upheld transactions which are expressed and structured as sales and in which the economic substance is consistent with a sale, rather than recharacterising them as secured financings, but a court could reach a different conclusion on the particular facts.

An insolvency official of the Seller might also seek to set aside the sale of the Loans as a transaction at an undervalue under section 238 of the Insolvency Act 1986, as a preference under section 239 of that Act, or as a transaction defrauding creditors under section 423 of that Act, or under the equivalent provisions of Scots law in the case of the Scottish Loans. A transaction is not at an undervalue where it is entered into in good faith for the purpose of carrying on business and there were reasonable grounds for believing that it would benefit the company. The Seller will warrant that the consideration paid for the Loans represents their fair value and that it is solvent on the Issue Date. If the sale were set aside, the Issuer might be required to return the Loans or their value to the Seller, and would have only an unsecured claim in the Seller's insolvency for the purchase price.

**The sale of the English Loans takes effect in equity only, and the Issuer will not obtain legal title to the English Loans unless a Perfection Event occurs.**

The English Loans and their Related Security will be sold to the Issuer by way of an equitable assignment. Legal title to the English Loans will remain with the Seller, and the Issuer will not apply to HM Land Registry to register itself as proprietor of the mortgages, nor give notice of the assignment to the Borrowers, unless one of the events described in the Mortgage Sale Agreement occurs (each a "Perfection Event"), which include the insolvency of the Seller, the termination of the appointment of the Mortgage Administrator, a requirement of law or regulation, a downgrade of the Seller below the level set out in the Mortgage Sale Agreement and the security over the Issuer's assets becoming enforceable. Following a Perfection Event, the Seller will be required to execute legal transfers and the Issuer will be entitled to give notice to the Borrowers, and the Seller will grant the Issuer a power of attorney for this purpose.

As a consequence of the Issuer holding equitable title only, until the Perfection Event: (a) a bona fide purchaser of the legal title to an English Loan for value without notice of the Issuer's interest could take free of that interest; (b) the rights of the Issuer may be subject to the rights of Borrowers to set off claims against the Seller, as described above, and to any other equities arising before notice; (c) the Issuer may need to join the Seller as a party to any proceedings against a Borrower or to enforce the Related Security; and (d) payments made by a Borrower to the Seller will discharge the Borrower's obligations, so that the Issuer will be exposed to the risk that the Seller fails to account for them, as described under "Collections are paid initially into an account of the Seller" below. The Seller will undertake in the Mortgage Sale Agreement not to deal with the legal title to the English Loans in a manner inconsistent with the Issuer's interest.

**The Issuer's interest in the Scottish Loans will be held under a Scottish declaration of trust.**

Under Scots law, the transfer of a real right in a standard security requires an assignation in the statutory form registered in the Land Register of Scotland. To avoid the cost of registering assignations of each Scottish Loan on the Issue Date, the Seller will, on the Issue Date, declare a trust under Scots law over the Scottish Loans and their Related Security in favour of the Issuer (the "Scottish Declaration of Trust"), and the Issuer will acquire the beneficial interest in them. The Seller will remain the registered heritable creditor under each standard security and will be the party entitled to enforce it, unless and until assignations are registered following a Perfection Event.

Under the Trusts (Scotland) Act 1921, as amended, and the general principles of Scots law, trust property held by a trustee does not form part of the trustee's patrimony available to its personal creditors in its insolvency, and the beneficial interest of the Issuer should therefore be protected on the insolvency of the Seller. For the trust to be effective, the Issuer, as beneficiary, must be given notice of the trust, the trust property must be identified with sufficient precision and the Seller must comply with its obligations as trustee. The Trusts and Succession (Scotland) Act 2024 reforms aspects of the Scots law of trusts. The Issuer's interest in the Scottish Loans will in other respects be subject to risks similar to those described above for the English Loans held in equity only, including risks relating to set-off by Borrowers and to the need to involve the Seller in enforcement. The Issuer's beneficial interest under the Scottish Declaration of Trust will be assigned in security by the Issuer in favour of the Security Trustee under a Scots law assignation in security.

**The insolvency of the Issuer, and the statutory regime applicable to floating charges, could reduce the amounts available to the Noteholders.**

The Issuer has been structured as an insolvency-remote special purpose vehicle: its activities are restricted by its articles of association and the Transaction Documents, the parties to the Transaction Documents have agreed limited recourse and non-petition provisions, and its directors are provided by the Corporate Services Provider. These arrangements reduce, but do not eliminate, the risk that the Issuer will become insolvent.

The security granted by the Issuer under the Deed of Charge will include fixed charges and a floating charge. Under section 72A of the Insolvency Act 1986, the holder of a qualifying floating charge created on or after 15 September 2003 is prohibited from appointing an administrative receiver, subject to the exceptions in sections 72B to 72GA, including the exception for capital market arrangements. The Issuer has been advised that the security should fall within that exception, so that the Security Trustee should be entitled to appoint an administrative receiver, but the exception has not been considered by the courts in a reported case and there is no assurance that it will apply. If it does not, the security could be enforced only by the appointment of an administrator, whose primary objective is to act in the interests of the creditors as a whole and who may not act in the interests of the Noteholders.

Whether a charge is fixed or floating is determined by the courts by reference to the degree of control which the chargee has over the charged assets, and not by the label which the parties give it. Following the decision of the House of Lords in *In re Spectrum Plus Limited* in 2005, a charge over assets, such as bank accounts or receivables, which the chargor is free to deal with in the ordinary course of business is likely to be characterised as a floating charge. Some of the security expressed in the Deed of Charge to be fixed may therefore take effect as floating security. Assets subject to a floating charge are available, before the floating charge holder, to meet the expenses of any insolvency proceedings and the claims of preferential creditors, which since 1 December 2020 include certain amounts owed to His Majesty's Revenue and Customs ("HMRC") as secondary preferential debts. In addition, under section 176A of the Insolvency Act 1986, a liquidator, administrator or receiver must set aside a prescribed part of the net property subject to a floating charge for the unsecured creditors, currently up to a maximum of £800,000, unless an exemption applies.

### Risks relating to the counterparties

**The Issuer depends on the Mortgage Administrator, and a failure by or replacement of the Mortgage Administrator could disrupt collections and increase costs.**

The Mortgage Administrator will administer the Loans on behalf of the Issuer under the Mortgage Administration Agreement, as described in Section 14, including collecting payments, setting the Standard Variable Rate, offering Product Switches, managing arrears and conducting enforcement. The performance of the Portfolio will depend to a significant extent on the quality of the Mortgage Administrator's administration and arrears management. Any deterioration in that quality, for example as a result of a loss of experienced staff, a failure of its information technology systems or a change in its policies, could increase arrears and Realised Losses.

The appointment of the Mortgage Administrator may be terminated by the Issuer or the Security Trustee on the occurrence of the termination events set out in the Mortgage Administration Agreement, including a failure to pay, a material breach of its obligations and its insolvency. No back-up mortgage administrator has been appointed on the Issue Date. If the appointment of the Mortgage Administrator is terminated, the Issuer, with the assistance of the Corporate Services Provider, will be required to use reasonable endeavours to appoint a substitute mortgage administrator with the requisite permissions under FSMA and experience of administering residential mortgage loans in England, Wales and Scotland. There is no assurance that a substitute will be found, or that it will be found within the period permitted by FSMA as described above. The transfer of administration to a substitute will involve the transfer of data and the redirection of Borrowers' direct debits, which is likely to cause temporary disruption to collections and an increase in arrears, and the fees payable to a substitute may be higher than those payable to the Mortgage Administrator. Those fees will rank ahead of payments on the Notes.

**The Issuer depends on the Cash Manager to make calculations and payments, and those calculations depend on information provided by the Mortgage Administrator.**

The Cash Manager will determine, on each Calculation Date, the Available Revenue Receipts and the Available Principal Receipts, the amounts to be drawn from or credited to the reserve funds, the entries to be made on the Principal Deficiency Ledger and whether a Sequential Payment Trigger has occurred, and will make payments on each Interest Payment Date in accordance with the Revenue Priority of Payments and the Principal Priority of Payments, as described in Section 15. These determinations will be made on the basis of information provided by the Mortgage Administrator. If that information is not provided in time, the Cash Manager will make its determinations on the basis of estimates derived from previous periods, as described in Section 15, and will make reconciling adjustments once the information becomes available. Errors in the information or in the Cash Manager's calculations could result in payments being made to the wrong parties or in the wrong amounts, and it may not be possible to recover amounts paid in error.

**Collections are paid initially into an account of the Seller, and the Issuer is exposed to the risk of commingling of collections with the Seller's own funds.**

Borrowers pay their monthly instalments, primarily by direct debit, into an account maintained in the name of the Seller (the "Collection Account"), into which payments on other mortgage loans of the Seller are also paid. The Mortgage Administrator will transfer collections in respect of the Loans from the Collection Account to the Issuer's transaction account with the Account Bank within the period set out in the Mortgage Administration Agreement, as described in Section 15. The Seller will declare a trust over the amounts standing to the credit of the Collection Account which relate to the Loans in favour of the Issuer (the "Collection Account Declaration of Trust").

Until collections are transferred to the Issuer's transaction account, they are held in the Collection Account together with the Seller's own funds. If the Seller becomes insolvent, or is subject to the exercise of resolution powers, while collections are held in the Collection Account, the Issuer may be delayed in obtaining those collections and may be unable to recover them in full. The effectiveness of the Collection Account Declaration of Trust depends on the amounts relating to the Loans being identifiable, and the tracing of trust money commingled with other funds may be difficult and costly. In addition, payments made by direct debit may be reclaimed by Borrowers under the Direct Debit Guarantee, and any amount so reclaimed after it has been transferred to the Issuer will reduce the Issuer's receipts. Following a Perfection Event, Borrowers will be directed to pay into an account in the name of the Issuer, but some Borrowers may continue to make payments to the Seller.

**A downgrade of the Swap Provider may require it to post collateral, to transfer its obligations or to obtain a guarantee, and if it fails to do so the Swap Agreement may be terminated.**

The Swap Agreement requires the Swap Provider, if its credit ratings fall below the levels specified in the Swap Agreement by reference to the criteria of the Rating Agencies, to take one or more remedial actions within the periods specified there, which may include posting collateral to the Issuer, transferring its obligations to a suitably rated replacement, obtaining a guarantee from a suitably rated guarantor or taking other action that maintains the ratings of the Rated Notes. Any collateral posted by the Swap Provider will be held in a separate account and will be available only to meet the obligations of the Swap Provider and will not form part of the Available Revenue Receipts, except to the extent provided in the Swap Agreement.

**A downgrade or insolvency of the Account Bank could result in the loss of, or delay in access to, funds held in the Issuer's accounts.**

The Issuer's transaction account, the General Reserve Fund and the Liquidity Reserve Fund will be held in accounts with the Account Bank. Under the Bank Account Agreement, if the Account Bank ceases to have the ratings required by the criteria of the Rating Agencies, the Issuer, or the Cash Manager on its behalf, will be required to transfer the accounts to a suitably rated bank within the period set out in the Bank Account Agreement, or to obtain a guarantee of the Account Bank's obligations. ### Macroeconomic and market risks

**Changes in Bank Rate and in market interest rates may affect Borrowers' ability to pay, the rate of prepayment and the excess spread of the transaction.**

The Monetary Policy Committee of the Bank of England raised Bank Rate from its historical low in December 2021 to its highest level for fifteen years in August 2023, in response to a period of high inflation, before beginning to reduce it from August 2024. Movements in Bank Rate, and in the market expectations of its future path reflected in swap rates and in the rates offered by lenders on new fixed-rate products, affect the monthly payments of Borrowers on Tracker Loans and on Loans paying the Standard Variable Rate immediately, and affect Borrowers of Fixed Rate Loans at the end of their fixed-rate periods.

A rise in interest rates may increase arrears and Realised Losses on the Loans, may reduce house prices and may reduce the rate of prepayment, extending the weighted average lives of the Notes. A fall in interest rates may increase prepayments and Product Switches, shortening the weighted average lives of the Notes and reducing the excess spread of the transaction if higher-yielding Loans are repaid or switched to lower rates. Changes in Bank Rate may also affect SONIA, the Standard Variable Rate and the rates payable on the Tracker Loans differently and at different times, with the effects on the basis risk of the Issuer described above.

**An increase in unemployment, or a fall in household incomes, may increase arrears and Realised Losses on the Loans.**

Arrears and defaults on residential mortgage loans in the United Kingdom have historically been closely related to the level of unemployment and to changes in household incomes. An economic downturn in the United Kingdom, whether caused by domestic factors, by a slowdown in the global economy, by financial market disruption, by changes in trade policy and tariffs, by geopolitical events or by a pandemic, may result in higher unemployment, lower wage growth, reduced working hours and lower self-employed income, any of which may reduce the ability of Borrowers to pay their Loans. An economic downturn would also be likely to reduce house prices, with the effect on Realised Losses described above. The coincidence of higher unemployment with lower house prices, which has characterised past downturns, would have a particularly adverse effect on the Portfolio.

**Increases in the cost of living may reduce the disposable income available to Borrowers to pay their Loans.**

Consumer price inflation in the United Kingdom reached its highest level for over forty years in October 2022, driven in particular by increases in energy and food prices, and the cost of essential goods and services has remained elevated relative to incomes for many households. Increases in the cost of living, including energy bills, food, council tax, insurance, transport and childcare, and the burden of other consumer debt, reduce the disposable income available to Borrowers to meet their mortgage payments, particularly where they coincide with increases in mortgage interest rates. Any further increases may result in higher arrears on the Loans, and in a higher proportion of Borrowers in financial difficulty requiring forbearance, with the consequences described above.

**General economic, political and market conditions in the United Kingdom and internationally may affect the Portfolio and the market value of the Notes.**

The performance of the Portfolio and the market value of the Notes may be affected by general economic, political and market conditions, including the fiscal and monetary policy of the United Kingdom government and the Bank of England, the level of public debt and the conditions in the gilt market, the United Kingdom's trading relationship with the European Union and other trading partners, conflicts and geopolitical tensions, energy supply and prices, and the conditions in the banking sector and in the market for mortgage-backed securities. Events of this kind have in the past led to sharp falls in the market value of asset-backed securities and to periods in which they could not be sold, irrespective of the performance of the underlying assets. The occurrence of a pandemic or other public health emergency could also lead to government measures, such as payment deferrals and suspensions of possession proceedings, which reduce or delay the receipts of the Issuer, as occurred in 2020 and 2021.

### Condition 5 — Interest

**5.1 Accrual.** Each Rated Note bears interest on its Principal Amount Outstanding from (and including) the Issue
Date. Interest is payable quarterly in arrear on each Interest Payment Date for the Interest Period ending on
(but excluding) that Interest Payment Date. The Class Z Notes do not bear interest, and no amount is payable on

accrue (after as well as before judgment) at the rate determined under this Condition until the day on which all
sums due on that Note are received by or on behalf of the relevant Noteholder.

**5.2 Rate of Interest.** The rate of interest payable on each Class of Rated Notes for each Interest Period (the
"Rate of Interest") is the aggregate of Compounded Daily SONIA for that Interest Period and the Margin of that
Class. The "Margin" of each Class is:

(a) for each Interest Period ending on or before the Step-Up Date, the margin for that Class shown in the column
    "Margin to Step-Up Date" in Section 3; and
(b) for each Interest Period beginning on or after the Step-Up Date, the margin for that Class shown in the
    column "Margin after Step-Up Date" in Section 3.

If the Rate of Interest of a Class for an Interest Period would be less than zero, it will be zero.

**5.3 Compounded Daily SONIA.** "Compounded Daily SONIA" means, for an Interest Period, the rate of return of a
daily compound interest investment in sterling, with the daily Sterling Overnight Index Average as reference
rate, calculated by the Agent Bank on the Interest Determination Date for that Interest Period by the following
method, the resulting percentage being rounded, if necessary, to the fourth decimal place, with 0.00005 being
rounded upwards:

(a) for each London Banking Day in the Observation Period, the Agent Bank multiplies the SONIA rate for that
    London Banking Day by the number of calendar days from (and including) that London Banking Day to (but
    excluding) the next London Banking Day, divides the result by 365 and adds one;
(b) the Agent Bank multiplies together the factors obtained under paragraph (a) for all the London Banking Days
    in the Observation Period and subtracts one from the product; and
(c) the Agent Bank multiplies the result by 365 and divides it by the number of calendar days in the Observation
    Period.

For this purpose: "**SONIA**" means, for any London Banking Day, the Sterling Overnight Index Average rate for
that day as provided by the Bank of England, as administrator of that rate, or by any successor administrator,
to authorised distributors and as then published on the relevant screen page (the "Relevant Screen Page") or,
if that page is unavailable, as otherwise published by those authorised distributors, in each case on the next
London Banking Day; "**London Banking Day**" means any day on which commercial banks are open for general
business (including dealing in foreign exchange and foreign currency deposits) in London; "**Observation
Period**" means, for an Interest Period, the period from (and including) the date falling five London Banking
Days before the first day of that Interest Period to (but excluding) the date falling five London Banking Days
before the Interest Payment Date at the end of it; and "**Interest Determination Date**" means, for an Interest
Period, the fifth London Banking Day before the Interest Payment Date at the end of it.

Under this method, which shifts both the SONIA rates and their weighting to the Observation Period, each Interest
Period is matched with an Observation Period of equal length that ends five London Banking Days before the
Interest Payment Date, so that the Rate of Interest is known before the payment is made.

**5.4 Fallback.** If, for any London Banking Day in an Observation Period, SONIA is not made available on the
Relevant Screen Page and has not otherwise been published by the authorised distributors, SONIA for that day
will be:

(a) the Bank Rate of the Bank of England (the "Bank Rate") prevailing at close of business on that London
    Banking Day; plus
(b) the mean of the spread of SONIA to the Bank Rate over the previous five days on which SONIA has been
    published, excluding the highest spread (or, if there is more than one highest spread, one only of those
    highest spreads) and the lowest spread (or, if there is more than one lowest spread, one only of those
    lowest spreads).

If the Bank of England publishes guidance as to how SONIA is to be determined, or as to any rate that is to
replace SONIA, the Agent Bank will, to the extent reasonably practicable, follow that guidance for so long as
SONIA is not available. If the Rate of Interest cannot be determined in accordance with Conditions 5.3 and 5.4,
it will be the Rate of Interest last determined for the relevant Class for the preceding Interest Period, with
the Margin applicable to the current Interest Period substituted for the Margin applicable to that preceding
Interest Period (or, for the first Interest Period, the rate that would have applied had the Notes been in issue
for a period of equal length ending on the Issue Date). This Condition is without prejudice to the
modification of the Conditions on a Benchmark Rate Disruption Event under Condition 12.7.

**5.5 Interest Amount.** The Agent Bank will, as soon as practicable after the Interest Determination Date,
calculate the amount of interest payable on each Class of Rated Notes for the Interest Period (the "Interest
Amount") by applying the Rate of Interest to the Principal Amount Outstanding of that Class on the first day of
the Interest Period (after any payment of principal on that day), multiplying the product by the actual number
of days in the Interest Period divided by 365, and rounding the result to the nearest penny (half a penny being
rounded upwards). The Interest Amount payable on each Note of the Class is its rateable share of that amount,
rounded down to the nearest penny.

### Condition 6 — Redemption

**6.1 Final redemption.** Unless previously redeemed in full, the Issuer will redeem each Class of Notes at its

allow for the enforcement of the security for a Mortgage Loan that is in default at its maturity and the sale of
the Property. The Issuer may not redeem the Notes other than in accordance with this Condition and Condition 10.

**6.2 Mandatory redemption.** Before the delivery of an Enforcement Notice, the Issuer will on each Interest
Payment Date apply the Available Principal Receipts in accordance with the Principal Priority of Payments set
out in Section 7, in redemption of the Notes:

(a) sequentially, first in redemption of the Class A Notes until they are redeemed in full, then of the Class B
    Notes, then of the Class C Notes, then of the Class D Notes and finally of the Class Z Notes; or
(b) for so long as no Sequential Payment Trigger has occurred and the other conditions for *pro rata* payment set
    out in Section 7 are satisfied, *pro rata* between the Class A Notes, the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and
    the Class D Notes, with the Class Z Notes redeemed only after the Rated Notes have been redeemed in full.

The trigger events that give rise to a Sequential Payment Trigger, and the permanence of the switch to sequential
payment, are described in Section 8. The amount applied to each Class is apportioned among the Notes of that
Class *pro rata* to their Principal Amount Outstanding, rounded down to the nearest penny.

**6.3 Principal Deficiency Ledgers.** The Cash Manager will maintain a principal deficiency ledger comprising five
sub-ledgers, one for each Class (the "Class A Principal Deficiency Ledger", the "Class B Principal Deficiency
Ledger", the "Class C Principal Deficiency Ledger", the "Class D Principal Deficiency Ledger" and the "Class Z
Principal Deficiency Ledger", together the "Principal Deficiency Ledgers"). On each Calculation Date:

(a) the Cash Manager will debit to the Principal Deficiency Ledgers the Realised Losses on the Mortgage Loans in
    the Collection Period, and the Available Principal Receipts applied under item (a) of the Principal Priority
    of Payments to pay a revenue shortfall;
(b) each debit will be recorded first on the Class Z Principal Deficiency Ledger, until its balance equals the
    Principal Amount Outstanding of the Class Z Notes, then on the Class D Principal Deficiency Ledger, the
    Class C Principal Deficiency Ledger, the Class B Principal Deficiency Ledger and finally the Class A Principal
    Deficiency Ledger, in each case up to the Principal Amount Outstanding of the relevant Class; and
(c) on the following Interest Payment Date, Available Revenue Receipts (and, for the Class A to D Principal
    Deficiency Ledgers, drawings on the General Reserve Fund) will be credited to the Principal Deficiency
    Ledgers at the positions set out in Section 6, starting with the Class A Principal Deficiency Ledger, and the
    amounts so credited will form part of the Available Principal Receipts.

A debit balance on a Principal Deficiency Ledger is not an Event of Default and does not of itself reduce the
Principal Amount Outstanding of any Note. To the extent that a debit balance has not been cleared by the Final
Maturity Date or on enforcement, the holders of the relevant Class will bear the corresponding loss under
Condition 11.

**6.4 Optional redemption on or after the Step-Up Date.** On the Step-Up Date and on any Interest Payment Date

### Condition 11 — Enforcement and Limited Recourse

**11.1 Proceedings.** After the delivery of an Enforcement Notice, the Note Trustee and the Security Trustee may, at
their discretion and without further notice, take such proceedings, steps or actions as they think fit to enforce
the Notes, the Trust Deed and the Security, including the appointment of a receiver or administrative receiver

    or requested to do so in writing by the holders of at least 25 per cent of the aggregate Principal Amount
    Outstanding of the Most Senior Class; and
(b) it has been indemnified and/or secured and/or prefunded to its satisfaction.

that the cash flows from the Portfolio are not likely to be sufficient to discharge those amounts as they fall
due, or if the holders of each Class of Notes have so directed by Extraordinary Resolution.

Note Trustee or the Security Trustee, having become bound to proceed, fails to do so within a reasonable time and

winding up, administration or liquidation of the Issuer until two years and one day after all the Notes have been

will apply all amounts received or recovered by it, including the balances of the General Reserve Fund and the
Liquidity Reserve Fund but excluding Swap Collateral (except to the extent applied in satisfaction of an amount
owed by the Swap Provider), in the order of priority set out in the Deed of Charge (the "Post-Enforcement Priority
of Payments"). Under that order, the fees, costs and expenses of the Note Trustee, the Security Trustee and any Receiver are
paid first; then the fees of the Mortgage Administrator, the Cash Manager, the Account Bank, the Agents and
the Corporate Services Provider; then amounts due to the Swap Provider other than subordinated swap termination
payments; then interest and principal on the Class A Notes, followed in turn by interest and principal on the
Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes, with interest on each Class paid before principal on that
Class and nothing paid on a Class until each Class ranking above it has been repaid in full; then principal on the
Class Z Notes; then subordinated swap termination payments; then interest and principal on the Subordinated Loan;
and finally deferred consideration to the Seller.

The Revenue Priority of Payments and the Principal Priority of Payments in Sections 6 and 7 cease to apply on the
delivery of an Enforcement Notice. After enforcement, revenue and principal receipts are no longer separated, the
reserve funds are not replenished and no amount is paid *pro rata* between Classes.

**11.5 Limited recourse.** The Notes are limited recourse obligations of the Issuer, payable only from the amounts
received by it from the Portfolio, the Swap Agreement, the Issuer Accounts and its other rights under the
Transaction Documents. If, after the Security has been fully realised and the proceeds applied in accordance with
the Post-Enforcement Priority of Payments, any amount remains unpaid on a Class of Notes, the Security Trustee
will certify that no further assets are available and the Noteholders of that Class will have no further claim
against the Issuer for the unpaid amount, which will be extinguished. The Notes are not obligations of, and are
not guaranteed by, Marlowe Building Society, Halcyon Bank plc, the Note Trustee, the Security Trustee, the
Corporate Services Provider, Holdings or any other person.

**11.6 Protection of the Note Trustee.** The Trust Deed contains provisions for the indemnification of the Note
Trustee and the Security Trustee and for their relief from responsibility, including from taking proceedings
unless indemnified and/or secured and/or prefunded to their satisfaction. They may rely on any certificate or
report given by the Issuer, the Cash Manager or the Mortgage Administrator, and on the advice of any professional
adviser, without further enquiry. No recourse may be had against any director, officer or shareholder of the
Issuer or of Holdings for any amount due on the Notes.

(a) for an Ordinary Resolution, persons holding or representing more than 25 per cent of the aggregate Principal

(b) for an Extraordinary Resolution other than one sanctioning a Basic Terms Modification, persons holding or

## Section 5 — Credit Structure {-}
**Subordination.** Payments on the Notes are made in order of seniority: the Class A Notes rank ahead of the
Class B Notes, which rank ahead of the Class C Notes, and so on down to the Class Z Notes. The Class Z Notes
absorb losses first. On the Issue Date the Notes ranking below the Class A Notes represent
12.50 per cent of all the Notes.

**Reserve funds.** Two reserve funds will be funded on the Issue Date from the proceeds of a Subordinated Loan
made by the Seller:

| Reserve | Required amount | Amount on the Issue Date (£) |
|---|---|---:|
| General Reserve Fund | 1.50 per cent of the Class A, B, C and D Notes | 5,820,000 |
| Liquidity Reserve Fund | 1.00 per cent of the Class A and B Notes | 3,660,000 |

The General Reserve Fund covers shortfalls of senior expenses and interest on the Class A to D Notes, and debit balances on the Class A to D Principal Deficiency Ledgers; it does not cover the Class Z Notes. The Liquidity Reserve Fund covers shortfalls of senior expenses and interest on the Class A and B Notes only.
Each reserve fund is topped up to its required amount from Available Revenue Receipts in the order set out in
Section 6.

**Principal Deficiency Ledger.** Realised losses, and principal receipts used to pay revenue items, are recorded
on the Principal Deficiency Ledger, starting with the Class Z sub-ledger and moving up to the Class A
sub-ledger. Excess revenue clears the sub-ledgers in order of seniority.

**Interest rate swap.** Most loans pay a fixed rate for two or five years while the Notes pay a floating rate.
The Issuer enters into a swap with the Swap Provider to cover this difference.

## Section 7 — Principal Priority of Payments {-}
Before the delivery of an enforcement notice, the Cash Manager will apply the Available Principal Receipts on

## Section 8 — Trigger Events {-}
A **Sequential Payment Trigger** occurs if any of the following events happens:

- **Arrears Trigger**: on any Calculation Date, the current balance of loans 3 or more months in arrears divided by the current balance of the portfolio exceeds 2.50 per cent.
- **Cumulative Loss Trigger**: on any Calculation Date, the cumulative realised losses divided by the portfolio balance at the Issue Date exceeds 0.40 per cent.
- **Reserve Trigger**: on any Interest Payment Date, the General Reserve Fund balance divided by the General Reserve Fund Required Amount is less than 85 per cent.

Once a Sequential Payment Trigger has occurred, principal is paid sequentially, the Class A Notes first,
and the switch is permanent.

### History

The Society traces its origins to the Marlowe Permanent Benefit Building Society, founded in Worcester in 1871
by a group of local tradesmen and nonconformist ministers who wished to help working families buy their own
homes. In its first fifty years the Society lent almost exclusively within the county of Worcestershire,
funding its loans from the savings of local members. Between 1928 and 1974 the Society grew by a series of
twelve transfers of engagements from smaller societies in the West Midlands, the Welsh Marches and the
Severn valley, and in 1975 it adopted its present name.

The Society's members rejected a proposal to convert the Society into a bank in 1997, when several large
building societies demutualised. The board at the time concluded, and every subsequent board has
reaffirmed, that the Society's long-term interest and that of its members is best served by remaining a
mutual. In 2009 the Society accepted a transfer of engagements from a smaller regional society based in
Gloucestershire, which added 14 branches and approximately 41,000 members. Since 2012 the Society's growth has
been organic.

The Society opened its intermediary lending channel in 2004 and began lending throughout England, Wales and
Scotland in 2011. It began lending in Scotland on the security of standard securities over Scottish
residential property in 2016, through intermediaries and, from 2019, through its branch in Edinburgh. The
Society has offered regulated mortgage contracts as defined in the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000
(Regulated Activities) Order 2001 since the regulation of mortgage lending began on 31 October 2004.

## Section 11 — Origination and Underwriting {-}

The information in this Section 11 has been provided by the Seller and describes the lending criteria and

(the "Lending Criteria"). The Mortgage Loans in the Provisional Portfolio were originated between 2019 and
2025, as stated in the Loan Warranties, and the Lending Criteria have been amended from time to time during
that period. This Section 11 describes the Lending Criteria as they applied during that period in all
material respects; where an amendment was material to the Mortgage Loans, it is described below. The Seller
may amend the Lending Criteria after the Issue Date, as described under "Changes to the Lending Criteria"
below, and any such amendment will apply to further advances and product switches made after the Issue Date.

This Section 11 does not contain, and should not be read as a restatement of, the warranties given by the
Seller about each Mortgage Loan. Those are the Loan Warranties set out in Section 12, and the remedies for
their breach are set out in the same Section. The characteristics of the Provisional Portfolio on the

### Distribution channels

The Seller originates residential mortgage loans through two channels.

(a) **Direct channel.** Applications in the direct channel are taken by the Seller's mortgage advisers in
its branches, by its telephone advisers in its customer contact centre in Worcester, and through its website.
Every regulated mortgage contract sold through the direct channel is sold with advice, given by a mortgage
adviser employed by the Seller who holds a mortgage advice qualification recognised by the FCA and is
assessed as competent under the Seller's training and competence scheme. The Seller does not offer
execution-only sales of new owner-occupied mortgage loans, other than in the limited circumstances permitted
by MCOB 4.8A, and it has not done so for any Mortgage Loan in the Provisional Portfolio.

(b) **Intermediary channel.** The Seller accepts applications from mortgage intermediaries that are authorised
by the FCA with permission to advise on and arrange regulated mortgage contracts, or that are appointed
representatives of such firms, and that are registered with the Seller (each, a "Registered Intermediary").
Registered Intermediaries submit applications through the Seller's intermediary portal. The Seller registers
an intermediary firm only after checking its FCA authorisation and permissions, and it monitors each firm's
submissions for the quality of applications, the incidence of misstatement and fraud, and early arrears. It
suspends or terminates the registration of any firm whose submissions fall below its standards, and it
reports any suspected fraud to the FCA and to the relevant fraud prevention agencies.

Registered Intermediaries are paid a procuration fee by the Seller on completion of each loan. The
procuration fee is a fixed proportion of the loan amount and does not vary with the interest rate, the
product or the loan-to-value ratio of the loan, so that the intermediary has no financial incentive to
recommend one of the Seller's products over another. The Seller discloses the procuration fee in the
illustration given to the borrower, as MCOB requires. The intermediary, not the Seller, gives the advice to
the borrower, but the Seller applies the same Lending Criteria and the same underwriting process to every
application regardless of the channel through which it is received.

Before the Seller receives a full application, the applicant (directly or through a Registered Intermediary)
may obtain a decision in principle. A decision in principle is based on the applicant's declared income and
commitments and on a credit search that leaves a soft footprint on the applicant's credit file. It is not an
offer of a loan and the Seller is not bound by it.

### The application

A full application is made on the Seller's application form, in paper or electronic form, and must be
signed or electronically confirmed by each applicant. The application records, for each applicant, his or her
identity and address history for at least the previous three years, nationality and residency status,
employment and income, existing credit commitments and other committed expenditure, dependants, and the
purpose of the loan; and, for the property, its address, tenure, type and purchase price or estimated value.

Every applicant must be an individual who is at least 18 years of age at the date of the application. The
loan must be repaid by the date on which the youngest applicant reaches the maximum age set in the Lending
Criteria, unless the Seller is satisfied, on evidence, that the applicant will have sufficient income in
retirement to meet the monthly payments for the part of the term that extends beyond his or her expected
retirement date. A maximum of four applicants may be party to a loan, and every person who is a legal owner of
the property must be an applicant.

The Seller lends to applicants who are United Kingdom nationals, nationals of other countries who have
indefinite leave to remain or settled status in the United Kingdom, and, subject to additional criteria,
nationals of other countries who have a right to live and work in the United Kingdom for a period that the
Seller considers sufficient. The Seller verifies the identity and address of every applicant in accordance
with the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations
2017, by electronic verification against credit reference agency data and, where that verification is not
sufficient, by examination of documents.

### Credit search and credit scoring

The Seller obtains a full credit search on every applicant from at least one credit reference agency. The
credit search records the applicant's existing credit commitments and their conduct, any county court
judgments (in England and Wales) and decrees (in Scotland), any bankruptcy, sequestration, individual voluntary
arrangement, protected trust deed or debt relief order, and the applicant's registration on the electoral
roll. The Seller declines any application in which an applicant:

(a) is an undischarged bankrupt, or has had his or her estate sequestrated and has not been discharged;

(b) has been the subject of a bankruptcy order, sequestration, individual voluntary arrangement, protected
trust deed or debt relief order within the period specified in the Lending Criteria before the date of the
application, whether or not since discharged or completed;

(c) has a county court judgment or decree registered against him or her in the period specified in the
Lending Criteria, above the value specified in them, that has not been satisfied;

(d) has had a property taken into possession by a lender, or has given up a property voluntarily to a lender,
at any time; or

(e) has missed payments on an existing mortgage, secured loan or rent obligation in the period specified in
the Lending Criteria before the application, beyond the number permitted by them.

Applications that pass these policy rules are scored by the Seller's application scorecard. The scorecard is a
statistical model, developed by the Seller's credit risk function from the performance of its own mortgage
lending and from credit reference agency data, that estimates the probability that a loan will fall into
serious arrears within a defined outcome period. It uses the applicant's credit file data, the applicant's
declared information and the characteristics of the loan. The score determines whether the application is
accepted, referred to an underwriter or declined. The scorecard is recalibrated at least every two years and
monitored quarterly by the credit risk function, and every change to it, including a change to a cut-off
score, is approved under the Seller's model governance framework. The scorecard in use from 2019 to 2021 was
replaced by a redeveloped scorecard in the second half of 2021; the redevelopment did not change the Seller's
credit risk appetite.

The Seller does not lend to applicants who are assessed as having a significant adverse credit history, and
it does not offer, and has not offered during the period in which the Mortgage Loans were originated, any
product designed for borrowers with impaired credit records. The Seller has not originated any self-certified
mortgage loan, and every Mortgage Loan in the Provisional Portfolio was originated after the verification of
income described below.

### Affordability

The Seller assesses the affordability of every loan in accordance with the responsible lending rules in
MCOB 11.6. Under those rules the Seller may not enter into a regulated mortgage contract, or vary one in a way
that increases the amount borrowed, unless it can demonstrate that the loan is affordable for the borrower,
having taken full account of the borrower's income and committed and basic essential expenditure, together
with the effect of reasonably expected future interest rate increases.

The affordability assessment has three elements.

(a) **Income.** The Seller determines the applicant's net income, after tax and national insurance
contributions, from the verified gross income described under "Verification of income" below. It includes
basic salary in full and, subject to evidence of regularity over the period required by the Lending Criteria,
a proportion of overtime, commission, bonus and allowances. It may include, subject to evidence, income from
pensions, from maintenance payments under a court order or a registered agreement, and from certain state
benefits. It does not include income from the letting of a room in the mortgaged property unless the Lending
Criteria expressly permit it.

(b) **Expenditure.** The Seller deducts from net income the applicant's committed expenditure, being the
payments due on other credit commitments, including any that the applicant has declared will be repaid on or
before completion only where the Seller has evidence that they will be; child care costs; maintenance
payments; and other contractual commitments. It then deducts basic essential expenditure and basic quality of
living costs. For those it uses the higher of the amount declared by the applicant and the amount produced by
the Seller's expenditure model, which estimates the expenditure of a household of the applicant's composition,
income and location from statistics published by the Office for National Statistics and is updated at least
once a year. The Seller does not accept a declared expenditure that is lower than its modelled expenditure
without evidence.

(c) **Interest rate stress.** The Seller calculates the monthly payment on the loan at a stressed interest
rate (the "Affordability Stress Rate") and requires the applicant's net income, after the deductions in
paragraph (b), to cover it. In accordance with MCOB 11.6.18, the Affordability Stress Rate reflects market
expectations of interest rates over at least the first five years of the loan. For a loan with an initial fixed
rate of five years or more, the Seller may apply a lower stress to the initial fixed rate, as MCOB permits. The
Affordability Stress Rate is set by the Seller's assets and liabilities committee, reviewed at least quarterly
by reference to market interest rate expectations, the Bank Rate and the Seller's standard variable rate, and
applies equally to every channel. Before August 2022 the Seller also applied the affordability test
recommended by the Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee, in addition to its own stress; that
recommendation was withdrawn with effect from August 2022 and the Seller has applied its own Affordability
Stress Rate alone since then.

In addition to the affordability assessment, the Seller limits the amount it will lend by reference to a
multiple of the applicants' gross income, which varies with the loan-to-value ratio of the loan and the level
of income. The Seller manages the proportion of its new lending at high loan-to-income multiples within the
limit on such lending that the PRA applies to mortgage lenders in accordance with the Financial Policy
Committee's recommendation, and within the lower internal limit set by its board risk committee.

Where the term of the loan extends beyond the expected retirement date of an applicant, the Seller assesses
affordability for the part of the term after retirement on the applicant's expected retirement income, as
MCOB 11.6 requires. Where the loan is on an interest-only basis, in whole or in part, the Seller assesses
affordability on a capital and interest basis, as described under "Interest-only and part and part loans" below.

### Verification of income

The Seller verifies the income of every applicant in every channel, as MCOB 11.6 requires, by evidence that
is independent of the applicant. The evidence required depends on the source of income.

(a) **Employed applicants.** The Seller requires the applicant's most recent payslips for the period specified
in the Lending Criteria, together with the most recent form P60 where variable income is included. It may
verify the income electronically, by checking credit reference agency current account turnover data or by an
open banking connection with the applicant's consent, in which case the Seller may reduce the documents it
requires. Where the applicant has been in his or her current employment for a short period or is on a fixed
term contract, the Seller requires additional evidence of continuity of income.

(b) **Self-employed applicants.** The Seller requires evidence of income for at least the two most recent
complete tax years, in the form of the tax calculations and tax year overviews issued by HMRC or accounts
certified by a qualified accountant, and it uses the average of those years or, if lower, the most recent
year. For an applicant who is a director of a limited company holding a material shareholding, the Seller
assesses salary and dividends and may, subject to the Lending Criteria, take account of the applicant's share
of retained profit.

(c) **Other income.** Pension income is verified by a pension statement or bank statements; maintenance and
benefit income by the court order, agreement or benefit award letter and bank statements.

Underwriters check the documents for consistency with each other, with the application and with the credit
search, and the Seller's fraud prevention team reviews every application that its fraud detection rules
identify for investigation. The Seller uses a national fraud database and shares data with other lenders
through it, as permitted by data protection law.

### Valuation

Every property offered as security must be valued for the Seller before the Seller makes an offer of loan.
The valuation is carried out in one of two ways.

(a) **Physical valuation.** A physical valuation is carried out by a valuer who is a member or fellow of the
Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors and a registered valuer under its valuation standards, and who is
instructed through the Seller's panel of valuation firms, managed for it by a panel management company. The
valuer inspects the property, reports on its condition, tenure, construction and suitability as security, and
gives an opinion of its market value. The Seller does not accept valuations instructed by the applicant or by
an intermediary.

(b) **Automated valuation.** For applications that meet the criteria set by the Seller's credit risk function,
the Seller may rely on an automated valuation model ("AVM") instead of a physical valuation. The Seller uses
an AVM supplied by an independent provider, which estimates the value of the property from comparable sales,
Land Registry and Registers of Scotland price data and the characteristics of the property, and returns a
confidence score. The Seller accepts an AVM valuation only where the confidence score meets the level set in
the Lending Criteria, the property is of standard construction, and the loan-to-value ratio and the loan amount
are within the limits set for AVM valuations, which are lower than those for physical valuations. AVM
valuations are used principally for remortgages and are not used for new-build properties or properties of
non-standard construction.

The Seller lends on freehold houses, leasehold houses and flats and, in Scotland, heritable property. A
leasehold property must have an unexpired lease term at the end of the mortgage term that meets the Lending
Criteria, and the ground rent and service charge must be within acceptable limits. The Seller does not lend
on properties of a construction type that its valuation panel classes as unsuitable for mortgage purposes, on
properties with agricultural restrictions, on properties used wholly or mainly for business purposes, or on
properties that are not to be occupied by the borrower as his or her main residence.

For new-build properties, the Seller requires the property to be covered by a structural warranty from a
provider that it accepts, and it applies lower maximum loan-to-value ratios to new-build flats and to
properties purchased from a developer with incentives, in accordance with the disclosure of incentives
standard used in the industry. The valuation of a new-build property is based on its market value without the
benefit of incentives.

The loan-to-value ratio of each Mortgage Loan at origination was calculated as the amount of the loan,
including any fees added to it, divided by the lower of the purchase price and the valuation of the property
(in the case of a purchase) or the valuation (in the case of a remortgage). The maximum loan-to-value ratio
permitted by the Loan Warranties is set out in Section 12, and the loan-to-value ratios of the Provisional
Portfolio are set out in Section 13.

### Underwriting and lending authorities

Every application that is not declined by the policy rules or by the scorecard is reviewed by an underwriter
before an offer is issued. Underwriters are employed by the Seller in its mortgage operations centre; the
Seller does not delegate any underwriting decision to an intermediary or to any third party. The underwriter
checks the application against the Lending Criteria, reviews the evidence of identity and income, confirms the
affordability assessment, reviews the valuation and the conveyancing requirements, and either approves the
application, refers it for a higher authority, requests further information or declines it.

Each underwriter holds a personal lending mandate (a "Lending Authority"), granted by the director of lending
and recorded on the mortgage administration system, that limits the loan amount and the loan-to-value ratio
up to which he or she may approve an application, and the types of case that he or she may approve. The
Seller has four tiers of Lending Authority, from trainee underwriters, whose decisions are all reviewed by a
senior underwriter, to the senior credit managers in the credit risk function. An application above an
underwriter's Lending Authority must be approved by an underwriter with a sufficient one. Applications for
the largest loan amounts permitted by the Lending Criteria must be approved by two holders of the highest tier
of Lending Authority, one of whom must be a member of the credit risk function.

The Seller's quality assurance team reviews a sample of the cases approved by each underwriter every month,
including every case approved by an underwriter in his or her first six months, and reports the results to
the credit risk function.

### Exceptions to the Lending Criteria

An underwriter holding the appropriate Lending Authority may approve an application that falls outside a
non-mandatory element of the Lending Criteria (an "Exception") where he or she is satisfied that the loan is
affordable and that the application presents an acceptable credit risk, and records the reasons. Exceptions
may be approved for matters such as the length of employment, the treatment of a particular source of income
or the age of a payslip. No Exception may be approved to an element of the Lending Criteria that the Seller
classifies as mandatory, which include the affordability assessment, the verification of income, the
requirements as to bankruptcy and possession, the maximum loan-to-value ratio and the requirement for owner
occupation.

The proportion of applications approved by Exception is monitored monthly by the credit risk function against
a limit set by the board risk committee, and the performance of loans approved by Exception is tracked
separately. Mortgage Loans approved by Exception are included in the Provisional Portfolio if they satisfy the
Loan Warranties.

### Offer and completion

When an application is approved the Seller issues a mortgage offer to the applicant, together with the
European Standardised Information Sheet required by MCOB 6A. An offer is valid for the period stated in it,
normally six months for a purchase. Completion is carried out by a conveyancing solicitor or licensed
conveyancer in England and Wales, or a solicitor in Scotland, who is a member of the Seller's conveyancing
panel and who acts for the Seller as well as for the borrower, unless the borrower's own conveyancer is
separately approved. The conveyancer must report on title in accordance with the standard instructions of the
Seller, and the Seller releases the loan only after a satisfactory report on title.

The Seller requires the mortgage over a property in England or Wales to be registered at HM Land Registry as a
first legal charge, and the standard security over a property in Scotland to be registered in the Land
Register of Scotland as a first-ranking standard security. The Seller requires the property to be insured
against fire and other usual risks for at least its reinstatement value, by buildings insurance arranged by
the borrower or, for a leasehold flat, under a block policy arranged by the landlord or management company. The
Seller does not require life cover as a condition of the loan.

### Products

The Seller offers its owner-occupied mortgage loans on the following interest rate bases. Each Mortgage Loan
is, at any time, on one of these bases, and the proportion of the Provisional Portfolio on each basis on the
Portfolio Reference Date is set out in Section 13.

(a) **Fixed rate products.** The loan bears interest at a fixed rate for an initial period, which for the
Mortgage Loans is two or five years (the "Fixed Rate Period"), after which it bears interest at the Seller's
Standard Variable Rate unless the borrower takes a new product. Mortgage Loans in their Fixed Rate Period are
"Fixed Rate Loans". Most fixed rate products carry an early repayment charge, expressed as a declining
proportion of the amount repaid, if the borrower repays the loan, or more than a permitted annual overpayment,
during the Fixed Rate Period. The interest rate risk that arises from Fixed Rate Loans is hedged by the Swap
described in Section 15.

(b) **Tracker products.** The loan bears interest at a margin over the Bank of England's Bank Rate (the "Bank
Rate"), either for an initial period or for the whole term (the "Tracker Loans"). The rate changes on the
first day of the month following a change in the Bank Rate, or on such other date as the offer states. Tracker
Loans are subject to a minimum rate, which is the margin over the Bank Rate set out in the offer, so that the
rate on a Tracker Loan cannot fall below that margin.

(c) **Standard Variable Rate.** The Seller's standard variable rate (the "Standard Variable Rate") is the
variable rate set by the Seller from time to time and applying to loans whose product period has ended. The
Seller may change the Standard Variable Rate in the circumstances set out in the mortgage conditions, which
include changes in the Bank Rate and in the Seller's cost of funds, and must give borrowers notice of any
change as the mortgage conditions and MCOB require. After the Issue Date the Mortgage Administrator will set
the Standard Variable Rate applicable to the Mortgage Loans on behalf of the Issuer, as described in Section 14.
Loans on the Standard Variable Rate are not subject to early repayment charges.

The Seller does not offer, and none of the Mortgage Loans is, an offset mortgage, a flexible mortgage with a
facility to redraw, a discounted variable rate product, a capped rate product or a loan in a currency other
than sterling. Overpayments are permitted on every product, subject to early repayment charges where they
apply, and reduce either the monthly payment or the term at the borrower's choice.

### Interest-only and part and part loans

The Seller offers interest-only loans and loans repayable partly on an interest-only basis and partly on a
capital and interest basis ("part and part" loans) to owner-occupiers who meet the following additional
criteria, which applied throughout the period in which the Mortgage Loans were originated:

(a) the borrower must have a repayment strategy for the interest-only part of the loan that is credible and
verified at the time of the application, as the responsible lending rules in MCOB 11.6 require for interest-only lending, and the Seller accepts as a repayment
strategy only the sale of the mortgaged property (subject to the minimum equity requirement in the Lending
Criteria), investments and pension lump sums of the types specified in the Lending Criteria, or a combination
of them;

(b) the amount on an interest-only basis, and the overall loan-to-value ratio, must be within the lower limits
set by the Lending Criteria for interest-only lending;

(c) the borrower must meet the minimum income requirement set by the Lending Criteria for interest-only
lending; and

(d) affordability is assessed on a capital and interest basis over the term of the loan, at the Affordability
Stress Rate, so that the borrower could afford to convert the loan to a capital and interest basis if the
repayment strategy failed.

The Mortgage Administrator contacts every interest-only borrower at intervals during the term, and in any case
in the years before the maturity of the loan, to ask the borrower to confirm that the repayment strategy
remains in place, in accordance with the FCA's guidance on the fair treatment of interest-only customers. The
treatment of interest-only loans that are not repaid at maturity is described in Section 14. The proportion of
the Provisional Portfolio repayable on an interest-only or part and part basis is set out in Section 13, and
the risks of such loans are described in Section 1.

### Further advances

A borrower may apply to the Seller for a further advance secured by the existing mortgage or standard security.
An application for a further advance is assessed under the Lending Criteria applying at the date of the
application, including a new affordability assessment, a credit search and, where required by the Lending
Criteria, a new valuation, which may be an AVM valuation.

The Issuer will not fund any further advance. If the Seller agrees to make a further advance on a Mortgage
Loan after the Issue Date, the Seller will repurchase that Mortgage Loan, and its Related Security, from the
Issuer before the further advance is made, at a price equal to its current balance plus accrued interest, under
the Mortgage Sale Agreement. The repurchase price will form part of the Available Principal Receipts and the
Available Revenue Receipts, as described in Section 15. As a result, no Mortgage Loan in the portfolio of the
Issuer will at any time have been increased by a further advance made after the Issue Date.

### Product switches and retention

On or before the end of a product period, the Seller may offer the borrower a new product, which may be a new
fixed rate or tracker product, or a change of the borrower's terms (a "Product Switch"). A Product Switch that
does not increase the amount borrowed does not require a new affordability assessment: the provisions of MCOB 11.6 and 11.7 allow a lender not to
carry out a full affordability assessment where an existing borrower switches product without increasing the
amount borrowed, and the Seller applies them where the borrower is up to date with payments. A Product Switch that increases the amount
borrowed is a further advance and is treated as described above.

A Mortgage Loan that is the subject of a Product Switch after the Issue Date will remain in the portfolio of
the Issuer only if the conditions set out in the Mortgage Sale Agreement are satisfied on the date of the
Product Switch (the "Product Switch Conditions"). The Product Switch Conditions include requirements that:

(a) the new product is a fixed rate product, a tracker product or a variable rate product of a type offered
by the Seller to its owner-occupied borrowers at the Issue Date, and the Mortgage Loan continues to be repayable
in monthly instalments;

(b) the final maturity date of the Mortgage Loan is not extended beyond the date permitted by the Loan
Warranties set out in Section 12;

(c) the Mortgage Loan is not, on the date of the Product Switch, in arrears beyond the level permitted by the
Mortgage Sale Agreement;

(d) the interest rate on the new product is not lower than the minimum rate specified in the Mortgage Sale
Agreement, by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA and the rates on the Notes; and

(e) no Sequential Payment Trigger has occurred and the aggregate current balance of Mortgage Loans that have
been the subject of a Product Switch to a new fixed rate product does not exceed the limit specified in the
Mortgage Sale Agreement.

If the Product Switch Conditions are not satisfied, the Seller will repurchase the Mortgage Loan on or before
the date of the Product Switch at its current balance plus accrued interest. The Seller is under no obligation
to offer a Product Switch to any borrower, and its decisions to offer products to borrowers will be made in the
ordinary course of its business, without regard to whether the Mortgage Loan has been sold to the Issuer. A
Mortgage Loan that becomes a Fixed Rate Loan as a result of a Product Switch after the Issue Date will not be
hedged by the Swap, as described in Section 15, and the resulting risk is described in Section 1.

### Changes to the Lending Criteria

The Seller may change the Lending Criteria from time to time. Every change is approved by the Seller's credit
committee, and any change that is material to the Seller's credit risk appetite is approved by its board risk
committee. A change to the Lending Criteria after the Issue Date will apply to Mortgage Loans only in relation
to further advances and Product Switches made after the change; it will not affect the Loan Warranties, which
are given on the Issue Date by reference to the Lending Criteria under which each Mortgage Loan was
originated.

The material changes to the Lending Criteria during the period in which the Mortgage Loans were originated
were:

(a) the replacement of the application scorecard in 2021, described under "Credit search and credit scoring"
above;

(b) the withdrawal of the Financial Policy Committee's affordability test in August 2022, after which the
Seller relied on its own Affordability Stress Rate alone, described under "Affordability" above;

(c) the increase in the Seller's modelled expenditure figures in 2022 and 2023, to reflect the increase in the

(d) the extension of AVM valuations to a wider range of remortgage applications in 2024, within the limits set
by the credit risk function.

None of these changes, in the opinion of the Seller, resulted in Mortgage Loans being originated to a credit
standard materially lower than that applied to the remainder of the Provisional Portfolio. The Seller has

## Section 14 — Servicing and Arrears Management {-}
The Mortgage Administrator services the loans under the mortgage administration agreement for a fee of
0.20 per cent per year of the current balance of the portfolio. It applies
the Seller's arrears policy: a borrower who misses a payment is contacted within ten days, offered a payment
arrangement where appropriate, and possession is sought only as a last resort and in line with the rules of
the Financial Conduct Authority on fair treatment of customers in financial difficulty.

The Cash Manager receives 30,000 pounds per year and the Note Trustee and Security
Trustee receives 20,000 pounds per year.

### Collections and payment methods

Borrowers pay their monthly instalments to the Mortgage Administrator. Each Mortgage Loan is repayable by
monthly instalments due on a day of the month chosen by the borrower at completion (the "Monthly Payment
Day"). The great majority of borrowers pay by direct debit under the Direct Debit Scheme, collected by the
Mortgage Administrator through the Bacs payment system into the Collection Account described in Section 15.
Borrowers may also pay by standing order, by faster payment or other bank transfer, by debit card through the
Mortgage Administrator's contact centre or website, or over the counter at a branch of the Society. Payments
received in any of those ways are credited to the Collection Account.

The Mortgage Administrator applies every receipt to the borrower's account on the day of receipt, first to
any amounts due other than interest and principal (such as fees and insurance premiums), then to interest and
then to principal, in accordance with the mortgage conditions, unless the borrower has directed that a
payment be applied as an overpayment. The Mortgage Administrator identifies each receipt as a revenue receipt
or a principal receipt of the Issuer on that basis, and reports it to the Cash Manager for the purposes of the
Revenue Ledger and the Principal Ledger described in Section 15. Redemption monies, including early repayment
charges, are received by the Mortgage Administrator from the borrower's conveyancer and credited to the
Collection Account.

Where a direct debit is returned unpaid, the mortgage administration system re-presents it automatically on
the date set by the Mortgage Administrator's collections policy, unless the borrower has cancelled the
mandate, in which case the Mortgage Administrator contacts the borrower to obtain a new mandate or another
means of payment. A returned direct debit is treated as a missed payment for the purposes of the arrears
management process described below from the Monthly Payment Day on which it fell due.

The Mortgage Administrator will, on behalf of the Issuer, set the Standard Variable Rate and any other
discretionary rate applicable to the Mortgage Loans. It has agreed in the Mortgage Administration Agreement
that it will not set the Standard Variable Rate applicable to the Mortgage Loans at a rate higher than the
Society's standard variable rate applicable to comparable loans that it holds for its own account, and that
it will set it at a rate not lower than the minimum rate determined in accordance with the Mortgage
Administration Agreement by reference to Compounded Daily SONIA, unless the Issuer and the Security Trustee
agree otherwise. In setting the rate, the Mortgage Administrator must comply with the mortgage conditions,
MCOB and the Consumer Duty, and the Issuer's rights are subject to those requirements.

### Arrears management

The Mortgage Administrator applies the Society's arrears policy to the Mortgage Loans, as summarised at the
start of this Section 14. That policy is designed to identify borrowers in payment difficulty early, to
establish their circumstances and to agree an arrangement that is sustainable for the borrower, in accordance
with MCOB 13 and the Consumer Duty. The arrears management process is organised in the following stages,
although a borrower's account may move between them, or be dealt with outside them, according to the
borrower's circumstances.

(a) **Early arrears.** When a payment is missed, the borrower is contacted by letter, text message or
telephone within ten days, as stated above. The Mortgage Administrator's early arrears team seeks to establish
the reason for the missed payment, whether it is a temporary or a longer-term difficulty, and the borrower's
income and expenditure. It gives the borrower the information required by MCOB 13, including information about
free and impartial debt advice. Where the difficulty is short-term, the borrower is asked to clear the arrears
or offered a short payment arrangement.

(b) **Established arrears.** Where the arrears continue, the account is transferred to the Mortgage
Administrator's specialist financial support team. That team carries out a full assessment of the borrower's
income and expenditure, using the standard financial statement used in the debt advice sector, and considers
which of the forms of forbearance described below is appropriate. It sends the statements of arrears and the
information sheet required by MCOB 13 and records every contact and every arrangement on the mortgage
administration system.

(c) **Pre-litigation.** Where no arrangement has been agreed, or an arrangement has failed and the borrower does
not engage with the Mortgage Administrator, the account is reviewed by a manager in the financial support team
before any step towards litigation is taken. The review confirms that the borrower has been offered every
appropriate form of forbearance, that the requirements of the relevant pre-action rules described below have
been met, and that the borrower is not in a vulnerable situation that requires a different approach.

(d) **Litigation and recovery.** Only after that review may the account be passed to the Mortgage
Administrator's litigation team for proceedings to be brought, as described under "Litigation and possession"
below. Possession is sought only as a last resort, as stated above.

The Mortgage Administrator identifies and records the characteristics of vulnerability of borrowers in
accordance with the FCA's guidance on the fair treatment of vulnerable customers, and adapts its contact and
its forbearance accordingly. It may suspend the arrears process for a borrower who has been bereaved, has a
serious illness or is in a breathing space under the Debt Respite Scheme (Breathing Space Moratorium and
Mental Health Crisis Moratorium) (England and Wales) Regulations 2020, during which it may not take enforcement
action and must suspend the application of default interest and charges as those Regulations require.

### Forbearance and the Mortgage Charter

Under MCOB 13, as amended by the FCA's rules on tailored support for borrowers in payment difficulties, the
Mortgage Administrator must consider the forms of forbearance that are appropriate to the borrower's
circumstances and must not apply a single form of forbearance to all borrowers. The forms of forbearance that
the Mortgage Administrator may offer include:

(a) an arrangement to pay the arrears over a period in addition to the monthly instalment;

(b) a temporary reduction in the monthly payment, or a temporary interest-only arrangement, for a defined
period, after which the payment is recalculated;

(c) an extension of the term of the Mortgage Loan, subject to the borrower's age and to the requirement in the
Loan Warranties as to final maturity set out in Section 12;

(d) the capitalisation of arrears, where the borrower has demonstrated that he or she can sustain the
recalculated monthly payment;

(e) a change to the repayment basis, including a permanent conversion of part of the loan to an interest-only
basis, where the borrower has a credible repayment strategy for that part; and

(f) the deferral of the remedy of arrears while the borrower sells the property voluntarily.

The Society is a signatory to the Mortgage Charter agreed between HM Treasury and mortgage lenders, with the support of the FCA, in
June 2023, and the Mortgage Administrator applies the commitments of the Mortgage Charter, as amended or
extended from time to time, to the Mortgage Loans. Those commitments include, in particular, that a borrower
who is up to date with payments may switch to interest-only payments for a temporary period, or extend the
mortgage term for a temporary period with the option to revert to the original term, without a new
affordability assessment and without the change affecting the borrower's credit file, and that a borrower will
not be required to leave the property without his or her consent within twelve months of the first missed
payment, except in exceptional circumstances. The FCA has amended MCOB to support those commitments.

The Mortgage Administrator will report to the Cash Manager, for inclusion in the investor report described
below, the number and aggregate current balance of the Mortgage Loans that are subject to each form of
forbearance. A Mortgage Loan that is subject to forbearance remains in the portfolio of the Issuer. The
Mortgage Administrator may agree any form of forbearance that is consistent with the Society's arrears policy,
MCOB and the Consumer Duty, and is not required to obtain the consent of the Issuer or the Security Trustee to
it. Forbearance may delay or reduce the receipts of the Issuer, and the arrears of a Mortgage Loan subject to
forbearance are counted for the purpose of the Arrears Trigger described in Section 8 in accordance with the
Arrears Ratio as defined in the Glossary of Defined Terms.

### Litigation and possession

Proceedings to enforce the Related Security are brought by the Mortgage Administrator in the name of the
Society, as legal owner of the Mortgage Loans, until a Perfection Event occurs, and thereafter in the name of
the Issuer. The costs of enforcement are recoverable from the borrower under the mortgage conditions and, to
the extent that they are not recovered, are deducted from the proceeds of enforcement before the Realised Loss
is calculated.

**England and Wales.** Before issuing a claim for possession of a residential property, the Mortgage
Administrator must comply with the Pre-Action Protocol for Possession Claims based on Mortgage or Home
Purchase Plan Arrears in Respect of Residential Property, which requires it, among other things, to provide
the borrower with information about the arrears, to consider any reasonable proposal for payment, to consider
postponing proceedings where the borrower has made a claim to a government agency or is pursuing the sale of
the property, and to inform the borrower of the date and time of any hearing. A claim is brought in the
County Court under Part 55 of the Civil Procedure Rules. The court may adjourn the proceedings, or suspend or
postpone an order for possession, under section 36 of the Administration of Justice Act 1970 and section 8 of
the Administration of Justice Act 1973, if it appears that the borrower is likely to be able to pay the sums
due within a reasonable period. An order for possession is enforced by a warrant of possession executed by a
county court enforcement officer or, with permission, by a writ of possession executed by a High Court
enforcement officer. The Mortgage Administrator must give notice to the occupiers of the property under the
Mortgage Repossessions (Protection of Tenants etc.) Act 2010 before an order is enforced.

**Scotland.** The enforcement of a standard security over a residential property in Scotland is governed by the
Conveyancing and Feudal Reform (Scotland) Act 1970, as amended by the Home Owner and Debtor Protection
(Scotland) Act 2010. Before applying to the court, the Mortgage Administrator must comply with the pre-action
requirements in section 24A of the 1970 Act, which include providing the borrower with information about the
arrears, making reasonable efforts to agree a proposal for payment, and considering any application by the
borrower to a debt payment programme or for a time to pay order. It must then serve a calling-up notice or a
notice of default, and apply to the sheriff court for a decree under section 24 of the 1970 Act. The court may
grant the decree only if it is satisfied that the pre-action requirements have been met and that it is
reasonable in the circumstances to do so. The borrower and any entitled resident may appear and be heard, and
the court may continue the proceedings or sist them. Following the decree, the Mortgage Administrator may
enter into possession and sell the property. Proceedings in Scotland are conducted by solicitors in Scotland
on the Mortgage Administrator's panel.

In both jurisdictions the time taken from the first missed payment to the sale of the property depends on the
borrower's engagement, the forbearance offered, the requirements of the pre-action rules and the Mortgage
Charter, the capacity of the courts and the condition of the local property market, and has historically been
longer in Scotland than in England and Wales. The Mortgage Administrator may accept a voluntary surrender of
possession by a borrower, and it will do so in preference to proceedings where the borrower agrees.

### Sale of repossessed properties

When the Mortgage Administrator takes possession of a property, it secures and insures the property and
instructs an asset manager on its panel to manage its sale. The asset manager obtains at least two independent
valuations, instructs local estate agents to market the property on the open market, and recommends to the
Mortgage Administrator the acceptance of offers. The Mortgage Administrator is under a duty, as mortgagee in
possession in England and Wales and as heritable creditor in Scotland, to take reasonable care to obtain the
true market value or the best price reasonably obtainable for the property at the time of sale, and it will
market every property for a period sufficient to achieve that. A property is sold by auction only where the
asset manager advises that auction is likely to achieve a better price, for example because of the condition
or the type of the property.

The proceeds of sale, after the deduction of the costs of possession and sale, are applied in discharge of the
Mortgage Loan and are credited to the Collection Account. Any shortfall is a Realised Loss, which is recorded on
the Principal Deficiency Ledger as described in Section 5. Any surplus is paid to any subsequent secured
creditor and then to the borrower. After the sale the Mortgage Administrator will pursue the borrower for any
shortfall where it considers it appropriate, in accordance with its policies and with industry guidance on
the recovery of mortgage shortfall debt, within the limitation periods under the Limitation Act 1980 in England
and Wales and the prescription periods under the Prescription and Limitation (Scotland) Act 1973 in Scotland.
Recoveries after the sale are revenue receipts of the Issuer, as described in Section 15.

### Mortgage Administrator reporting

The Mortgage Administrator will deliver to the Issuer, the Cash Manager and the Security Trustee, within the
period set out in the Mortgage Administration Agreement after the end of each month, a report on the Mortgage
Loans for that month (a "Monthly Mortgage Administration Report"). Each Monthly Mortgage Administration Report
will set out the receipts on the Mortgage Loans, split between revenue and principal; the redemptions,
repurchases and Product Switches in the month; the arrears of the Mortgage Loans by number of months in
arrears; the Mortgage Loans subject to forbearance, by form of forbearance; the properties in possession and
sold, and the Realised Losses on them; and the information needed by the Cash Manager to calculate the Arrears
Ratio and the Cumulative Loss Ratio on each Calculation Date.

The Mortgage Administrator will also provide the Cash Manager with the loan-level information on each Mortgage
Loan needed for the loan-level reports required by the transparency requirements of the Securitisation
Regulations and SECN, described in Section 17. On the basis of the Monthly Mortgage Administration Reports and
its own records, the Cash Manager will prepare a quarterly investor report (the "Investor Report") for each
Interest Payment Date, which will be made available to Noteholders as described in Section 17.

### The interest rate swap

**Purpose.** The Fixed Rate Loans bear interest at fixed rates for their Fixed Rate Period, while the Class A,
Class B, Class C and Class D Notes bear interest at Compounded Daily SONIA plus a margin. If Compounded Daily
SONIA rose, the interest received on the Fixed Rate Loans would not rise with it, and the Issuer might not have
enough revenue to pay interest on the Notes. To hedge that risk, the Issuer will enter into an interest rate
swap transaction (the "Swap") with Halcyon Bank plc (the "Swap Provider") under an ISDA master agreement, a
schedule, a credit support annex and a confirmation (together, the "Swap Agreement"). The Swap Agreement is
governed by English law.

**Mechanics.** On each Interest Payment Date:

(a) the Issuer will pay to the Swap Provider an amount equal to a fixed rate, set on the Issue Date, applied to
the Notional Amount for the relevant calculation period; and

(b) the Swap Provider will pay to the Issuer an amount equal to Compounded Daily SONIA for that calculation
period applied to the same Notional Amount.

The two amounts are netted, and only the net amount is paid, by the party owing the larger amount. A net amount
due to the Swap Provider ranks at item (c) of the Revenue Priority of Payments in Section 6, ahead of interest on
the Notes. A net amount due to the Issuer forms part of the Available Revenue Receipts.

The "Notional Amount" for each calculation period is the lower of (a) the amount for that period set out in a
schedule to the confirmation, which was calculated on the Issue Date on the assumption that the Fixed Rate
Loans amortise in accordance with their terms, are not in default and prepay at a constant rate, and which
reduces to zero as each Fixed Rate Loan reaches the end of its Fixed Rate Period, and (b) the aggregate current
balance of the Mortgage Loans that are Fixed Rate Loans in their original Fixed Rate Period at the start of the
period. The Swap therefore hedges only the original Fixed Rate Period of the Fixed Rate Loans in the Provisional
Portfolio, as stated in Section 1. It does not hedge the Tracker Loans, the Mortgage Loans at the Standard
Variable Rate, or Mortgage Loans that become Fixed Rate Loans by a Product Switch after the Issue Date. The
mismatch between the Bank Rate and the Standard Variable Rate, on the one hand, and Compounded Daily SONIA, on
the other, is not hedged, and the resulting basis risk is described in Section 1.

**Termination.** The Swap Agreement may be terminated early on the occurrence of an event of default or a
termination event under it, which include: the failure of either party to make a payment when due, subject to a
grace period; the insolvency of either party; the delivery of an enforcement notice in relation to the Notes;
the redemption of all of the Notes; a change in tax law that requires a party to deduct tax from its payments;
and the failure of the Swap Provider to take the action required on a downgrade described below. On early
termination, a termination payment may be due from either party, calculated on the basis of market quotations
for a replacement transaction or of the loss of the non-defaulting party.

A termination payment due from the Issuer ranks at item (c) of the Revenue Priority of Payments, unless the
Swap Agreement was terminated because the Swap Provider was the defaulting party or because of the Swap
Provider's failure to take the action required on a downgrade, in which case it ranks at item (o) as a
subordinated swap termination payment. Any premium received by the Issuer from a replacement swap provider will
be applied first to pay any termination payment due to the outgoing Swap Provider, and any termination payment
received by the Issuer will be applied first to the cost of entering into a replacement swap. Neither amount
forms part of the Available Revenue Receipts except to the extent that it exceeds the amount so applied.

**Tax.** If the Swap Provider is required by law to deduct tax from its payments, it must gross up its payments
so that the Issuer receives the amount it would have received without the deduction. The Issuer is not required
to gross up its payments to the Swap Provider.

### Rating triggers: the Swap Provider

The Swap Agreement contains rating triggers set by reference to the criteria of the Rating Agencies. If the
ratings of the Swap Provider, or of any guarantor of its obligations, fall below the first level of ratings
specified in the Swap Agreement (the "Initial Required Ratings"), the Swap Provider must, at its own cost,
within the period set out in the Swap Agreement (normally fourteen days), transfer collateral to the Swap
Collateral Account in accordance with the credit support annex, or transfer its obligations to an entity with
the Initial Required Ratings, or obtain a guarantee from such an entity.

If the ratings of the Swap Provider fall below a second and lower level of ratings specified in the Swap Agreement
(the "Subsequent Required Ratings"), the Swap Provider must, at its own cost, within the period set out in the
Swap Agreement (normally thirty days), transfer its obligations to an entity with the required ratings or obtain
a guarantee from such an entity, and must in the meantime continue to transfer collateral, in a greater amount,
under the credit support annex. If the Swap Provider fails to take the required action within the required
period, an additional termination event will occur, and the Issuer may terminate the Swap Agreement.

Collateral transferred by the Swap Provider is held in the Swap Collateral Account and recorded on the Swap
Collateral Ledger. It does not form part of the Available Revenue Receipts or the Available Principal Receipts,
and is not applied in the Revenue Priority of Payments. It may be applied only to satisfy the obligations of the
Swap Provider under the Swap Agreement on its termination, or returned to the Swap Provider, in accordance with
the credit support annex.

### Rating triggers: the Account Bank

If the Account Bank ceases to have the ratings specified in the Bank Account Agreement by reference to the
criteria of the Rating Agencies (the "Account Bank Required Ratings"), the Cash Manager, on behalf of the Issuer,
must, within thirty calendar days of the downgrade (or such longer period as the Rating Agencies confirm will not
adversely affect the ratings of the Notes):

(a) close the Issuer Accounts and open replacement accounts on substantially the same terms with a bank that has
the Account Bank Required Ratings, and transfer to them the balances of the Issuer Accounts; or

(b) obtain a guarantee of the obligations of the Account Bank from a bank that has the Account Bank Required
Ratings; or

(c) take such other action as the Rating Agencies confirm will not adversely affect the ratings of the Notes.

Authorised Investments held with or issued by the Account Bank must be realised or transferred on the same
basis. The costs of the replacement are borne by the outgoing Account Bank, to the extent set out in the
Bank Account Agreement.

### Conflicts of interest of Halcyon Bank plc

Halcyon Bank plc acts in this transaction as the Arranger, the Account Bank, the Collection Account Bank and
the Swap Provider. In each of these roles it acts in its own interest and under the terms of the relevant
Transaction Document, and it owes no fiduciary duty to the Noteholders. Its rights as Swap Provider, including its
right to a termination payment ranking ahead of interest on the Notes, may conflict with the interests of the

and Swap Provider at the same time, and the rating triggers described in this Section 15 would then require the
replacement of more than one counterparty within a short period. The risks arising from these concentrations are
described in Section 1.

## Section 17 — Risk Retention and Regulatory Disclosures {-}
The Seller retains a material net economic interest of not less than 5 per cent of the nominal value of the securitised exposures by holding randomly selected exposures, which would otherwise have been securitised, from a pool of not less than 100 potentially securitised exposures at origination, as required by the risk retention rules of the UK securitisation framework (SECN 5 of the FCA Handbook and the Securitisation Part of the PRA Rulebook). The Seller will not sell, hedge or otherwise mitigate the credit risk of the retained
exposures, and will confirm its retention in each quarterly investor report.

### Withholding Tax on Interest

**The general rule.** The Issuer is incorporated in England and Wales and is resident in the United Kingdom for
tax purposes, and interest on the Class A Notes, the Class B Notes, the Class C Notes and the Class D Notes will
have a United Kingdom source. As the Notes may remain outstanding for more than one year, that interest is
expected to be "yearly interest" for United Kingdom tax purposes. A company which pays yearly interest with a
United Kingdom source is generally required to deduct income tax at the basic rate (currently 20 per cent) from
the payment, unless an exemption or relief applies or HMRC has given a direction to the contrary under an
applicable double taxation treaty.

**No quoted Eurobond exemption.** The exemption for "quoted Eurobonds" in section 882 of the Income Tax Act
2007 (the "ITA 2007") applies only to securities which are, and continue to be, listed on a recognised stock
exchange or admitted to trading on a multilateral trading facility operated by a regulated recognised stock
exchange. No application has been or will be made for the Notes to be listed or admitted to trading, and that
exemption is therefore not available.

**The qualifying private placement exemption.** Section 888A of the ITA 2007 and the Income Tax (Qualifying
Private Placement) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/2002) (together, the "QPP Rules") provide that the duty to deduct
income tax does not apply to a payment of interest on a security which is a "qualifying private placement". In
summary, and without describing every condition, the Issuer may pay interest on a Note without deduction under
the QPP Rules where:

(a) the Note is a security representing a loan relationship to which the Issuer, a company, is party as
debtor;

(b) the Note is not listed on a recognised stock exchange;

(c) the term of the Note does not exceed 50 years, a condition which is satisfied because the Final Maturity
Date of the Notes is 20 July 2064;

(d) the aggregate value of the securities issued under the arrangement is not less than £10 million;

(e) the person beneficially entitled to the interest is resident in a "qualifying territory", meaning a
territory with which the United Kingdom has a double taxation treaty containing a non-discrimination
provision, and is not connected with the Issuer;

(f) that person holds the Note for genuine commercial reasons and not as part of a tax advantage scheme, and
the Issuer has issued the Notes for genuine commercial reasons and not as part of a tax advantage scheme; and

(g) the Issuer has received from that person a certificate (a "Creditor Certificate") confirming its
beneficial entitlement to the interest, its residence in a qualifying territory and the absence of any
connection with the Issuer, and the Issuer has no reason to believe that any statement in it is incorrect.

As the Notes are held in global registered form through the clearing systems, the Issuer can apply the QPP
Rules only to interest to which a Noteholder is beneficially entitled and in respect of which a valid Creditor
Certificate has been delivered, through the procedures agreed with the paying agent and the clearing
systems, before the relevant Interest Payment Date.

**Payments to United Kingdom companies and other exempt recipients.** The Issuer may also pay interest on the
Notes without deduction where it reasonably believes, at the time of the payment, that the person beneficially
entitled to the interest is a company resident in the United Kingdom, or a non-resident company carrying on a
trade in the United Kingdom through a permanent establishment which is required to bring the interest into
account in calculating its chargeable profits, or another person listed in sections 933 to 937 of the ITA 2007.
If that belief proves to be incorrect, the Issuer may be liable to account for the tax that it should have
deducted, together with interest and, in certain circumstances, penalties.

**Treaty relief.** A Noteholder resident in a jurisdiction which has a double taxation treaty with the United
Kingdom may be entitled to receive interest without deduction, or subject to deduction at a reduced rate, if
HMRC directs the Issuer accordingly following a claim by that Noteholder in the prescribed form, or may recover
from HMRC some or all of any tax deducted. A direction does not apply to payments made before it is given.

**The Class Z Notes.** The Class Z Notes do not bear interest, and the rules on the deduction of tax from
payments of interest described above therefore do not apply to them. Payments of principal on any class of
Notes may be made without deduction for or on account of United Kingdom income tax.

**Consequences of a deduction.** If interest on the Notes is paid subject to a deduction of United Kingdom
income tax, neither the Issuer nor any other person will be obliged to pay any additional amount to the
Noteholders in respect of the amount deducted, as provided in the Conditions set out in Section 4. A change in
law which requires such a deduction may entitle the Issuer to redeem the Notes in the circumstances set out in
the Conditions.

**Payments under the Swap Agreement.** Payments made by the Swap Provider to the Issuer, and by the Issuer to the
Swap Provider, under the Swap Agreement are not expected to be payments of interest and are therefore not
expected to be subject to any deduction for or on account of United Kingdom tax. If a deduction were required as
a result of a change in law, the consequences would be those set out in the Swap Agreement and described in
Section 15.

**The Notes.** No United Kingdom stamp duty or stamp duty reserve tax ("SDRT") should be payable on the issue of

### Taxation of the Issuer

**The Securitisation Company Regulations.** The Issuer expects to be a "note-issuing company" within the meaning

profit. The Transaction Documents provide for the Issuer to retain a small amount of profit in each accounting
period, and the Issuer expects its liability to corporation tax to be limited to tax on that retained profit at
the prevailing rate.

**Conditions of the regime.** The application of the Securitisation Company Regulations depends on the Issuer
continuing to satisfy their conditions, including conditions relating to the nature of its business and its
assets, the issue of capital market investments as part of a capital market arrangement, the payments which it

retained profit, as described in Section 1. A change in the tax treatment of the Issuer may entitle the Issuer to

**Holdings.** Holdings is not expected to be a securitisation
company. It will be subject to corporation tax on its income and gains under the general rules. Holdings is not

**Value added tax.** Supplies of services to the Issuer under the Transaction Documents may be subject to value

## Glossary of Defined Terms {-}
"**Arrears Ratio**" means the aggregate current balance of the loans that are 3 or more months in arrears divided by the aggregate current balance of the portfolio, on the last day of a collection period.

"**Cumulative Loss Ratio**" means the aggregate of all realised losses since the Issue Date divided by the current balance of the portfolio on the Issue Date.

"**Realised Loss**" means the amount by which the balance of a loan exceeds the proceeds of enforcement after the property has been sold.

"**Principal Deficiency Ledger**" means a ledger, one sub-ledger per class, recording realised losses and principal used to pay revenue items, cleared from Available Revenue Receipts.

"**Step-Up Date**" means the Interest Payment Date in July 2031, after which the margins step up and the Issuer may redeem all the Notes.

"**Interest Payment Date**" means the 20th day of January, April, July and October in each year.

"**Calculation Date**" means the last day of the month before each Interest Payment Date.
